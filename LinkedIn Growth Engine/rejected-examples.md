# Rejected Examples — Grey AI LinkedIn

These posts fail the quality bar. Study the patterns to avoid them.

---

## Rejection 1: Abstract Opener

```
𝐀𝐈 𝐬𝐤𝐢𝐥𝐥𝐬 𝐚𝐫𝐞𝐧'𝐭 𝐚 𝐝𝐢𝐟𝐟𝐞𝐫𝐞𝐧𝐭𝐢𝐚𝐭𝐨𝐫 𝐚𝐧𝐲𝐦𝐨𝐫𝐞.

Accenture made them a formal promotion requirement...
```

**ER:** 0.0%
**Why it fails:** The first line is a statement about Accenture, not about the reader. "Aren't a differentiator anymore" is abstract — what does that mean for ME? The reader has no role in the story. Compare to the approved rewrite: "Accenture just made AI skills a promotion gate for its entire global workforce. Not optional. A gate."

**Fix:** Lead with the entity doing something concrete, or lead with the consequence for the reader.

## Rejection 2: Industry Report Voice

```
𝐓𝐡𝐞 𝐔.𝐒. 𝐠𝐨𝐯𝐞𝐫𝐧𝐦𝐞𝐧𝐭 𝐣𝐮𝐬𝐭 𝐭𝐡𝐫𝐞𝐚𝐭𝐞𝐧𝐞𝐝 𝐭𝐨 𝐭𝐫𝐞𝐚𝐭 𝐀𝐧𝐭𝐡𝐫𝐨𝐩𝐢𝐜 𝐥𝐢𝐤𝐞 𝐚 𝐝𝐞𝐟𝐞𝐧𝐬𝐞 𝐜𝐨𝐧𝐭𝐫𝐚𝐜𝐭𝐨𝐫.

The Pentagon filed a preliminary injunction to prevent Anthropic from withdrawing its Claude model from military use...
```

**ER:** 3.4%
**Why it fails:** Reads like a policy briefing. The reader is a spectator, not a participant. Dense setup paragraph. No clear consequence for the reader's team or career. The reader thinks "interesting" and scrolls.

**Fix:** Find the personal angle. "The AI tool your team uses for coding might soon be classified as defense infrastructure. What happens to your access when that contract kicks in?"

## Rejection 3: Hardware Analysis (No Emotional Stakes)

```
𝐀𝐒𝐌𝐋 𝐇𝐢𝐠𝐡-𝐍𝐀 𝐄𝐔𝐕 𝐭𝐨𝐨𝐥𝐬 𝐚𝐫𝐞 𝐩𝐫𝐨𝐝𝐮𝐜𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧-𝐫𝐞𝐚𝐝𝐲.

This is the chipmaking technology that will determine...
```

**ER:** 6.9%
**Why it fails:** Zero emotional stakes for the audience (senior decision-makers in law, finance, tech management). No one reading Grey AI's feed cares about chipmaking tools. The content is accurate but irrelevant to the audience.

**Fix:** Either skip (no Grey AI angle) or find the downstream consequence: "The chip shortage that's been throttling AI deployment just got a fix. Here's what that means for the tools your team can access in 6 months."

## Rejection 4: Monotonous "Your..." Pattern

```
Post 1: 𝐘𝐨𝐮𝐫 company isn't tracking whether you use AI yet.
Post 2: 𝐘𝐨𝐮𝐫 team's AI assistant choice just became a platform decision.
Post 3: 𝐘𝐨𝐮𝐫 team is splitting into 2 groups right now.
Post 4: 𝐘𝐨𝐮𝐫 AI investment thesis needs to survive this number.
Post 5: 𝐘𝐨𝐮𝐫 AI coding tool might be running a Chinese model.
```

**Why it fails:** Five consecutive posts with the same first word. In a feed, this creates visible monotony — signals template-driven content, not a human with range. Each individual post might score well, but the sequence fails the brand fit test.

**Fix:** Rotate opener categories. Same 5 posts rewritten:
```
Post 1: JPMorgan just started tracking AI usage across 65,000 employees. (entity-action)
Post 2: Same AI tool. Two professionals. Two opposite mistakes. (contrarian)
Post 3: Two groups are forming inside every team right now. (contrarian)
Post 4: A $1 billion Disney deal produced $2.1M in revenue. (stat-consequence)
Post 5: A debug proxy just exposed what's running under your AI coding tool. (mystery)
```

## Rejection 5: Bland CTA

```
...What do you think about this development?
```

**Why it fails:** "What do you think?" is the lowest-performing CTA in the dataset. Too vague. Requires the reader to form an opinion from scratch. Nobody answers this.

**Fix:** Make it specific and answerable in 3 seconds:
- "Is your team measuring this yet — or still guessing?"
- "Which side of this would your team land on?"
- "Have you seen this happen at your company?"

## Rejection 6: Over-Explained Post

```
𝐘𝐨𝐮𝐫 𝐀𝐈 𝐚𝐠𝐞𝐧𝐭 𝐰𝐢𝐥𝐥 𝐭𝐞𝐥𝐥 𝐲𝐨𝐮 𝐢𝐭 𝐡𝐚𝐬𝐧'𝐭 𝐜𝐡𝐚𝐧𝐠𝐞𝐝.

Belief drift is a phenomenon where AI systems gradually shift their behavioral
patterns over time without explicit notification to the user. This can manifest
in several ways including response style changes, confidence calibration shifts,
and subtle alterations to the model's default assumptions about user intent...
```

**ER:** 0.0%
**Why it fails:** "Belief drift" is jargon nobody knows on first read. The body reads like a textbook definition. Dense paragraph. No personal stakes. No consequence for the reader.

**Fix:** Cut the explanation entirely. Show the consequence: "The AI your team trained on last quarter is giving different answers now. Nobody told you it changed. Nobody had to."

---

## Pattern Summary — What Gets Rejected

| Pattern | Why It Kills Performance |
|---------|------------------------|
| Abstract first line | Reader has nothing to feel about themselves |
| Policy/hardware analysis | Reader is a spectator, not a participant |
| Same opener 3+ times in a row | Signals template, not a human with range |
| "What do you think?" CTA | Too vague to answer; nobody comments |
| Dense explanation paragraphs | Mobile death; reader scrolls past |
| Jargon without setup | Reader doesn't know what the term means |
| News recap without consequence | "Interesting" → scroll |
