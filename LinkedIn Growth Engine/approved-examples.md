# Approved Examples — Grey AI LinkedIn

These posts meet the quality bar. Study the patterns: varied openers, concrete consequences, sharp POV, easy CTAs, mobile-friendly formatting.

---

## Example 1: Entity-Action Opener (Carousel)

**Opener type:** Entity-Action
**CTA type:** Binary Question

```
𝐉𝐏𝐌𝐨𝐫𝐠𝐚𝐧 𝐣𝐮𝐬𝐭 𝐬𝐭𝐚𝐫𝐭𝐞𝐝 𝐭𝐫𝐚𝐜𝐤𝐢𝐧𝐠 𝐀𝐈 𝐮𝐬𝐚𝐠𝐞 𝐚𝐜𝐫𝐨𝐬𝐬 𝟔𝟓,𝟎𝟎𝟎 𝐞𝐦𝐩𝐥𝐨𝐲𝐞𝐞𝐬.
𝐀𝐧𝐝 𝐭𝐢𝐞𝐝 𝐢𝐭 𝐝𝐢𝐫𝐞𝐜𝐭𝐥𝐲 𝐭𝐨 𝐩𝐞𝐫𝐟𝐨𝐫𝐦𝐚𝐧𝐜𝐞 𝐫𝐞𝐯𝐢𝐞𝐰𝐬.

Two categories: light user or heavy user. That classification now lives inside the same system as promotions and compensation.

Every industry is about to have this conversation. Swipe to see what both sides of this divide actually look like.

Is AI usage a metric at the place where you work — or is that still a few years away?
```

**Why it works:** Leads with a recognizable institution doing something alarming. The reader infers the threat ("could my company do this?") without being told. Concrete number (65,000). Binary framework. Easy CTA.

## Example 2: Mystery Entity Opener (Text)

**Opener type:** Mystery
**CTA type:** Binary Question

```
𝐀 𝐂𝐄𝐎 𝐣𝐮𝐬𝐭 𝐬𝐚𝐢𝐝 𝐨𝐮𝐭 𝐥𝐨𝐮𝐝 𝐰𝐡𝐚𝐭 𝐦𝐨𝐬𝐭 𝐛𝐨𝐚𝐫𝐝𝐬 𝐚𝐫𝐞 𝐨𝐧𝐥𝐲 𝐭𝐡𝐢𝐧𝐤𝐢𝐧𝐠.

Block cut 40% of its workforce — not layoffs from underperformance. A structural bet that AI replaces the headcount permanently.

The quiet part: most boards are running the same math. They just haven't said it yet.

If your board ran this calculation tomorrow, would your team survive it?
```

**Why it works:** 71.2% ER — Grey AI's highest-performing post. The mystery ("A CEO") creates curiosity. The reader immediately asks "Is MY board thinking this?" The consequence is specific (40%, permanent). The CTA is personal and answerable.

## Example 3: Contrarian Opener (Text)

**Opener type:** Contrarian
**CTA type:** Challenge

```
𝐂𝐚𝐧 𝐰𝐞 𝐭𝐚𝐥𝐤 𝐚𝐛𝐨𝐮𝐭 𝐭𝐡𝐞 𝐩𝐫𝐨𝐦𝐩𝐭 𝐟𝐫𝐚𝐦𝐞𝐰𝐨𝐫𝐤 𝐢𝐧𝐝𝐮𝐬𝐭𝐫𝐢𝐚𝐥 𝐜𝐨𝐦𝐩𝐥𝐞𝐱?

CRISP. RISEN. CO-STAR. RACE.

Most are reverse-engineered from good prompts and turned into acronyms so someone can sell a course.

You don't need a framework. You need three habits:

→ Tell the AI what role to play
→ Give it the specific context
→ Ask one clear question at a time

Clear beats clever. Every time.

Do you use a prompt framework — or just write clearly?
```

**Why it works:** Opens by challenging something the reader probably follows. Irreverent but backed by a real point. The "three habits" give concrete value. Short, punchy, mobile-friendly. The CTA invites a quick opinion.

## Example 4: Stat-Consequence Opener (Carousel)

**Opener type:** Stat-Consequence
**CTA type:** Reframe Question

```
𝐀 $𝟏 𝐛𝐢𝐥𝐥𝐢𝐨𝐧 𝐃𝐢𝐬𝐧𝐞𝐲 𝐝𝐞𝐚𝐥 𝐩𝐫𝐨𝐝𝐮𝐜𝐞𝐝 $𝟐.𝟏𝐌 𝐢𝐧 𝐫𝐞𝐯𝐞𝐧𝐮𝐞.
𝐓𝐡𝐞𝐧 𝐎𝐩𝐞𝐧𝐀𝐈 𝐬𝐡𝐮𝐭 𝐢𝐭 𝐝𝐨𝐰𝐧.

3.3 million downloads. A billion-dollar partnership. Six months of operation. Total lifetime revenue: $2.1M.

The gap between AI that gets attention and AI that generates revenue just got its most measurable data point.

Swipe to see what your AI strategy is actually building.

What if the real question isn't "are we using AI?" — but "is our AI generating revenue?"
```

**Why it works:** Leads with numbers that create immediate dissonance ($1B deal → $2.1M revenue). The contrast does the arguing. The consequence is clear (attention ≠ revenue). The CTA reframes the reader's internal question.

## Example 5: You/Your Opener (Text)

**Opener type:** You/Your
**CTA type:** Verdict Close

```
𝐘𝐨𝐮𝐫 𝐀𝐈 𝐜𝐨𝐝𝐢𝐧𝐠 𝐭𝐨𝐨𝐥 𝐦𝐢𝐠𝐡𝐭 𝐛𝐞 𝐫𝐮𝐧𝐧𝐢𝐧𝐠 𝐚 𝐂𝐡𝐢𝐧𝐞𝐬𝐞 𝐦𝐨𝐝𝐞𝐥.
𝐘𝐨𝐮𝐫 𝐯𝐞𝐧𝐝𝐨𝐫 𝐝𝐢𝐝𝐧'𝐭 𝐭𝐞𝐥𝐥 𝐲𝐨𝐮.

A debug proxy revealed Cursor shipped Kimi 2.5 — built by Beijing-based Moonshot AI — as their proprietary "Composer 2." The post hit 444,000 views before the co-founder admitted it was "a miss."

Most enterprise contracts don't require disclosure of the base model running underneath.

The supply chain opacity problem just got a proof of concept.
```

**Why it works:** "You/Your" works here because it creates genuine personal fear (my tool might be compromised). The consequence is concrete (supply chain opacity). The verdict close is punchy and doesn't ask a question — it lands the point. Note: this opener type should be used no more than 1-2x per week.

## Pattern Summary

Notice across all 5 examples:
- **5 different opener types** (Entity-Action, Mystery, Contrarian, Stat-Consequence, You/Your)
- **3 different CTA types** (Binary Question, Challenge, Reframe Question, Verdict Close)
- **All short** (under 100 words for captions, under 150 for text)
- **All have concrete consequences** (not just observations)
- **All have a clear point of view** (not neutral summaries)
- **All are mobile-friendly** (short paragraphs, generous breaks)
