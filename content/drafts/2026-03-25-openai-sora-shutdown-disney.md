## HEADLINE
OpenAI has shut down its AI video social app six months after launch — abandoning a $1 billion deal with Disney before any cash changed hands — after the app's downloads fell 66% in three months and generated just $2.1 million in lifetime revenue.

## KEY FACTS
- Peak downloads: 3.3 million (November 2025); fell to 1.1 million by February 2026 — a 66% decline in three months
- Total lifetime in-app purchase revenue: $2.1 million against enormous compute costs
- Disney deal: $1 billion investment plus multi-year licensing for Disney, Marvel, Pixar, and Star Wars characters — no money changed hands before collapse
- The company is simultaneously pulling back from a separate e-commerce feature (Instant Checkout) — a parallel consumer retreat
- The underlying video model remains available behind a paid subscription; OpenAI is not exiting video AI, only the social product
- Source credibility: Named company, download data from Appfigures, Disney confirmed no money changed hands

## GREY AI ANGLE
The gap between a technically impressive AI product and a sustainable AI business just became the most concrete data point in the industry. For leaders evaluating their own AI investments, the signal is direct: technical capability generates attention, not revenue. The organizations building durable AI value in 2026 are doing it in enterprise workflows where AI replaces real cost — not in consumer experiences where AI provides novelty.

## HOOK OPTIONS

**Provocative:** A $1 billion deal. The most recognized AI brand in the world. Technically impressive product. $2.1 million in revenue. Your AI investment thesis needs to survive contact with that number.

**Binary:** AI that generates attention: technically impressive, viral at launch, 3.3 million downloads in month one. AI that generates revenue: embedded in workflows, replaces real cost, scales without a marketing campaign. Most 2026 AI strategies are building the first one and calling it the second.

**Stat Gap:** Peak downloads: 3.3 million. Revenue after six months: $2.1 million total. A $1 billion deal — never funded. The gap between AI hype and AI value doesn't get more measurable than that.

## RAW MATERIAL
The app launched September 2025 as an AI-first social platform; its flagship feature "Characters" let users generate videos using deepfake likenesses of celebrities and themselves. It was plagued throughout its run by deepfakes of public figures (Martin Luther King Jr., Robin Williams), copyright violations (Mario, Naruto, Pikachu), and a court order forcing a name change. ChatGPT context: 900 million weekly active users — Sora's 3.3M peak was a rounding error on the company's own main product. The Disney deal structure: $1 billion investment plus multi-year licensing for Disney, Marvel, Pixar, and Star Wars intellectual property; Disney confirmed no funds were transferred before the shutdown. The underlying Sora 2 model continues behind the ChatGPT paywall — the strategic retreat is from consumer social, not from video AI capability.
