### 1. nvidia-vera-agent-cpu-200b — 94/100 — WINNER

**Hook (9):** "$20 billion in Vera CPU sales — in one year, from a product that didn't exist 12 months ago. Jensen Huang calls it a '$200 billion market we've never addressed before.' All three winning-formula elements are present: $20B as the concrete revenue anchor (actual revenue, not a projection — more credible as a shock number); the "X isn't Y, it's Z" reframe embedded in the cost-curve argument (this isn't a hardware announcement — it's the signal that agent-compute costs are about to fall structurally, making today's expensive pilot the top of the cost curve, not the equilibrium); and "your competitors are piloting today" as the personal-stakes verb that puts the reader in a competitive context they cannot ignore. The $200B appears in line 2 as a CEO direct quote — an attributed statement, not an independent stat stacked alongside the $20B anchor — which satisfies the one-number-per-beat rule. No "you/your" opener. Passes all memory hard gates.

**Clarity (9):** One causal chain: $20B Vera revenue → agent-specific infrastructure is scaling now → cost-per-agent-action falls → founders building workflows now compound the advantage. Understandable in under 5 seconds. No decoding required.

**Readability (9):** Single-image format with a tight caption target. Brief content supports one dominant claim with natural whitespace. No dense blocks required.

**POV (9):** "Founders deferring agent adoption because of current economics are calibrating to the top of the cost curve, not the bottom." This is specific, uncomfortable, and opinionated — the reader who hears this and is currently deferring agent work feels directly challenged. Not neutral. Not advisory. The brief commits to a clear position on adoption timing.

**Consequence (9):** Two named consequences — the learning curve on workflow design is the scarce resource (compute gets cheaper, operational experience does not); founders building agentic workflows at today's prices compound the advantage when agent compute falls. Both consequences are specific and actionable for a 10-50 person company deciding whether to run agents in production now.

**Engagement (9):** Disagreement-Bait CTA (Option 2): "Jensen Huang says billions of AI agents are coming and every one needs dedicated compute. Founders who have been running agents agree and share what they learned. No forced binary. Open opinion invitation. Benchmark confirms this is the highest-performing CTA structure in the external dataset.

**Mobile (9):** Single-image format — caption targets under 90 words. Brief supports a tight, focused layout optimized for a mobile feed read-in-one-pass.

**Brand Fit (9):** Direct Grey AI territory. The practical adoption decision for founders — framed around competitive positioning and the organizational readiness gap, not hardware specs — is the core of Grey AI's audience positioning. The argument that "the scarce resource is operational experience, not compute" connects implicitly to the SPARK Suite thesis (AI literacy for non-technical professionals) without pitching it.
