## HEADLINE
Dairy Queen is deploying AI voice agents across US and Canadian drive-thrus at scale — an AI agent handling order-taking, upselling, and transaction completion with zero human involvement at approximately 90% accuracy.

## STRATEGY
- Audience: Founders and operators of small businesses in retail, food service, hospitality, or any customer-facing business with inbound order or inquiry volume — the people who say "we can't afford to miss calls" or "we lose sales when the line gets backed up."
- Current belief: AI-powered customer interaction is a Fortune 500 initiative. Fast food chains running AI drive-thrus are expensive experiments built for companies with massive R&D budgets. The technology that Wendy's or McDonald's pilots is not the same technology available to a 15-person retail operation or a regional service business.
- Reframe: Dairy Queen is a franchise operation — thousands of independently owned locations running on tight margins, not a tech lab. The AI that takes orders in their drive-thru is built by Presto, a vendor already operating inside Carl's Jr., Hardee's, Taco John's, and Fazoli's. That same vendor model — AI agent handles inbound, qualifies, and upsells — is already available through tools costing less per month than a part-time employee. The technology gap between a fast food franchise and a small operator is not budget. It is a decision.
- Practical consequence: Any customer-facing business with inbound call volume, order intake, or reservation handling that is still running on human-only interaction is now running a more expensive model than a fast food franchise. The comparison is uncomfortable — but it is accurate. The operational question has shifted from "can we afford AI customer interaction" to "can we afford not to have it while our competitors do."
- Core claim: Dairy Queen's drive-thru AI rollout is the mainstream proof point that AI agents handling commercial transactions at 90% accuracy is not an enterprise experiment — it is the current operating baseline for any customer-facing business willing to make the decision.
- Tension point: Presto's system was reported by Bloomberg in 2023 to include human workers in countries like the Philippines as a backup layer for AI failures — meaning the "90% AI" number may include an undisclosed human fallback. The transparency gap around what "AI drive-thru" actually means operationally has not been resolved.
- CTA direction: Forced Binary — is your customer interaction stack more or less automated than what a Dairy Queen franchise is running right now?

## KEY FACTS
- Dairy Queen deploying Presto AI voice agents in drive-thrus across US and Canada — wide franchise rollout following last year's successful test
- Presto's system achieves approximately 90% order accuracy; designed specifically to upsell customers
- Presto also operating in Carl's Jr., Hardee's, Taco John's, and Fazoli's — not a single-brand experiment
- Transparency risk: Bloomberg 2023 reporting indicated Presto's "AI" drive-thrus include human workers in the Philippines as backup — the accuracy number may include human-assisted completions
- Source credibility: The Verge, April 17, 2026; Presto commercial deployment confirmed across multiple major QSR chains

## CREDIBILITY LAYER
1. **Source attribution**: Presto, the commercial AI drive-thru vendor — deployed across Dairy Queen, Carl's Jr., Hardee's, Taco John's, and Fazoli's. This is a named company with confirmed commercial contracts at named QSR brands, not a pilot announcement.
2. **One concrete fact-checkable detail**: Presto's system achieves approximately 90% order accuracy — a named performance metric from an active commercial deployment. The Bloomberg 2023 reporting on human backup workers in the Philippines is a named, verifiable concern about what that 90% figure includes.
3. **Institutional self-report stat**: Dairy Queen explicitly stated the goal of the AI system is to speed up service and "encourage customers to add more food to their orders" — a direct institutional disclosure that the agent is designed to execute revenue-generating upsell behavior, not just take orders.

**Is this a news-jack?** No — the Dairy Queen rollout is an analytical anchor for the agentic commerce pattern available to small operators, not a 24-hour reactive take on a breaking story.
