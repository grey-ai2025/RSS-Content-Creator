## HEADLINE
A Harvard Business School study of nearly all US job vacancies from 2019 through March 2025 found postings for routine-task roles dropped 13% while postings for analytical, creative, and technical roles grew 20% — and the routine roles that survived are being deskilled (7% skill-diversity drop) before they disappear, not after.

## STRATEGY
- Audience: Founders and hiring managers at 10-50 person companies making 2026 headcount decisions — people allocating the same budget across roles and assuming the categories they've always hired for still carry the same strategic value.
- Current belief: AI threatens blue-collar and entry-level work first; knowledge workers in finance and tech are safer. The jobs disappearing are the obvious ones. My hiring strategy doesn't need to change until the market forces me — I'll see the signal before it costs me.
- Reframe: The deskilling finding (7% drop in skill diversity inside routine roles) is the early-warning signal that precedes the headcount cut. Finance and technology — the sectors most people assume are safe — led the reductions. The routine knowledge roles surviving today are not being preserved; they are being simplified before they are eliminated. The wages for those roles are being compressed now, while the headcount line still looks intact. For a founder, the budget implication is not about layoffs — it is about the return on each hire: the same dollar spent on an augmentation-prone role (analyst, clinical neuropsychologist, investment manager — roles that combine AI leverage with judgment and social skill) generates compounding value; the same dollar spent on a routine-task role is now funding a role mid-deskill.
- Practical consequence: The signal that a role is heading toward elimination is not a headcount cut — it is a narrowing of the skills required to perform it. That narrowing has already been measured across 900+ occupations using nearly all US job vacancies over six years. The hiring decision a founder makes this quarter is not about replacing a person with AI. It is about whether the role they are hiring for is in the 13% shrinking category or the 20% growing one.
- Core claim: AI is not eliminating knowledge work wholesale — it is bifurcating it, and the deskilling of surviving routine roles is the warning sign that arrives before the layoffs do.
- Tension point: Finance and technology — the sectors where most of this audience works and hires — produced the largest reductions in routine-task postings. The "knowledge work is safe" assumption is precisely wrong for the sectors relying on it most.
- CTA direction: Opinion-bait — founders and hiring managers have a strong, specific opinion about which roles on their teams are in the 13% category vs. the 20% category, and naming one out loud is the kind of response this post earns.

## KEY FACTS
- 13% decline in job postings for routine, structured-task occupations (2019–March 2025, nearly all US vacancies)
- 20% growth in postings for analytical, technical, and creative roles over the same period
- 7% drop in skill diversity within automation-prone occupations — the surviving roles are being simplified, not preserved
- Finance and technology sectors led the reductions — the sectors most often cited as "safe" for knowledge workers
- Dataset: nearly all US job vacancies, 2019–March 2025; methodology used ChatGPT to categorize 19,000+ job tasks across 900+ occupations
- Source credibility: Harvard Business School working paper — first released December 2024, updated August 2025; dataset covers the full pre- and post-ChatGPT labor market period

## GREY AI ANGLE
The bifurcation finding maps directly onto the "pattern-bound vs. judgment-heavy" framework Andrew Ng articulated on May 7: AI accelerates pattern-bound tasks and displaces the roles built around them, while judgment-heavy roles with AI leverage grow in demand. For any founder building an AI-native team, the HBS data is the six-year empirical confirmation of that framework — translated into hiring strategy. AI literacy in 2026 means knowing which roles on your team are on which side of the 13%/20% split, and making headcount decisions that compound into the 20% category rather than fund roles mid-deskill in the 13%.

## RAW MATERIAL
Harvard Business School working paper "Displacement or Complementarity? The Labor Market Impact of Generative AI" (December 2024, updated August 2025): Analyzed nearly all US job vacancies, 2019–March 2025. Key findings — 13% decline in postings for occupations dominated by structured, repetitive tasks; 20% growth in postings for analytical, technical, and creative roles; 7% reduction in skill diversity within automation-prone occupations. Largest reductions: finance and technology sectors. Augmentation-prone examples named: investment managers, financial analysts, microbiologists, clinical neuropsychologists — roles combining automatable tasks with judgment, social skills, and technical depth. Researcher quote (Srinivasan): "Retraining is essential for jobs where generative AI is reducing skill diversity." Methodology: ChatGPT used to categorize 19,000+ job tasks across 900+ occupations; augmentation scores built on proportion of exposed vs. unexposed tasks per occupation.
