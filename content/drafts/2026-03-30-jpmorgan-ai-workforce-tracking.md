## HEADLINE
JPMorgan is tracking AI tool usage across 65,000 employees and tying it to performance reviews.

## STRATEGY
- Audience: Enterprise leaders, managers, HR and L&D professionals, and individual contributors in tech-adjacent roles watching the AI mandate wave arrive.
- Current belief: AI adoption at work is a personal choice — some people will use it, some won't, and the organization tolerates that variance.
- Reframe: AI proficiency is now a managed performance metric, not a preference. The choice has been made for you — the only variable is your classification.
- Practical consequence: Professionals who are not actively building AI habits are accumulating a paper trail of non-adoption that will surface in performance cycles.
- Core claim: The largest enterprises have stopped asking employees to try AI — they are now measuring whether they do, and using that data against them or for them.
- Tension point: "Light user" is a career label you may already have without knowing it.
- CTA direction: Binary-question (are you a light user or heavy user at your company?)

## KEY FACTS
- 65,000 engineers and technologists at JPMorgan are now required to use AI tools as part of standard workflow
- Employees are classified as "light users" or "heavy users" based on tracked engagement with ChatGPT and Claude Code
- AI usage classifications may now directly influence individual performance reviews
- All AI outputs still require human verification before client-facing or decision-making use
- Source credibility: One of the world's largest financial institutions, managing trillions in assets, 300,000+ total employees

## GREY AI ANGLE
This is the clearest enterprise signal yet that AI literacy has moved from soft skill to managed competency — teams that have not built structured AI adoption habits are now accumulating measurable evidence of the gap. For any organization building an AI readiness strategy, JPMorgan's model is the benchmark for what accountability looks like at scale.

## RAW MATERIAL
JPMorgan is categorizing approximately 65,000 engineers and technologists as either "light users" or "heavy users" based on tracked engagement with AI tools including ChatGPT and Claude Code — and those classifications may now influence performance reviews. The bank requires human verification of all AI outputs before client-facing use, but the pressure to engage is embedded in the performance management system. This is one of the largest financial institutions in the world, managing trillions in assets, making this a precedent-setting signal for enterprise AI accountability at scale.
