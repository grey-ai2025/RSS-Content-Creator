## HEADLINE
A civil trial seeking more than $150 billion in damages and a complete OpenAI restructuring began jury selection today — with Satya Nadella on the witness list and a verdict that could force the dominant AI platform millions of businesses depend on to unwind its corporate structure.

## STRATEGY
- Audience: Founders and operators at 10-50 person companies who have built products, workflows, or client-facing features on top of OpenAI's API — and who evaluate their AI vendor stack by model quality and pricing, not by the legal stability of the vendor's corporate structure.
- Current belief: The Musk-Altman trial is tech-world drama. Whatever happens in court doesn't affect my API key, my pricing, or my ability to ship. Legal disputes between billionaires are background noise — not a vendor risk signal.
- Reframe: A court win for Musk would require OpenAI to structurally unwind its for-profit conversion — which directly affects its IPO path, its capital access, its pricing autonomy, and the architecture of the product any business currently using it has built on. This is not a governance dispute between founders. It is a live legal test of whether the platform millions of businesses depend on is allowed to exist in its current form. The trial is the vendor risk — the outcome is the variable your stack hasn't priced in.
- Practical consequence: Any founder building on OpenAI today is holding a platform dependency against a corporate structure that a court may rule was illegally obtained. That's not a reason to stop building. It's a reason to have a contingency: which workflows could route to an alternative model if OpenAI pricing, access, or governance changed materially overnight?
- Core claim: The Musk-Altman trial is not a spectator sport for founders building on OpenAI — it is the most consequential live vendor risk event in the AI stack since the platform became infrastructure.
- Tension point: Satya Nadella — Microsoft, OpenAI's largest investor — is on the witness list. The testimony that surfaces about how the for-profit conversion was actually decided will reveal the specific governance decisions that every business building on OpenAI is now downstream of.
- CTA direction: Reframe-question — does your team have a contingency for your AI workflows if the platform they run on changes materially in pricing, access, or governance?

## KEY FACTS
- Musk is seeking more than $150 billion in damages — one of the largest damage claims in tech legal history
- Witness list: Sam Altman, Elon Musk, Satya Nadella (Microsoft CEO), Greg Brockman, Mark Zuckerberg — the roster of people who built and funded the platform your API calls run through
- Core legal argument: OpenAI was founded as a nonprofit for humanity's benefit; its for-profit shift defrauded founding donors — if the court agrees, structural unwinding follows
- A court ruling for Musk could force OpenAI to restructure, halt IPO plans, and fundamentally change the platform millions of businesses depend on
- Source credibility: Court proceedings are public record; confirmed trial start date April 27, Oakland, California; named witnesses from publicly filed court documents
