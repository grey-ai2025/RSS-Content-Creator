## HEADLINE
Apple's next iOS release will allow users to replace Siri's AI responses with third-party models — turning the world's largest consumer device into an open AI platform and forcing enterprises to treat their AI assistant choice as a long-term platform commitment.

## KEY FACTS
- iOS 27 will let users select third-party AI chatbots — including downloaded models from the App Store — to handle Siri's responses
- Apple retains the gatekeeping layer: only App Store-approved models are eligible
- ChatGPT: 900 million weekly active users as of February 2026; Gemini: 750 million monthly active users — the iPhone's user base makes this the single largest AI distribution decision in tech
- This follows Google launching chat-history import tools from rival AI assistants the prior week — the platform consolidation is accelerating on both sides
- Source credibility: Apple's most reliable long-range reporter; confirmed by independent corroboration across multiple publications

## GREY AI ANGLE
For enterprise leaders managing AI tool choices across their teams, this shifts the calculus: picking an AI assistant is no longer a software subscription decision — it is a platform lock-in decision with iPhone-scale distribution consequences. The organizations evaluating AI assistants today are making choices that will define their integration architecture for the next product cycle.

## RAW MATERIAL
Apple's architecture choice is revealing: third-party models "fetch replies for Siri" — meaning Siri retains device-level control while outsourcing intelligence to whichever model the user (or enterprise IT policy) selects. Apple retains App Store gatekeeping as the accountability layer — the same mechanism it used when opening to third-party browsers and keyboards. The competitive frame: this is Apple acknowledging it cannot win the model capability race and choosing instead to own the distribution and device-integration layer. The enterprise implication is concrete: if your workforce is predominantly iPhone users, the model that wins Apple's default position gains ambient access to how your team works — calendar context, communication history, on-device data — before any enterprise procurement decision is made.
