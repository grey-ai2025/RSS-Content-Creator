## HEADLINE
AI-powered search now intermediates brand discovery — and 90% of AI citations come from third-party sources, not brand-owned websites.

## STRATEGY
- Audience: Marketing leaders, growth and demand-gen teams, content strategists, and brand managers at organizations that depend on organic search for visibility and lead generation.
- Current belief: SEO — optimizing your website to rank in Google's blue links — is still the primary lever for organic brand visibility.
- Reframe: The primary channel for brand discovery has shifted from the brand's own website to the sources that AI systems trust and cite. You can have a perfectly optimized website and be invisible in AI-generated answers.
- Practical consequence: Brands investing exclusively in traditional SEO are optimizing for a channel that AI Overviews are systematically replacing. Brands cited by AI earn 35% more organic clicks and 91% more paid clicks. Brands not cited by AI are losing both.
- Core claim: The game for organic brand visibility has moved from ranking in search results to being cited by AI — and the rules are fundamentally different.
- Tension point: Most marketing teams are still measuring success by metrics (impressions, rank, clicks) that don't capture whether their brand appears in AI-generated answers at all.
- CTA direction: Reframe-question (how much of your brand's organic visibility comes from AI citation vs. ranked pages?)

## KEY FACTS
- Brands cited in AI Overviews earned 35% more organic clicks and 91% more paid clicks than brands not cited
- 90% of AI citations come from third-party publishers and sources — not brand-owned websites (McKinsey)
- When users saw an AI search summary, click-through to traditional results dropped to 8% vs. 15% without a summary (Pew Research) — nearly half
- Impressions climbed 49% after AI Overviews launched; click-throughs dropped 30% (BrightEdge) — more visibility, less traffic
- 32% of marketing leaders now name GEO as their top strategic priority (Conductor)
- Source credibility: Pew Research, BrightEdge, McKinsey, Seer Interactive, and Conductor — five independent research organizations measuring the same shift from different angles

## GREY AI ANGLE
GEO and AEO are the marketing-layer equivalent of the AI literacy gap: organizations that understand how AI systems select and cite sources will achieve disproportionate visibility, while those optimizing for pre-AI metrics will find themselves invisible to AI-intermediated discovery. For AI-forward organizations, this is a competitive moat — being cited by AI at scale requires the same third-party authority and structured content that supports AI tool integration more broadly.
