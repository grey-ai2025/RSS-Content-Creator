## HEADLINE
Anthropic and OpenAI have simultaneously launched joint ventures with private equity firms to distribute enterprise AI services through portfolio companies — converting PE ownership networks into the new AI go-to-market layer and splitting the enterprise market into those inside the network and those outside it.

## STRATEGY
- Audience: Founders and operators at 10-50 person companies — particularly those that are PE-backed, considering PE investment, or competing with PE-backed peers — who have not yet realized that AI vendor access is being reorganized around ownership relationships, not procurement decisions.
- Current belief: I choose my AI vendor based on capability, pricing, and integration. The labs compete for my business. If a better model exists, I can access it through the standard API or enterprise tier. My AI stack is a technical and commercial decision I control.
- Reframe: Both labs have simultaneously built distribution networks through the PE firms that own thousands of mid-market companies. If your business is inside one of those ownership networks, your AI vendor may now be pre-selected by your investor. If you are outside those networks, you get the standard API — not the "forward-deployed engineer" embedded in your workflows, not the preferred pricing, not the dedicated implementation support. The enterprise AI market is bifurcating between two tiers: companies inside PE consortiums who get white-glove service, and everyone else who buys from the API docs.
- Practical consequence: For PE-backed founders, the relevant question is whether your sponsor is in the OpenAI or Anthropic consortium — and what obligations or advantages that creates for your AI stack decisions. For founders outside PE networks, the question is whether the "forward-deployed engineer" tier of AI implementation is becoming a competitive advantage that the standard tier cannot replicate. The go-to-market for enterprise AI just changed from "sales cycle" to "portfolio relationship."
- Core claim: PE firms owning thousands of portfolio companies just became the most important AI distribution channel ever built — and whether your company is inside or outside those ownership networks is now a factor in what level of AI support you can access.
- Tension point: The two ventures have no overlapping investors — Anthropic and OpenAI have each built a distinct ownership network. The enterprise AI market is not just bifurcating between AI haves and have-nots; it is bifurcating between two competing ownership consortiums, each with preferred-access AI and exclusive implementation teams. The labs are not competing for your procurement decision. They are competing for your investor.
- CTA direction: Reframe-question — if your AI vendor is now partly determined by who owns your company, has that changed how you think about either your cap table or your AI stack?
