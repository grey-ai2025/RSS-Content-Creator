## HEADLINE
The White House released a seven-point AI legislative blueprint that explicitly bars states from enacting AI regulations conflicting with federal policy — replacing a growing patchwork of state-level AI laws with a single, lighter-touch federal standard oriented around national AI dominance rather than precautionary safety.

## KEY FACTS
- The framework explicitly preempts state AI regulations that interfere with the "national strategy to achieve global AI dominance" — a direct block on California, Colorado, Texas, and others
- Child safety burdens shift from platform operators to parents under the new framework — a significant reversal of the tech-accountability model state legislators had been advancing
- The framework is a "legislative blueprint" — Congress must still act for key provisions to become law
- Source credibility: White House release; named states with prior legislation; independently corroborated by multiple publications

## GREY AI ANGLE
For enterprise AI governance teams, the signal is significant in both directions. The immediate upside: one federal standard replaces dozens of competing state requirements — reducing the regulatory surface area to track. The longer-term governance risk: a softer federal safety net means less external accountability structure to point to when explaining AI safety posture to boards, customers, and international partners operating under the EU AI Act. The organizations most exposed are those whose AI governance strategy was designed around state-law compliance rather than internal principles.

## RAW MATERIAL
The exact framing in the framework: preemption of state laws that interfere with "the national strategy to achieve global AI dominance" — this is an explicitly competitive, geopolitical rationale for deregulation, not a technical safety determination. The child safety reversal is the starkest practical example: platforms lose the accountability burden, parents gain it. Congress must still act — this is a blueprint, not enacted law — which means the patchwork problem is not yet resolved, only targeted. The EU AI Act is now the dominant international regulatory frame; US enterprises operating globally face a split-standard environment regardless of what Congress does. The three-year window between now and the next election creates uncertainty about whether federal preemption fully materializes.
