## HEADLINE
Google quietly released an AI dictation app that runs entirely on-device with no internet required — filtering filler words, cleaning up verbal stumbles, and outputting professional-quality text from natural speech, powered by local Gemma models.

## STRATEGY
- Audience: Executives, consultants, analysts, and knowledge workers who spend significant time producing written output and are hesitant to use cloud-based AI tools for sensitive dictation due to privacy concerns.
- Current belief: Useful AI dictation requires cloud connectivity — on-device speech-to-text only transcribes words literally, which still requires editing. Privacy-first AI tools trade capability for privacy. If you want quality output from voice, you have to accept that audio goes to a server.
- Reframe: On-device AI inference has reached the quality threshold where it can clean up natural speech — filler words, self-corrections, verbal stumbles — entirely locally, with output quality competitive with cloud-based tools. The privacy/capability trade-off just collapsed for voice-to-text workflows.
- Practical consequence: Any professional who dictates but avoids cloud tools for sensitive content — legal, strategic, client-facing, or personally sensitive communications — now has a free, zero-setup entry point to AI-quality voice output that stays entirely on-device. The barrier that prevented AI dictation adoption in privacy-sensitive workflows just dropped.
- Core claim: On-device AI inference is now capable enough to power production-quality voice-to-text with professional output formatting — and the privacy/capability trade-off that shaped enterprise AI dictation policy for the last three years no longer holds.
- Tension point: Google is entering a market (AI dictation) it previously ceded to third-party tools by giving away, free, the capability those paid tools have been selling. The competitive dynamic for productivity AI just shifted.
- CTA direction: Binary-question (has your organization's AI productivity toolkit been shaped by the assumption that privacy and quality are mutually exclusive for voice tools — and should that assumption be revisited?)

## KEY FACTS
- Built on Gemma ASR models — runs fully on-device after initial download, no internet required in offline mode
- Automatically filters filler words ("um," "uh") and mid-sentence corrections in real time — not just transcription, but active cleanup
- Transformation modes: Key Points, Formal, Short, Long — reformats dictated text into different output structures without re-recording
- Optional cloud mode uses Gemini models for enhanced quality when connectivity is available
- Can import custom vocabulary, names, and jargon from the user's Gmail account for domain-specific accuracy
- Competes directly with paid tools Wispr Flow, SuperWhisper, and Willow — a market Google is entering with a free, built-in-device-level tool
- Source credibility: Google (named company); App Store listing publicly accessible; Android version with system-wide keyboard integration referenced in store description — independently verifiable
