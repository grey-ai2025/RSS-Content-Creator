## HEADLINE
Institutional capital is shifting away from AI software and model companies toward physical data center infrastructure — with AI workloads projected at 30% of global data center capacity within two years and electricity demand rising 175% by 2030.

## KEY FACTS
- AI workloads projected to reach ~30% of total global data center capacity within two years
- Global data center electricity consumption may rise ~175% by 2030 vs. 2023 — equivalent to adding a top-10 power-consuming nation to the world's energy grid
- Analysis describes current investor behavior as a "flight to quality" — away from AI software hype, toward infrastructure operators
- Source credibility: Named major investment bank analysis; hyperscale cloud provider investment figures corroborated

## GREY AI ANGLE
The AI capability available to your organization in 2027 will not be constrained by algorithmic progress — it will be constrained by kilowatts, land, and fiber. Enterprise leaders building three-year AI roadmaps need a supply-side lens, not just a model-capability lens. The competitive moat is no longer the algorithm. It is the factory that runs it — and capital markets have already priced that shift.

## RAW MATERIAL
Key framing: "flight to quality" — investors concentrating capital in companies that own and operate large data centers and computing hardware, away from early-stage AI software excitement. Infrastructure bottlenecks identified: energy supply, land acquisition, fiber networks, grid connections. The 175% electricity increase by 2030 vs. 2023 is the most striking data point — makes abstract "AI infrastructure" tangible by converting it to power grid equivalence. Hyperscale cloud providers are investing tens of billions annually in new data centers and compute hardware — the capital is already moving. The strategic implication: building new models matters less than securing the underlying systems to run them.
