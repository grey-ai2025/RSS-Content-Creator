## HEADLINE
OpenAI released GPT-5.5 — its third major model in five months — pushing toward a single "super app" that consolidates coding, browsing, and enterprise AI into one vendor stack, forcing every team that locked into an AI workflow in the last six months to reassess.

## STRATEGY
- Audience: Founders and team leads at 10-50 person companies who have built AI workflows, agent pipelines, or standardized on a specific model version — and who treat their AI tool decisions as annual contracts rather than rolling reassessments.
- Current belief: AI model selection is a one-time decision. Pick the best model for your use case, integrate it, and revisit once per year at renewal. Benchmark scores determine which model wins.
- Reframe: Three major OpenAI releases in five months means the model your team standardized on six months ago is already two generations behind. The release cadence itself is the operational problem — not the individual model. Teams that built agent workflows around a specific version have a brittle dependency. The "super app" framing compounds this: OpenAI is not competing on model quality alone. It is consolidating coding (Codex), browsing, and enterprise AI into one platform — making the best-of-breed stack a progressively harder position to maintain.
- Practical consequence: Any team with annual AI tool contracts, agent workflows pinned to a specific model version, or a best-of-breed stack assembled against last year's benchmarks faces a structural reassessment rhythm problem — not a model quality problem. The fix is not picking the right model once. It is building a 90-day model review cadence into how the team operates.
- Core claim: The GPT-5.5 launch is not a model story — it is a procurement cadence story. Three major releases in five months means annual AI stack decisions are already outdated before renewal.
- Tension point: Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki said the last two years were "surprisingly slow" — implying the current acceleration is the new baseline, not a one-time burst. Teams that built one-time AI procurement processes are now running permanently behind.
- CTA direction: Reframe-question — does your team have a model reassessment cadence, or are you reviewing your AI stack the same way you review annual software licenses?

## GREY AI ANGLE
The GPT-5.5 release is the clearest available illustration of the AI organizational readiness gap: most teams built one-time AI procurement processes for a world where major releases happened annually. Three releases in five months means that gap is now structural. For any team building an AI adoption framework, this story surfaces a specific governance question: what is the team's reassessment cadence for model versions, agent workflow dependencies, and best-of-breed stack decisions — and is that cadence currently shorter than the model release cycle? AI literacy in 2026 includes understanding that a model pinned in an agent workflow is a technical debt decision, not just a capability preference.
