## HEADLINE
85% of organizations want to go fully agentic within three years; 76% say their current operations and infrastructure can't support it — a gap PwC describes as "adding sticky tape to parts of an operating model that is breaking."

## STRATEGY
- Audience: Founders and operators at 10-50 person companies who have deployed AI tools, are evaluating agentic workflows, and are measuring success by task completion volume rather than business outcomes — and who assume agent ROI is primarily a technology problem.
- Current belief: Deploying agents is the hard part. Once the agents are running, the ROI follows. If the agents are doing more work, that counts as productivity gain. The organizational infrastructure will adapt around the technology.
- Reframe: The 76% can't-support-it finding is not a technology readiness gap — it is an organizational design failure. Companies are deploying agents on top of existing broken processes. The agent accelerates the breakage. Real agent ROI only emerges when organizations redesign the process the agent will run, not just the tool. One enterprise in the research tripled its ROI after switching from measuring output (tasks completed) to measuring outcomes (business results). The measurement was the fix, not the technology.
- Practical consequence: For a 10-50 person company, the competitive advantage over enterprise in the agentic transition is redesign speed. An enterprise takes years to restructure workflows around agents; a founder-led company can do it in weeks. The metric shift is the most immediately actionable lever: if your team measures AI success by tasks completed or prompts generated, you are measuring the wrong thing and your ROI conversation is built on the wrong denominator.
- Core claim: The 85%/76% gap isn't an AI readiness problem — it's an organizational design problem, and the companies that redesign the process before deploying the agent will triple their ROI over the ones that deploy the agent and wait for the process to adapt.
- Tension point: 85% of organizations want to be fully agentic. 76% cannot support that transition with what they have. Those two numbers are the same organization — the same companies that want the outcome are the ones who haven't built the infrastructure to get there. The ambition and the readiness are inside the same building.
- CTA direction: Opinion-bait — every founder who has deployed agents and measured the results has a strong, specific opinion on whether they measured output or outcomes, and what the difference felt like.

## GREY AI ANGLE
The Agentic Business Transformation framework in this research is the clearest available template for what AI organizational readiness actually requires: technology stack redesign (agents as connective tissue, not add-ons), workforce redesign (hybrid human-AI teams with defined decision boundaries), and metric redesign (outcomes, not outputs). Teams that have deployed agents but are still measuring by output volume have the first pillar without the second and third. AI literacy at the operational level means knowing which pillar your current deployment is missing — and the 76% who can't support the agentic transition they want are missing at least two of the three.
