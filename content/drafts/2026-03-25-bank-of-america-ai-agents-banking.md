## HEADLINE
A major US bank has deployed AI agents to approximately 1,000 financial advisers — handling client queries, investment recommendations, and daily workflow management in real time — while its existing AI systems already do work equivalent to 11,000 full-time employees.

## KEY FACTS
- Initial deployment covers ~1,000 financial advisers in client-facing advisory roles — not back-office automation
- Existing virtual assistant already handles work equivalent to ~11,000 full-time employees across the institution
- JPMorgan, Wells Fargo, and Goldman Sachs are running parallel AI productivity deployments
- An industry analyst described current banking AI products as "a little boring from a product standpoint" — functionality has not yet matched the hype
- Source credibility: Named institution, named enterprise AI framework, named industry analyst on record

## GREY AI ANGLE
AI agents have crossed from back-office efficiency into client-facing financial advice — the room where trust, judgment, and regulatory exposure converge. For leaders in any client-facing professional services role, this deployment marks the threshold: AI agents are no longer being tested in low-risk workflows. They are operating where decisions have real consequences.

## HOOK OPTIONS

**Provocative:** AI just entered the room where your financial adviser sits with clients. Your industry is probably next — and "we're still evaluating" is not a deployment strategy.

**Binary:** Back-office AI: automates forms, flags anomalies, handles routine queries. Client-facing AI: handles investment recommendations, manages real-time advisory workflows, operates where errors have legal and financial consequences. Most organizations are ready for one. Very few are ready for the other.

**Stat Gap:** One bank's AI already does the work of 11,000 employees. They just deployed agents to 1,000 more advisers — in the rooms where clients make investment decisions. The back-office phase is over.
