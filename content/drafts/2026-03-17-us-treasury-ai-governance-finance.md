## HEADLINE
The US Treasury, working with over 100 financial institutions, has published a structured AI governance framework with 230 control objectives — organized by four stages of AI adoption — specifically addressing the risks existing technology frameworks have not covered.

## KEY FACTS
- 230 control objectives calibrated to four AI adoption stages: Initial, Minimal, Evolving, and Embedded
- Developed collaboratively with over 100 financial institutions, regulators, and technical bodies
- Risks specifically addressed: algorithmic bias, decision-making opacity, LLM unpredictability, and systemic risk from AI making consequential decisions without oversight
- Source credibility: US Treasury / Cyber Risk Institute; framework adapts the NIST AI Risk Management Framework for financial sector use

## GREY AI ANGLE
Every financial services organization now has a baseline governance expectation that explicitly covers LLM unpredictability and agentic AI decision-making — not just legacy algorithmic systems. The four-stage adoption model removes the "we're only experimenting" defense. More broadly, financial sector governance frameworks historically travel upstream into other industries — this is the template every enterprise AI governance program will eventually face.

## RAW MATERIAL
The four adoption stages — Initial, Minimal, Evolving, Embedded — mean no institution can claim the framework doesn't apply by arguing they're "only experimenting." The four functional governance areas (Govern, Map, Measure, Manage) are adapted directly from NIST AI RMF for financial sector-specific risks. Cross-functional requirement: technology, risk, compliance, and business teams must coordinate — no single team owns it. Specific risks covered include LLM unpredictability, which existing tech governance frameworks (written before LLMs existed) do not address. Historical precedent: financial sector governance frameworks routinely become the template adopted by enterprise software, healthcare, and professional services sectors in subsequent cycles.
