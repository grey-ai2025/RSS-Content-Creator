## HEADLINE
Only 11% of enterprises have successfully deployed and scaled AI agents — and the gap between them and the other 89% is not a spending problem, it's a process design problem.

## STRATEGY
- Audience: C-suite executives, enterprise AI leads, digital transformation directors, and strategy leaders responsible for AI ROI at scale.
- Current belief: AI adoption is primarily a technology and budget problem — the organizations spending more and buying better models will get ahead.
- Reframe: The leading 11% are not spending more; they are designing differently. They rebuild processes first and embed agents inside reimagined workflows. The 89% are layering AI on top of existing processes and getting incremental gains at best.
- Practical consequence: Every month an enterprise applies AI to existing workflows instead of redesigning those workflows, it compounds a structural disadvantage that more spending cannot close.
- Core claim: The enterprise AI gap is not a technology gap — it is an organizational design gap that spending cannot fix.
- Tension point: Most organizations are measuring AI ROI the wrong way. Their benchmarks reward incremental gains and conceal how far behind they actually are relative to the 11%.
- CTA direction: Reframe-question (is your AI strategy layering tools or redesigning processes?)

## KEY FACTS
- Only 11% of organizations have successfully deployed and scaled AI agents to drive enterprise-wide outcomes — despite massive investment
- Average planned AI spend over the next 12 months: $186 million per organization (ASPAC leading at $245M)
- 64% report AI delivers "meaningful outcomes" — but this masks a 20-point gap between AI leaders (82%) and peers (62%)
- Only 20% of experimental-stage organizations feel confident managing AI risk, vs. 49% among AI leaders — governance enables speed, not the reverse
- Source credibility: KPMG Global AI Pulse — inaugural survey from one of the world's largest professional services firms

## GREY AI ANGLE
The governance finding inverts the standard enterprise narrative: mature AI risk frameworks accelerate deployment and scaling among the leading 11%, while organizations treating governance as an afterthought remain stuck at experimentation. For teams building AI readiness programs, this is the most actionable signal in the data — governance is not the braking system; it is the enabling infrastructure.

KPMG's inaugural Global AI Pulse survey: only 11% of organizations have successfully deployed and scaled AI agents to drive enterprise-wide outcomes, despite a weighted average AI spend of $186M planned for the next year. The key differentiator is not budget — it is deployment philosophy. AI leaders redesign processes first, then embed agents; the 89% layer AI onto existing workflows and capture only incremental gains. The governance inversion is the most counterintuitive data point: organizations with mature governance frameworks deploy faster and scale further, with 49% of AI leaders confident in risk management vs. only 20% of experimental-stage organizations. The 64% "meaningful outcomes" headline masks a structural 20-point gap between leaders and peers.
