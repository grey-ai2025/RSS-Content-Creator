## HEADLINE
OpenAI models are now available on Amazon Bedrock and Google Cloud — ending the arrangement that made Azure the only enterprise channel for running OpenAI in production, and collapsing the thesis that Microsoft owns OpenAI's distribution.

## STRATEGY
- Audience: Founders and CTOs at 10-50 person companies who either (a) chose Azure specifically to access OpenAI in production, or (b) chose AWS or Google Cloud and treated OpenAI as an Azure-only option — and who are now making vendor and cloud decisions based on a competitive landscape that changed this week.
- Current belief: Microsoft and OpenAI are effectively one platform decision. To run OpenAI models at scale in production, you need Azure. Teams on AWS or Google Cloud either live without frontier OpenAI access or accept a multi-cloud dependency. The "OpenAI = Azure" equation is stable because it is the deal — not a default.
- Reframe: The exclusivity arrangement is over. OpenAI models are already running on Amazon Bedrock and Google Cloud. Any team that chose Azure to secure OpenAI access is now on Azure for Azure's own merits — the OpenAI moat is gone. Any team that chose AWS or GCP and treated OpenAI as out of reach now has native access. The competitive calculus for cloud vendor selection just changed for every organization that used the OpenAI-Azure relationship as a deciding factor — which is most of them.
- Practical consequence: Every founder and CTO who locked in Azure partially on the OpenAI-access thesis now has two decisions to re-examine: whether Azure remains the best infrastructure choice on its own merits, and whether the OpenAI models they wanted are now accessible from their existing cloud without switching costs. Teams on AWS or GCP should evaluate OpenAI access on their current platform before assuming they need Azure for frontier model availability.
- Core claim: The "Microsoft owns OpenAI's distribution" thesis is dead — and any cloud vendor decision made on that assumption is now running on an outdated premise.
- Tension point: OpenAI missed its Q1 revenue targets and restructured its largest partnership in the same reporting cycle — suggesting the distribution expansion was not a negotiated win from a position of strength. The broader distribution may be urgent for OpenAI's IPO narrative more than it is a strategic gift to enterprise customers. Teams accessing OpenAI on AWS or GCP should track whether the preferential pricing and capacity guarantees that came with Azure exclusivity follow the models to new platforms.
- CTA direction: Opinion-bait — did your team's cloud vendor decision factor in the OpenAI-Azure relationship, and does this week's change affect that decision?

## KEY FACTS
- OpenAI models now available on Amazon Bedrock and Google Cloud — Azure exclusivity ended
- Microsoft retains a revenue share arrangement and preferred partner status but loses exclusivity
- OpenAI reported Q1 revenue below internal targets in the same cycle — distribution expansion likely motivated by IPO narrative requirements
- AWS and Google Cloud both confirmed OpenAI model availability as part of their enterprise AI offerings
- The change eliminates the structural reason many enterprise teams chose or maintained Azure for AI workloads
- Source credibility: The Verge reporting with named platform confirmations (Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud); confirmed by OpenAI and Microsoft statements; OpenAI Q1 revenue miss confirmed by multiple outlets — named companies, named platforms, named financial context
