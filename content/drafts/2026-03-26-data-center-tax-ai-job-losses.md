## HEADLINE
A US senator proposed taxing data centers to fund retraining for workers displaced by AI — citing a VC writing software investments to zero and a major law firm that has stopped hiring first-year associates.

## KEY FACTS
- Entry-level job postings in the US have declined 35% since 2023
- A venture capitalist told the senator he is writing software startup investments "down to zero" partly due to AI capabilities
- A major law firm confirmed it has stopped hiring first-year associates because AI handles that work
- AI holds lower public approval than ICE in a recent NBC News poll: 46% negative vs. 26% positive
- The tax proposal has not yet been introduced as formal legislation
- Source credibility: On-record senator at a named policy summit; supporting data from Revelio Labs and NBC News polling

## GREY AI ANGLE
The implicit social contract between the AI industry and the workforce is now being demanded in writing. For enterprise leaders, the data center tax debate is the opening move in a longer negotiation about who bears the transition cost when AI displaces workers — and organizations that have not yet built an answer to that question will find it increasingly difficult to avoid.

## RAW MATERIAL
The senator's framing — extracting a "pound of flesh" from the data center industry — is the most direct political language applied to the AI social contract debate so far. Virginia context: the state's data center tax breaks cost nearly $2 billion per year in lost revenue; Henrico County already used data center tax revenue to fund affordable housing. The moratorium counter-proposal from Sanders/AOC, introduced the same day, frames the political range: the debate is between stopping the build-out and taxing it, not between building it and building it responsibly. The most consequential data point for enterprise audiences: the NBC News approval polling. When AI has lower public trust than ICE, the political energy for accountability measures is significant regardless of which specific bill advances.
