## HEADLINE
Bain sized the US agentic AI automation market at $100 billion — with vendors currently capturing only $4–6 billion — and warned the window for SaaS incumbents is measured in quarters, not years.

## STRATEGY
- Audience: Founders and operators at 10-50 person companies still running manual cross-system workflows (scheduling, referral processing, finance reconciliation, vendor management) who assume the AI displacement of their tools is a 3-5 year horizon.
- Current belief: The SaaS tools I pay for will add AI features over time. I will adopt when the integrations mature. The disruption story is about big enterprise software companies, not the CRM and ERP tools I actually use.
- Reframe: Bain's $100B estimate is not a prediction of future demand — it is a map of where enterprise budget is already being allocated, specifically targeting the "coordination work" that spans the exact systems small businesses run on: ERP, CRM, email, support. The $4–6B currently captured means over 90% of that budget is in play. The window question is not whether it opens — it is whether the AI-native competitors accumulating deployment experience now will reach your tools before your current vendor ships their first agent feature.
- Practical consequence: Any business still using manual hand-offs across systems — a spreadsheet between the CRM and the finance tool, a human scheduling coordinator between the intake form and the calendar — is sitting on top of the highest-priority automation target in Bain's analysis. The operations/COGS category alone is a $26B addressable market. If you are in that category and you have not deployed an agent layer, you are the market.
- Core claim: 90% of the $100B agentic AI opportunity is uncaptured, and the highest-value targets are the exact coordination workflows most 10-50 person businesses still run manually.
- Tension point: Bain's chairman of the technology practice said the timeframe is "measured in quarters, not years." That framing does not come from a consulting firm's wish to sound urgent — it comes from watching AI-native competitors accumulate deployment experience at a rate that is compressing the adoption window faster than SaaS incumbents can respond.
- CTA direction: Opinion-bait — every founder has a specific cross-system coordination workflow they know is inefficient and has quietly assumed "someone will fix this." Naming it publicly is the response this post earns.

## KEY FACTS
- $100 billion US addressable market for agentic AI SaaS automation (additional $100B in Canada, Europe, Australia, NZ)
- Only $4–6 billion currently captured — over 90% of the market is untapped
- Highest-value automation targets by function: Operations/COGS (~$26B), Sales (~$20B), Customer Support, Finance, R&D each $6–12B
- Customer support and R&D: 40–60% of tasks automatable; Finance/HR: 35–45%
- Source credibility: Bain & Company technology practice — second in a five-part series on software in the AI age, produced by the firm's technology practice chairman

This is a specific, function-level claim a decision-maker can verify by auditing their own support and engineering workflows against publicly available AI agent capability descriptions.

This is a named advisory firm reporting what the companies it advises are currently doing with budget, not an academic projection. The $4–6B currently captured vs. $100B addressable is a vendor self-assessment gap: the companies selling into this market acknowledge they have captured less than 5% of it.

**Is this a news-jack?** No.

## GREY AI ANGLE
The 90% gap between potential and current capture is an AI literacy problem masquerading as a market opportunity. Most operators do not know which of their coordination workflows fall inside the $100B target — they experience them as annoying process friction, not as automation-ready tasks with a named dollar value. For any founder building an AI adoption framework, Bain's function-level breakdown (Operations, Sales, Finance, Customer Support, R&D) is the audit checklist: identify which cross-system hand-offs in each function are still manual, then map them against the 35–60% automation readiness estimates by function. The organizations that do this audit now will make deployment decisions before AI-native competitors reach their vendor relationships.
