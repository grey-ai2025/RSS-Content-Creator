## HEADLINE
Databricks has launched an AI-native security platform that uses AI agents to detect and respond to threats across the same data infrastructure it already manages for enterprise clients — entering the security market through two targeted acquisitions funded by its recent $5 billion raise.

## KEY FACTS
- The platform (Lakewatch) performs classic threat detection and investigation using AI agents powered by Claude — not rule-based SIEM logic
- Databricks acquired two startups: a data control plane company (raised $12M) and a two-person team that launched its product only in November 2025
- One acquisition's co-founder was previously chief scientist at Splunk, creator of the Search Processing Language
- $5 billion funding round closed last month; acquisitions were explicitly funded from this war chest
- Source credibility: Named company, named acquisitions with funding history, named individuals with verifiable prior roles

## GREY AI ANGLE
The next phase of enterprise AI governance is not policy — it is infrastructure. When the company managing your data pipelines also runs agents that monitor threats inside those same pipelines, the security and AI operations functions converge. For enterprise technology leaders, this signals where AI governance is heading: away from perimeter defenses and toward agents that audit other agents from inside the stack.

## HOOK OPTIONS

**Provocative:** Your data platform just became your security platform. If you didn't notice the line blurring, your threat model is already out of date.

**Binary:** Legacy SIEM: external system monitoring your infrastructure with predefined rules. AI-native SIEM: agents running inside your data layer, trained on your own operational patterns, flagging anomalies in real time. Two different architectures — and only one was built for a world where AI agents are the threat surface.

**Contrarian:** Most enterprise security conversations focus on protecting AI from external attacks. The more interesting move is building AI that monitors your infrastructure from the inside — and the company that already owns your data layer just made that move.

## RAW MATERIAL
Lakewatch is being positioned as a SIEM — Security Information and Event Management — but built with AI agents rather than the rule-based detection logic that defined the category. The vertical integration logic: Databricks already manages the data infrastructure enterprises use for analytics and AI training; Lakewatch adds the security monitoring layer on top of that same infrastructure. The Splunk connection (SiftD co-founder Steve Zhang was Splunk's chief scientist) is a direct competitive signal — Databricks is going after Splunk's legacy SIEM customers. Antimatter's "data control plane" concept is now the governance architecture inside Lakewatch: a mechanism that lets enterprises deploy agents securely while protecting sensitive data from agent access overreach.
