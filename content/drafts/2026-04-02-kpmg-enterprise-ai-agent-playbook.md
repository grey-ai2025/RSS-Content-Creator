## HEADLINE
KPMG's global survey finds only 11% of enterprises have reached scaled AI agent deployment — despite average planned AI spend of $186 million — and the defining differentiator is process redesign before deployment, not budget size.

## STRATEGY
- Audience: Senior enterprise leaders, AI strategy owners, COOs, and technology executives responsible for AI adoption outcomes at scale.
- Current belief: AI ROI is primarily a function of investment size and tool selection. More spend, better tools, better results.
- Reframe: The 89% of enterprises not seeing scaled AI results are spending at roughly the same rate as the 11% who are. The variable isn't budget — it is whether organizations redesigned their processes before deploying AI, or layered AI onto existing workflows and hoped for compounding returns.
- Practical consequence: The 20-point value gap between AI leaders and peers compounds over a 3-5 year competitive horizon. Every quarter spent layering AI onto unreformed processes is a quarter of compounding gap — not neutral experimentation.
- Core claim: Most enterprises are not behind on AI investment — they are behind on the process redesign that makes AI investment compound.
- Tension point: $186 million in planned spend, and 89% have nothing to show at scale. The question most boards haven't asked: what did the 11% do differently?
- CTA direction: Reframe-question (is your AI strategy a spending decision or a process redesign decision?)

## KEY FACTS
- Only 11% of global enterprises have reached the stage of deploying and scaling AI agents with enterprise-wide results
- Average planned AI spend over the next 12 months: $186 million per organization globally
- AI leaders vs. everyone else: 82% see meaningful value vs. 62% — a 20-point gap that compounds over a 3-5 year horizon
- Critical failure mode: layering AI onto existing workflows produces only incremental gains; process redesign first is what unlocks compounding returns
- Among AI leaders: 49% feel confident managing AI risks vs. 20% among those still experimenting — governance capability enables faster, safer scaling, not slower
- Source credibility: KPMG Global AI Pulse survey — first quarterly edition; global enterprise sample

## GREY AI ANGLE
The KPMG data reframes the AI readiness conversation: organizations that embed governance and process redesign early scale faster and with more confidence — not slower. For teams building AI literacy programs, this is the empirical argument that governance is a speed enabler, not a drag. The 49% vs. 20% confidence gap between AI leaders and laggards on risk management suggests that organizations which skip governance infrastructure to move faster are, in practice, moving slower.
