## HEADLINE
AI has not yet caused widespread job losses — but a measurable performance gap between workers who use AI fluently and those who don't is already forming and widening.

## KEY FACTS
- "Power users" — workers with genuine AI tool fluency — are pulling ahead of peers in measurable output and performance metrics
- The gap is already visible and described by researchers as growing with each capability improvement
- Entry-level job postings in the US have declined 35% since 2023
- A major law firm has stopped hiring first-year associates because AI now handles that work
- Source credibility: AI developer internal research presented at a major policy summit in Washington D.C.

## GREY AI ANGLE
The AI readiness question has shifted from "will my job exist?" to "will I be in the group that's pulling ahead or falling behind?" For team leaders, this is an organizational health problem: if part of your team is compounding AI proficiency and another part isn't, you're building a performance inequality problem inside your own org right now.

## HOOK OPTIONS
Pick the 3 strongest hook styles for this story. Write one attempt for each:

**Provocative:** AI hasn't taken your job yet. But some of your colleagues are using it to outperform you by a margin that's getting harder to close.

**Binary:** Two types of workers exist right now. Those pulling ahead with AI — better output, faster delivery, expanding scope. And those who aren't — doing the same work, at the same pace, as the gap compounds. You already know which group is more promotable.

**Stat Gap:** Entry-level job postings are down 35% since 2023. AI hasn't displaced workers en masse yet. It has displaced the floor that protected them from each other.

## RAW MATERIAL
The finding comes from actual AI tool usage data, not a survey — making the "power user pulling ahead" pattern an observed behavior, not a self-reported intention. The 35% decline in entry-level job postings (Revelio Labs) is the structural backdrop: displacement at the hiring level is already happening for specific roles even before the aggregate numbers move. The Accenture/Wharton framing this week is the leadership parallel: "intelligence may be scalable, but accountability is not" — the implication being that the output gap compounds while the accountability gap stays fixed on leaders. Harvard Business School GenAI finding corroborates: AI boosts output but cannot replace expertise — meaning power users with expertise plus AI proficiency are pulling ahead fastest.
