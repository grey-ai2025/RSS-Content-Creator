## HEADLINE
Okta's CEO argues that every AI agent operating inside an enterprise needs its own identity layer — authentication, permissions, and audit trail — just like a human employee, and most companies are deploying agents without any of it.

## STRATEGY
- Audience: CISOs, compliance officers, enterprise architects, and AI governance leads responsible for securing and auditing agentic AI deployments inside their organizations.
- Current belief: AI agents are a capability layer — you deploy them, they do tasks, and existing security and identity infrastructure covers them the same way it covers software applications.
- Reframe: AI agents are not applications. They act, access data, and make decisions on behalf of the organization in ways that require the same identity accountability as a human employee. Deploying agents without a formal identity layer is not a temporary gap — it is an active compliance and security liability.
- Practical consequence: Organizations cannot answer the most basic audit question — which agent took which action, with what permissions, and when — unless agent identity is built into the deployment from the start. That gap is compounding every day agents operate without it.
- Core claim: "Intelligence may be scalable, but accountability is not" — and the accountability layer for AI agents is agent identity infrastructure, not post-hoc policy.
- Tension point: The same week enterprises are racing to expand agentic AI deployments, the identity problem underneath those deployments remains largely unaddressed.
- CTA direction: Verdict-close (agent identity is not an optional governance layer — it is the baseline accountability requirement for agentic AI)

## KEY FACTS
- Okta CEO: every AI agent needs authentication, permissioning, and an audit trail — the identical infrastructure required for human employees
- Most enterprises are currently running agents with human-level credentials — creating compounding security and compliance risk
- Without agent identity, organizations cannot answer: which agent took which action, with what permissions, and when
- The "SaaSpocalypse" concept: AI agents executing tasks end-to-end may render traditional SaaS applications obsolete — McKinnon says it would be "naive" not to prepare
- Source credibility: Named CEO of a major enterprise identity provider, on-record in a published interview

## GREY AI ANGLE
Agent identity is the accountability infrastructure the Accenture/Wharton "intelligence may be scalable but accountability is not" framing points directly at. Every team deploying agentic AI workflows in 2026 is making an implicit governance decision: either agents get their own identity layer with scoped permissions and audit trails, or the organization accepts accountability gaps it cannot see or measure. This is not a Okta-specific issue — it applies to every enterprise agent deployment regardless of vendor.
