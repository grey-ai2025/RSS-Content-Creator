## HEADLINE
Iran's Revolutionary Guard published a video naming a $500 billion AI data center campus as a specific military target — the first time AI infrastructure has been explicitly identified as a geopolitical strike target by a state military force.

## STRATEGY
- Audience: Enterprise technology leaders, CISOs, risk and business continuity officers, and any executive responsible for infrastructure resilience in organizations that depend on cloud-based AI services.
- Current belief: AI infrastructure risk is primarily a software and cybersecurity problem — model vulnerabilities, data breaches, prompt injection. Physical infrastructure sits behind layers of provider-managed security and is not a meaningful risk variable for enterprise continuity planning.
- Reframe: AI infrastructure is now a named military target. The same week, North Korea compromised software supply chains and an AI company's own security tool received a CVE. The threat model for enterprise AI continuity has crossed into kinetic, geopolitical territory that cloud SLAs were never designed to address.
- Practical consequence: Any enterprise whose critical workflows depend on cloud-based AI services is exposed to geographic concentration risk they likely have not modeled. A successful strike on a major data center campus would cascade disruption across every model, agent, and application built on top of it. Business continuity frameworks built before AI infrastructure became a military target need to be updated.
- Core claim: AI infrastructure geographic concentration is now a board-level enterprise risk — not a provider-managed technical detail — and it belongs in the risk register alongside cybersecurity and vendor concentration.
- Tension point: Nearly half of US data center projects are already delayed due to tariff impacts on power infrastructure components. The timeline for building resilient, geographically distributed AI infrastructure is getting longer at exactly the moment the threat is becoming more concrete.
- CTA direction: Reframe-question (does your enterprise continuity plan model what happens when the data center running your AI stack is disrupted by a physical threat, not a cyberattack?)

## GREY AI ANGLE
AI infrastructure resilience is the governance layer that enterprise AI readiness frameworks have systematically omitted. Most enterprise AI risk assessments model cyberattacks, vendor failures, and data breaches — but not the physical concentration risk created by dependence on a small number of large data center campuses that now sit at the intersection of geopolitical conflict. AI literacy in 2026 includes understanding that the models and agents your organization depends on run somewhere — and that "somewhere" is now a named variable in active military planning. For teams building AI governance programs, infrastructure geographic risk is the blind spot that belongs in the next risk register update.
