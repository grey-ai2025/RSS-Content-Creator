## HEADLINE
One of the most widely used AI coding platforms confirmed its newest model — marketed as "frontier-level" and proprietary — is built on an open-source Chinese AI model. The disclosure was not proactive; it was surfaced by an external researcher through code analysis.

## KEY FACTS
- The platform's Composer 2 model is confirmed to be built on Kimi 2.5, developed by a Beijing-based AI lab backed by Alibaba and a major Chinese venture firm
- Kimi 2.5 was released as open source in January 2026 — technically available for anyone to build on, but not disclosed in the product's public positioning
- The architecture was identified not by the company, but by an outside researcher examining the code
- Source credibility: Company confirmed the finding after external exposure; characterized by a major tech publication as "particularly fraught" given current US-China AI tensions

## GREY AI ANGLE
Enterprise AI procurement has a new due diligence gap: the model stack beneath the tool you purchased. CTOs and security teams now need to ask not just "what does this AI do?" but "what model is this AI actually running, and where did it come from?" This incident is the clearest example yet of what AI supply chain opacity looks like in practice — and it arrives at the same moment governments are tightening export controls on AI infrastructure.

## RAW MATERIAL
The reveal mechanism matters: the company did not disclose the underlying model — an outside researcher identified it through code analysis. The vendor's public positioning used the phrase "frontier-level coding intelligence" without referencing the model origin. The backing structure: the underlying model comes from a lab backed by Alibaba and HongShan (formerly Sequoia China). Current US-China AI technology export controls are tightening simultaneously — making model provenance a compliance question, not just a transparency preference. The enterprise procurement question this surfaces: when you license an AI tool, your vendor agreement does not typically specify which base model it runs — and that agreement may need to change.
