## HEADLINE
The European Parliament voted to delay the EU AI Act's high-risk compliance deadline by 16 months — while simultaneously backing an urgent ban on nudify apps — revealing a governance framework that is moving slowly on systemic risk and quickly on visible harm.

## KEY FACTS
- High-risk AI compliance deadline pushed from August 2026 to December 2027 — a 16-month delay
- Sector-specific systems (medical devices, toys) proposed at August 2028
- AI content watermarking deadline delayed from August 2026 to November 2026
- Vote passed by a large majority — but parliament must still negotiate with the European Council before changes become law
- The EU has previously missed its own guidance publication deadlines
- Source credibility: European Parliament vote, named regulatory framework, confirmed dates

## GREY AI ANGLE
Your EU AI Act compliance roadmap just shifted — again. For enterprise teams who built their AI governance timeline around August 2026 dates, this is an operational revision, not reassurance. The delay buys time but doesn't reduce the ultimate compliance burden. More importantly, the EU's pattern — missing its own deadlines while moving urgently on specific harms — tells you something about where enforcement energy will actually concentrate.

## RAW MATERIAL
The parliamentary mechanics matter: the vote is a position, not law. The parliament must negotiate with the European Council (27 member states) before changes can be legally implemented — meaning it's unclear whether the delay will take effect before the original August 2026 deadline arrives. The nudify ban context: the EU launched an investigation into X/Grok over sexualized deepfakes earlier this year; the ban passed in the same session as the compliance delay. The juxtaposition is the brief — urgent action on explicit harm, extended timeline on systemic risk. The exemption language in the nudify ban: AI systems with "effective safety measures preventing users from creating such images" are exempt — meaning the bar is technical implementation, not intent declaration. US-EU divergence framing: as the US moves toward lighter-touch federal preemption and the EU delays its own high-risk deadline, the international split-standard environment for global enterprises gets wider, not narrower.
