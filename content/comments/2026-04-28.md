### 6. Hugo Bowne-Anderson — AI agents fail in production via cascading errors

**Post link:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hugo-bowne-anderson-045939a5_the-most-common-way-ai-agents-fail-in-production-activity-7285773503784529920-TxKl

**Post snippet:** Hugo Bowne-Anderson shares findings from ZenML's analysis of 400+ LLM deployments. The most common production failure: agents get lost and compound errors — small mistakes build on each other because nothing tells the agent its first step was wrong. Backtracking is architecturally difficult once an agent is many steps in.

**Angle:** The cascading error finding maps directly to today's post — agents fail in production not because they're incapable, but because the workflow design has no error recovery logic built in.

**Comment:**
Cascading errors are a workflow design problem as much as a model problem. When agents operate in processes that have no explicit checkpoints or rollback logic — designed for humans who naturally pause and ask for clarification — a wrong step in step two becomes a structural failure by step ten. Building error recovery into the workflow before deployment is the work that happens before any of the capability questions matter.

**Post snippet:** Andrew Ng explains the Reflection design pattern for agentic workflows: generate output, prompt the model to critique its own work, revise, iterate. He emphasizes that this mimics human quality control and produces "surprising performance gains" across coding, writing, and QA tasks. The critical skill for teams building agentic systems: a disciplined eval and error analysis process.

**Angle:** The eval discipline point is underappreciated. Most teams building agents skip evals entirely — they demo the agent, it looks good, they ship. The reflection pattern requires knowing what "correct" looks like before you build the critique loop.

**Comment:**
The eval discipline point is the one teams skip. Reflection and self-critique loops only work if the team has defined what correct output looks like before the agent runs — and most agentic deployments skip that step entirely. The agent runs, it looks fine in the demo, nobody builds the eval framework. Reflection without a definition of "right" is just the agent confidently repeating its own mistakes.
