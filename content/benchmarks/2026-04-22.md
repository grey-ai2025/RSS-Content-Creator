# LinkedIn Influencer Benchmark — 2026-04-22

## Internal Benchmark — Grey AI Top Performers

**April 6 Audit Winning Formula (confirmed active as of April 22):**
Concrete dollar/multiple anchor + "X isn't Y, it's Z" reframe + personal-stakes verb — all three elements must appear in the hook line itself, not staged across the post or buried in the body. This formula is corroborated by the April 22 cross-influencer analysis: 7 of 14 top posts use a structurally identical combination of a specific number, a category reframe, and a stakes verb in the opening sentence.

**Confirmed data from project memory and prior audit files:**
- Carousel remains the default format for Grey AI. April 13 audit: 0 carousels = 68% impression drop; carousel ER is 4.6x text ER for Grey AI's audience specifically.
- Bold-Unicode openers suppress reach. April 6 audit: 1/16 posts cleared 100 impressions when bold Unicode was used in the opening line.
- Vary first words across the week. Repeating "you/your" as the opening word degrades algorithmic freshness signals.
- The hook must front-load all three formula elements. A post that buries the stat on slide 3 loses the reader before the reframe lands.

**Implication for post-writer and draft-writer agents:**
Every Grey AI hook must contain all three formula elements in one sentence. Example application for April 22 content: "Most enterprise AI programs are still in pilot — that's not a strategy, it's a $4M productivity gap compounding while competitors close the distance." Dollar anchor (specific), reframe (pilot is not strategy), personal stakes verb (compounding). All three in one line.

## Pattern Analysis

### Hook Patterns

1. **"Wild:" + stat anchor + competitive reframe — dominant pattern in short posts, 4/14 top posts:** Beliunas deploys this as a signature format across all four of his top posts in this cycle. The structure: a credibility cue ("Wild:") that signals even the author finds the data surprising, followed immediately by a specific verifiable number (40B banking events, $30B ARR, "100s of AI startups"), followed by a competitive reframe (what this means for the existing market structure). The pattern works because each element has a distinct job: the credibility cue reduces skepticism, the number anchors credibility, the reframe creates urgency. For Grey AI, direct equivalents: "Wild: The average enterprise AI pilot runs for 14 months before conversion to production — that's $2.3M in sunk cost and zero productivity gain." All three elements in one sentence.

2. **"X isn't Y, it's Z" category reframe — 5/14 posts:** Nadella's "AI isn't suggesting, it's executing"; Mollick's "this isn't working with AI, it's managing AI"; Schick's "AI isn't a market event, it's hard-power infrastructure"; Miller's "the hardest AI shift isn't efficiency, it's psychological"; Shumer's "this isn't a trend, it's a threshold." This structure directly matches the Grey AI April 6 winning formula. The reframe is mandatory in every hook. A hook that only contains a stat without a reframe is an announcement, not a provocation. A reframe without a stat is an opinion, not an argument. Together they are a benchmark post.

3. **Threshold announcement ("we crossed a line this week / FINALLY / already arrived") — 3/14 posts:** Shumer's "Something Big Is Happening," Mollick's "The Shape of the Thing," Shumer's "AI progress conversation has FINALLY hit mainstream." These posts earn high engagement because they create urgency without a deadline — the threshold was crossed, which means the reader is already behind if they haven't acted. For Grey AI: "The firms that closed their AI adoption gap in Q1 2026 aren't announcing it. Their competitors will find out when they lose the next deal."

4. **Competitive extinction verdict ("X died today" / "X just got competed away") — 2/14 posts:** Beliunas's "100s of AI startups died today" and "OpenAI just built a CFO in plain sight." This pattern is the highest-engagement hook for breaking product news because it forces binary reactions — agree (and name the companies) or dispute (and defend the sector). Both comment types are algorithmically equivalent. For Grey AI: "Most enterprise AI vendors are selling services that Claude Cowork just eliminated. That's not a risk to note for later — it's happening now."

5. **Personal conviction declaration ("This has been my thesis" / "I've been saying this for years") — 2/14 posts:** Schick's Industrial Intelligence post and Mollick's agentic era framework. The personal conviction structure earns trust because it signals skin in the game — the author committed to this position publicly before it was confirmed. For Grey AI: "We told clients in Q4 2024 that AI pilots without an operationalization plan were cost centers. The April 2026 data confirms it."

For Grey AI carousels, a dedicated "stat-stack slide" (4–5 concrete numbers, each one rewriting a received belief) earns high save rates from enterprise audiences who use these numbers in internal presentations.

- **Taxonomy/framework as reference content — 2/14 posts:** Mollick's Models-Apps-Harnesses framework (Agentic Era Guide) and Schick's Industrial Intelligence three-layer stack (energy + semiconductors + compute = sovereignty). Named frameworks earn saves because they function as reference material that readers return to. For Grey AI: naming the framework in the carousel title ("The 3-Layer AI Stack CFOs Are Missing," "The 4-Stage AI Operationalization Curve") earns saves from readers who won't use it today but want to file it for a future board presentation.

- **Personal authority + process reveal — 1/14 posts:** Miller's agentic workflow explainer. The working demonstration format earns the highest save-per-impression ratio because it signals immediate usability. For Grey AI, when publishing a process or methodology post, the hook should frame it as a working demonstration ("We ran this inside a $2B PE portfolio company — here's the exact workflow") rather than a guide or a prediction.

## Key Insights

These five patterns are confirmed as the highest-signal findings from the April 22 scrape. Downstream agents (SEO, draft-writer, post-ranker) should treat these as mandatory calibration inputs.

### Insight 1: The "function replacement at a dollar figure" hook is the new highest-engagement format in April 2026
Posts that translate AI capability into the specific dollar cost of the human function it replaces outperform abstract capability posts by a wide margin in this cycle. Beliunas's Claude Cowork analysis ("$20/month vs. $400K COO") and Shumer's workforce disruption essay both anchor the hook to a specific cost differential. For Grey AI: every hook that describes an AI capability should include the dollar cost of the human equivalent. This is a direct application of the April 6 formula (dollar anchor + reframe + personal stakes), now confirmed across multiple influencer profiles.

### Insight 2: Agentic AI posts that treat the transition as already complete outperform posts that treat it as upcoming
Six of fourteen top posts frame agentic AI in present or past tense ("agents now execute multi-step tasks," "we crossed the threshold last week," "the era of managing AIs has begun"). Zero high-engagement posts in this cycle use "will" or "coming soon" framing for agentic capabilities. Draft-writer agents should default to present-tense framing for any agentic AI topic: "Enterprise AI workflows are already executing without human prompting at [named company category]. Most firms haven't registered the gap yet."

### Insight 3: The "competitive extinction verdict" structure is the highest-engagement hook for product news
"100s of AI startups died today" (Beliunas on Claude Cowork) and "OpenAI just built a CFO in plain sight" (Beliunas on Hiro) both outperformed neutral product announcement framing. The extinction verdict forces binary responses — agreement and dispute — both of which are algorithmically equivalent. For Grey AI: any post about a new AI product or capability should frame it as a competitive verdict on an existing market, not a neutral announcement. ### Insight 4: Medium-length posts (150–300 words) with a binary split structure remain the highest-reliability format across all profiles

## Actionable Takeaways

1. **Translate every AI capability into the dollar cost of the human function it replaces, in the hook line.** "Claude Cowork costs $20/month vs. a $400K COO" is the highest-engagement hook structure in April 2026. For Grey AI: "Most PE portfolio reporting teams spend $450K/year in labor on a workflow one AI agent executes in 4 hours." Dollar anchor + function name + time comparison. All in one sentence.

2. **Use present-tense framing for all agentic AI content.** "Agents are already executing multi-step workflows inside enterprise finance stacks without human prompting" outperforms "AI agents will transform enterprise workflows." The transition is already underway — frame it as current reality, not incoming disruption. Readers who frame it as current reality feel behind, which creates urgency. Readers who frame it as incoming disruption feel safe, which kills engagement.

3. **Apply the competitive extinction verdict structure to any post about a new AI product or capability.** Instead of "Anthropic launched Claude Cowork," write "Anthropic just eliminated the market for a class of AI workflow automation vendors — and most of them haven't realized it yet." The verdict structure forces binary reactions. The "haven't realized it yet" closer positions the reader as having insider knowledge, which drives share behavior.

4. **Build every carousel around a binary split with the old belief stated confidently on slide 1.** The old belief must be stated as the reader currently holds it — not as a straw man. "Most enterprise AI programs are in pilot" is a true statement that the reader who holds it feels addressed by. The reframe on slide 2 then lands harder because it is dismantling a position the reader actually occupies. Slides 3–6 deliver the evidence. Slide 7 delivers the CTA. This structure survives at every scroll-exit point.

5. **Name the analytical framework in the carousel title.** "The 3-Layer AI Stack CFOs Are Missing" earns saves from enterprise professionals who will reference it in future presentations. A named framework converts a read into a save, and a save is the highest-intent engagement signal available on LinkedIn. Post-ranker agents should prioritize briefs that propose a named framework over briefs that do not.
