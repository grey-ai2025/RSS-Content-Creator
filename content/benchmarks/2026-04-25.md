Concrete dollar/multiple anchor + "X isn't Y, it's Z" reframe + personal-stakes verb — all three elements in the hook line, not buried in the body. ## Pattern Analysis

### Hook Patterns

**1. Single-numeric-anchor + immediate reframe in line 1 — dominant pattern, 12/15 top posts:**
Every high-engagement post uses exactly one primary number in the opening sentence or the first two sentences. No top performer stacks three or more numbers in line 1. The structure: name the number, make it surprising, attach the reframe in the same breath. Beliunas's "Google admitted" post and Nadella's product posts use zero numbers but compensate with named authority or behavioral metaphor. Conclusion: you can substitute a named authority for a number when the authority is extraordinary (Brin personally, Altman publicly), but you cannot have a naked opinion hook without either.

New pattern confirmed April 25: the **investment-scale anchor** (A$25 billion) functions identically to a product-metric anchor ($130% credit improvement) — both produce a single number that is legible to the audience's professional frame (enterprise procurement, policy) without requiring domain expertise to interpret.

**2. "X isn't Y, it's Z" category reframe — confirmed in 11/15 posts:**
Nadella's Cowork ("execution, not assistance"), Mollick's management superpower ("management, not technical skill"), Schick's hard-science claim ("not chatbots, it's biology/chemistry/physics"), Beliunas's one-person unicorn ("not hiring faster, building AI OS"), Miller's AI-prompts-you inversion, Shumer's "bigger than Covid" (not overblown, bigger than anything), Ng's context-not-model bottleneck, Altman's "agents to novel-insight systems," Beliunas's Google admission ("not benchmarks, real code"), Beliunas's Perplexity Computer ("not a chatbot, an orchestration system"), Nadella's "Looking Ahead" ("not models, systems"). The reframe is the engagement mechanism. A number without a reframe is an announcement. A reframe without a number is an opinion. The combination is the payload.

**3. Competitive-admission hook — 3/15 posts (highest trust efficiency per word):**
Beliunas's "Google admitted" (losing party's own words), Nadella's Cowork ("execution, not assistance" implicitly names the category being displaced), Beliunas's one-person unicorn (implicitly argues against the staffed-startup status quo). The competitive-admission structure where the losing party's own words do the work is the highest-trust format available on LinkedIn because it preempts the "this is just marketing" objection.

**4. Demonstration hook with verifiable proof — new confirmed pattern, April 25:**
Mollick's multi-model harbor benchmark. The task description is the hook. The gallery is the proof. The audience can verify the claim themselves. This pattern is more credible than statistics because the evidence is interactive. Grey AI application: when a workflow improvement claim is made, show the before/after output rather than quoting a percentage.

**5. Investment/policy-scale announcement — new confirmed pattern, April 25:**
Nadella's A$25B Australia post. The mechanism: national-scale commitment (not product announcement) + government partnership (policy legitimacy) + skills-training commitment (social license). Three comment populations arrive simultaneously: enterprise procurement, policy professionals, and skeptics. Grey AI application: frame AI deployments in terms of the organizational or sector-level financial commitment they represent, not just the tool cost.

## Actionable Takeaways

1. **Use the competitive-admission structure whenever a named party publicly acknowledges inferiority.** Quote their own words. "Sergey Brin personally formed a strike team because Claude outperforms Gemini" outperforms "Claude outperforms Gemini" — the named authority's admission is 10x more credible than an outside observer's verdict. Grey AI application: when a PE firm, law firm, or consulting company publishes a report acknowledging that AI has replaced a workflow they charge for, quote the report directly in the hook. "McKinsey's own study says AI agents can execute 70% of what a $500K engagement covers. The 30% they're keeping is the part they're not publishing."

2. **Translate every April–May 2026 agentic AI capability release into a dollar-function-replacement hook within 24 hours.** Copilot Agent Mode is now default in Word, Excel, and PowerPoint. Claude Cowork is live. Perplexity Computer runs 19 models in parallel. These are not features — they are direct replacements for specific human roles with specific salary lines. The correct hook format: "[Function] costs $[salary range]/year. [Product] does [specific workflow] for $[monthly cost]. The CFO math isn't complicated." Apply the Grey AI winning formula to each new product release.

3. **One numeric anchor in line 1, reframe in the same sentence — no exceptions for text posts.** The rule confirmed across 12/15 top posts: one primary number, attached to a reframe, in the opening sentence. Supporting stats belong in slide 2 or the body paragraph. Post-ranker agents: flag any brief with multiple numbers stacked in line 1 for revision before scoring. Exception: when a named authority is the anchor (Brin personally, Altman publicly), the authority substitutes for the number.

4. **Lead process and tool posts with a demonstration frame, not a guide frame.** Miller's "I built this" and Ng's "the bottleneck isn't the model" both outperform generic "here's how to use X" guides. The mechanism: a demonstration frame positions the author as someone who has already done the work (skin in the game), while a guide frame positions the author as a teacher (no stakes). Grey AI's specific advantage: "We ran this inside a CFO's office at a $2B PE-backed portfolio company — here's the exact workflow, the 95% time saving, and the one thing that almost broke it." That specificity is a claim no 10M-follower influencer can match.

5. **Drop explicit CTAs for competitive verdict and threshold-claim posts; use a single open classification question for process and framework posts.** 11/15 top performers have zero CTA. The forced (a)/(b)/(c) binary is absent from every top post this cycle. When a CTA is needed, the correct form is a classification question: "Which group has more people on your team right now?" or "Where does your organization draw the line between AI assistance and AI authority?" — not a preference poll. The classification question earns comments from both sides because readers are answering about their own situation, not expressing a preference about someone else's.
