## Pattern Analysis

### Hook Patterns

1. **Stat anchor + contrarian verdict ("Wild: [number] means [received wisdom is wrong]") — dominant pattern, 5/13 top posts:** The most reliable hook structure in this April 2026 scrape cycle. The formula: open with a specific, verifiable number that most readers would not have predicted; follow immediately with a statement that rewrites the category. Beliunas's "$30B ARR — Anthropic passed OpenAI" is the clearest example, but Nadella's Copilot comment counts, Shumer's 80M-view stat, and Ng's 3,000-developer registration all use the same logic. The number is the credibility anchor; the reframe is the engagement driver. Neither works alone. For Grey AI, direct equivalents include: "$X in cost saved per quarter," "Y% reduction in headcount," "Z enterprise deployments this year." The stat must be specific enough to be credible and surprising enough to justify the reframe.

2. **"X isn't Y, it's Z" category reframe — 4/13 posts:** Mollick's "using AI used to mean chatbots — now it means autonomous task execution"; Nadella's "AI isn't suggesting, it's executing"; Shumer's "AI progress conversation isn't niche anymore, it's mainstream"; Schick's "AI infrastructure isn't a market event, it's hard-power statecraft." This structure directly matches the Grey AI April 6 winning formula and remains the mandatory hook pattern for all Grey AI posts. A reframe without a stat anchor is weaker but still outperforms question hooks and narrative hooks at Grey AI's follower scale.

3. **News-jack with named competitive signal ("X just dropped / X just passed Y") — 3/13 posts:** Beliunas deploys this consistently: "Anthropic just dropped their own OpenClaw version," "Revolut just dropped PRAGMA," "Wild: Anthropic just passed OpenAI in revenue run rate." The named competitive signal (who just moved against whom) activates tribal engagement from both sides of the AI platform war. For Grey AI, the direct equivalent is connecting a named enterprise AI deployment (McKinsey, Goldman, HSBC, government mandate) to a business implication the reader hasn't priced in yet.

4. **Personal authority + social proof at scale ("I taught X to Y people for free") — 2/13 posts:** Miller and Mollick both front-load a completed action at scale before making any claim. The social proof number (20,000 people taught; 100,000+ newsletter subscribers) functions as a trust transfer — readers accept the subsequent claim because the scale signal implies the author has tested it on a large enough sample. For Grey AI at current follower scale, the equivalent is "We ran this inside [PE portfolio / enterprise client] and here's what broke."

5. **Vindication + insider signal ("FINALLY" / "the market hasn't priced this in yet") — 2/13 posts:** Shumer's "AI progress conversation has FINALLY hit mainstream" and Mollick's "agents have crossed a threshold, and the market hasn't priced it in." Both activate the reader's desire to be identified as early — being part of a group that knew before the crowd is a social identity signal that drives shares beyond content quality. For Grey AI, "most PE firms are still treating AI as a pilot when their competitors already closed the productivity gap" uses the same mechanism.

## Actionable Takeaways

1. **The hook must contain the stat, the reframe, and the personal-stakes verb in one sentence — not staged across the post.** The April 6 formula is confirmed by 8 of 13 top posts in this April 20 scrape. The post that opens "Wild: Anthropic just passed OpenAI in revenue run rate at $30B ARR — and it's not even selling software, it's writing it" delivers all three elements in 18 words. The post that says "AI is changing revenue models" in the hook and buries the stat in slide 3 loses the reader before the reframe lands. Grey AI equivalent: "Most CFOs think AI is an R&D cost — it's a $3M productivity gap compounding every quarter while competitors use it as infrastructure, not software."

2. **Use the stat-stack format for any breaking data point or competitive landscape update.** Beliunas's "$30B ARR / 2x five major public companies combined / Claude Code at $2.5B / 4% of GitHub commits" pattern is directly replicable: take a single credible institutional data point and stack 3–5 implications underneath it, each one more concrete than the last. For Grey AI: when a McKinsey report, government AI mandate, or enterprise deployment number drops, build the carousel around 4–5 extractions from that single source, each one expressed as a concrete dollar or percentage, not a tendency.

3. **Frame agentic AI as already arrived, not coming.** Every high-engagement post in the agentic AI theme uses past-tense or present-continuous framing ("agents have crossed a threshold," "AI is now executing multi-step tasks," "you can now assign tasks and they complete them"). Posts that frame agents as "the future of work" underperform posts that frame them as "already running inside enterprises you haven't heard about yet." Grey AI translation: "The firms that automated their CFO reporting stack in Q1 2026 are running with a structural productivity advantage — your competitors probably know this and you don't."

4. **Build every carousel around a named binary split with the old belief on slide 1.** The slide-1 hook is not an introduction — it is a provocation. The old belief must be stated confidently enough that a reader who holds it feels addressed: "Most enterprise AI implementations are still in pilot. That's not a strategy, it's a liability." The reframe lands on slide 2. Slides 3–6 deliver the evidence. Slide 7 delivers the CTA. This structure is readable at every point of scroll-exit: a reader who sees only slides 1–2 still receives the key argument.

5. **Target niche professional segments explicitly when the content earns it.** Miller's PE-specific Vertical AI post and Schick's NATO/defense-specific infrastructure post both demonstrate that naming a specific high-value audience segment (private equity, defense, enterprise operations) earns disproportionate engagement from that segment because those professionals almost never see content targeted at their exact context. Grey AI has natural credibility with PE, VC, and enterprise operations audiences. Using segment labels explicitly in the hook ("Most PE operating partners are running AI pilots that never graduate to infrastructure") earns comment density from high-LTV readers who do not engage with generic AI-for-business content.
