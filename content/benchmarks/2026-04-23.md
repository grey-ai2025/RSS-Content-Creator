# LinkedIn Influencer Benchmark — 2026-04-23

## Internal Benchmark — Grey AI Top Performers

**April 22 Audit Revisions (active from 2026-04-23 forward):**

The April 22 audit revised several positions from the April 13 audit. The following are the current live rules for Grey AI content, confirmed by cross-influencer data gathered in this cycle:

- **Format-as-default is dropped.** Carousel is no longer mandatory. Single-image is now a first-class format option. Format selection must be driven by the content type and the engagement goal, not by default. (Influencer data: top posts in this cycle are 79% text-only. At Grey AI's scale, carousel remains higher-leverage than text; but single-image with a strong visual anchoring a numeric claim is now in play.)
- **(a)/(b)/(c) Forced Binary CTA is deprecated.** Replace with open question CTAs that invite position-taking ("What's your read on this?" / "Which way are you leaning?") or single direct asks. Influencer data: the CTAs pulling actual comments in this cycle are open-ended questions that create space for disagreement, not forced-choice menus.
- **Hashtags capped at 2-3.** No influencer in the top-15 posts this cycle uses more than 3 hashtags. Most use 0-2. Hashtag inflation signals low-confidence content and suppresses feed distribution.
- **Hook simplicity: one numeric anchor in line 1 is now a hard gate.** Multi-stat openers (3+ numbers in the opening line) underperform posts with a single anchor number. The anchor must be specific, surprising, and tied to a stakes claim. This is directly confirmed by influencer data below.

**April 6 Winning Formula (still active, refined):**
Concrete dollar/multiple anchor + "X isn't Y, it's Z" reframe + personal-stakes verb — all three elements in the hook line itself. The April 22 audit adds: the numeric anchor must stand alone in line 1. Burying it after a preamble ("In a world where AI is changing everything, a $450K budget gap is emerging...") kills the mechanism. The number must be the first or second word group.

## Pattern Analysis

### Hook Patterns

**1. Single-numeric-anchor + reframe in line 1 — confirmed dominant pattern, 10/14 top posts:**
Every post in the top tier that uses a number uses exactly one primary numeric anchor in the opening line or within the first two sentences. Not one post with multiple numbers jammed into line 1 appeared in the top performers. The single-anchor pattern is: name the number, make it surprising, tie it to a reframe immediately. Examples: "four days" (Mollick MBAs), "70% variance reduction" (Mollick agentic study), "one employee" (Beliunas unicorn), "40 billion banking events / +130%" (Beliunas PRAGMA — the training data figure is the lead anchor; the outcome metrics stack follows). This directly confirms the April 22 audit position: one numeric anchor in line 1 is a hard gate. Multi-stat openers that front-load three or more numbers before the reframe do not appear in the top-performing posts of this cycle.

**2. "X isn't Y, it's Z" category reframe — 8/14 posts:**
Nadella's "execution, not assistance" (Copilot Cowork), Mollick's "management, not technical skill" (management superpower), Schick's "war, not market event" (AI sovereignty), Beliunas's "control panel for AI workers, not a chatbot upgrade" (Google Gemini leak), Miller's "AI prompts you, not the reverse" (proactive AI), Shumer's "most people are using this wrong" (Codex/Claude Code), Ng's "bottleneck is PM, not coding" (software engineering), Altman's "2025 was agents; 2026 is novel insight systems." This is the structural core of the April 6 winning formula. The reframe is not optional decoration — it is the engagement mechanism. A hook with a number but no reframe is an announcement. A hook with a reframe but no number is an opinion. Both underperform the combination.

**3. Insider correction or threshold declaration — 5/14 posts:**
Shumer's "most people are doing this wrong," Mollick's "management is the AI superpower no one talks about," Nadella's "I tried it this week — here's what surprised me," Beliunas's "Google just leaked," Miller's "more AI users need to hear this." These hooks all position the post author as having crossed a knowledge threshold the reader has not yet reached. The engagement mechanism is the knowledge gap: readers who recognize the gap comment to learn more, readers who dispute the gap comment to defend their existing understanding. Both are high-engagement comment types.

**4. Competitive extinction verdict — 2/14 posts:**
Beliunas's "Google just leaked its answer to Claude Cowork" and the carry-forward from April 22 ("100s of AI startups died today"). This hook type is specifically activated by product news. Not appropriate for evergreen content. Most reliable for breaking news within 24 hours of a product announcement or competitive move.

**5. Personal conviction + named framework — 2/14 posts:**
Schick's "Industrial Intelligence" / "Full Stack Control" and Ng's "Product Management Bottleneck." Named frameworks earn saves. Saves are the highest-intent engagement signal. Draft-writer agents: include a named framework in the carousel title or slide 1 for any post that synthesizes a structural argument.

## Actionable Takeaways

1. **One numeric anchor in line 1 — hard gate, no exceptions.** Every hook gets one number. That number must be specific, surprising, and tied to a reframe in the same sentence or the one immediately following. Supporting stats go in slide 2 or the body. "40 billion banking events — that's what Revolut trained PRAGMA on. The result: +130% credit scoring and a competitive moat that no European bank can replicate in under five years." One anchor number (40B), one reframe (competitive moat), one stakes verb (replicate). This is the confirmed formula.

2. **Remove the CTA for strong-hook posts; replace with an open single question for medium-hook posts.** No more (a)/(b)/(c) forced binary. If the hook is a genuine provocation (competitive verdict, threshold claim, category reframe), no CTA is needed — the provocation does the work. If the post is a process reveal or framework post, close with one open question: "Where does this break down for your team?" or "What would you hand off first?" Never a menu.

3. **Cap hashtags at 2, target 0-1 for provocative posts.** Provocative threshold claims and competitive verdicts distribute via engagement, not hashtags. Hashtags signal low-confidence content to the algorithm when appended to strong posts. Use 2 hashtags for topic-specific posts where search discoverability matters (agentic AI, enterprise AI, FinTech AI). Use 0 for threshold claims and competitive verdicts.

4. **Lead with a personal demonstration frame for any tool or workflow post.** "We built this inside a live client engagement" outperforms "Here's how to use X" and "X will transform Y." The personal demonstration is a trust signal that converts skeptics. Grey AI's best hook advantage is client specificity — name the function (PE portfolio ops team, legal ops director, CFO office) and the outcome (4-hour reporting workflow, $450K labor cost eliminated). This is a claim no influencer with 10M followers can match.

5. **Treat single-image as a first-class format for single-claim posts.** When the argument is one number + one reframe + one visual, a single-image post with a 150-300 word caption is the correct format. Reserve carousel for multi-slide arguments that require the binary-split structure (old belief on slide 1, reframe on slide 2, evidence on slides 3-6, CTA on slide 7). The format should serve the argument, not the other way around.
