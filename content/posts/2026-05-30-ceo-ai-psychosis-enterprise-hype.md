# AI Psychosis — The Demo-Production Gap Behind 115,430 Layoffs

## Caption

Box CEO Aaron Levie named it "AI psychosis" — and named himself as a likely carrier.

"CEOs are uniquely prone because they're sufficiently distant from the last mile of work that still has to happen."

ClickUp's CEO ran the experiment. Deployed 3,000 internal AI agents. Cut 22% of staff simultaneously.

Harvard Business Review found the result: the bottleneck didn't disappear. It moved to the executives approving everything the agents produce.

That is the mechanism behind 115,430 tech layoffs in the first five months of 2026. UC Berkeley's meta-analysis found no robust relationship between AI adoption and aggregate productivity gain. The demo showed one environment. The layoffs were made for another.

The last-mile work didn't go away. The people who knew how to do it did.

Box CEO Aaron Levie says CEOs are "sufficiently distant from the last mile of work" — the last mile is where the demo stops and production starts. Have you seen a version of this gap in your own organization? What was the distance?

#AgenticAI #FutureOfWork

---

## Strategy Brief

- Audience: Founders and team leads at 10-50 person companies who have made or are about to make workforce decisions based on AI capability demos
- Reframe: The demo-production gap is a structural CEO information failure, not an individual character flaw — and UC Berkeley's data shows the gap is large enough to have produced nearly a year's worth of tech layoffs in five months
- Consequence: Companies that cut people to fund agents are discovering the bottleneck moved upward (Harvard), not disappeared — and the people who could handle the last mile are gone
- Core claim: AI psychosis isn't a CEO failing — it's what happens when the information system that feeds leadership decisions is the demo environment, not the production environment
