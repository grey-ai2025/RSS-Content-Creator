# AI Agent Failure in Production — The Missing Workflow Redesign Step

## Caption

480 workplace tasks. Three major AI labs. One result: every agent failed most of what it was assigned.

That's not a model problem. The agents that worked in demos worked fine. What failed was the workflow they were dropped into — unchanged, designed for humans, full of the same handoffs and ambiguity that slowed humans down.

Mercor ran agents from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind against real production tasks — not benchmarks, not demos. Same outcome across all three. The Anthropic capability research that most enterprise teams cite as justification for agent deployment is based on task-level performance assessments, not actual workplace deployments. Two different definitions of "capable."

Most teams deploying AI agents did one thing: plugged a faster tool into a slow process.

The slow process wins. Every time.

The fix isn't a better model. It's tearing up the workflow and rebuilding it around how agents actually operate — autonomous decision points, explicit handoffs, task boundaries that match agent parameters, not human cognition.

No vendor sells that step. No benchmark measures it. Every company buying AI agents is buying Step 1 and Step 3. Step 2 is still sitting on the whiteboard.

Has your team formally redesigned any workflow around your AI agents — or deployed them into processes that were originally built for human workers?
