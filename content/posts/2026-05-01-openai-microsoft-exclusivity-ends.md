# OpenAI-Microsoft Exclusivity Ends — Cloud Vendor Decisions Just Changed

## Caption

'OpenAI equals Azure' was the enterprise AI distribution thesis for three years. As of this week it is no longer a constraint — it is a choice.

OpenAI models are already live on Amazon Bedrock and Google Cloud. The Azure-only requirement is gone.

Microsoft keeps a revenue share and some preferred partner status. That is not exclusivity. The reason most enterprise teams cited for choosing Azure for AI workloads just stopped being a reason.

One detail worth tracking: OpenAI missed its Q1 revenue targets that same quarter. The distribution expansion was not a win from strength — it was a requirement for the IPO story.

The moat your vendor decision was built on is gone.

Did your team's cloud vendor decision factor in the OpenAI-Azure relationship — and does this week's change affect that calculation?

#EnterpriseAI #CloudComputing

## Strategy Brief

- Audience: Founders and CTOs at 10-50 person companies who factored the OpenAI-Azure exclusivity into cloud vendor decisions — either by choosing Azure to secure OpenAI access, or by treating OpenAI as unavailable on AWS or GCP
- Reframe: The exclusivity that made Azure the only enterprise channel for running OpenAI in production is already gone. Any cloud vendor decision made on that assumption is running on an outdated premise.
- Consequence: Teams that chose Azure for OpenAI access now have a different infrastructure question: does Azure earn its place on its own merits? Teams on AWS or GCP that avoided OpenAI now have native access without switching costs.
- Core claim: The "Microsoft owns OpenAI's distribution" thesis is not dying — it is already dead. Cloud vendor decisions made on that assumption need to be re-examined against the actual options that now exist.
