# AI Job Reshape — The Entry-Level Pipeline Collapse

## Caption

55% of US jobs get reshaped in 2 years. Only 12% actually get cut.

The layoff headlines are tracking the wrong number.

The real story isn't substitution. It's that the on-ramp into senior knowledge work is being dismantled in plain sight.

Software engineering headcount has grown every year since 2022 — even with AI writing half the code. Because demand for software is unbounded, AI amplified the role instead of erasing it.

But insurance sales, IT support, junior analyst seats — the routine work that built senior careers — is exactly what AI absorbs first. The pyramid keeps its top. It loses its base.

In five years, the companies that kept hiring juniors and redesigned the work around AI supervision will have a senior bench. The ones that "got efficient" in 2026 will be paying premium rates for senior talent the market no longer produces.

What's your team's plan for hiring juniors when AI does 60% of what they used to do — replace the seats, or redesign the role?

#FutureOfHiring #AIWorkforce #TalentStrategy

---

## Strategy Brief

- **Audience:** Founders, COOs, and team leads at 10–200 person companies making 2026 hiring and headcount decisions under AI cost pressure
- **Reframe:** The dominant "AI is taking jobs" narrative tracks the 10-15% substitution number. The structural risk is the 88% that need redesign — and specifically the entry-level seats whose absence breaks the senior talent pipeline
- **Consequence:** Companies that pre-emptively cut juniors in 2026 will be unable to staff senior roles in 2031, because the on-ramp that produced senior knowledge workers — supervised execution of routine work — no longer exists
- **Core claim:** The Amplified job segment (software engineering as the proof) shows that AI grows headcount when demand is unbounded. Most companies have at least one Amplified function and one entry-level on-ramp worth defending — they're cutting both anyway
