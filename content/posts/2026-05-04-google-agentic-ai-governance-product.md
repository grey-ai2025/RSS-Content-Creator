# Google Agentic AI Governance — Agent Pilots Stall Without Infrastructure

## Caption

86-89% of AI agent pilots never reach production. The failure isn't the model — it's the $0 governance infrastructure that should have been the deployment prerequisite.

OutSystems asked 1,879 IT leaders about agentic AI. 97% said they're exploring it — but only 36% have centralized governance, and only 12% have a platform to manage AI sprawl. Gartner called it: 40%+ of agentic projects cancelled by 2027, governance gaps as the named cause.

Google just productized the answer at Cloud Next. Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform assigns each agent a cryptographic identity so you can trace what it touched. A dedicated Agent Gateway governs every data interaction before it happens. Not compliance — the infrastructure that determines whether an agent gets deployed at all.

Governance isn't the follow-on. It's the prerequisite.

The pilots that reach production share three things: a traceable identity, governed data access, and an audit trail. Most skip all three.

Name the person on your team who owns those three questions for every agent currently running.

## Strategy Brief

- Audience: Founders and ops leads at 10-50 person companies who have deployed or are evaluating AI agents and assumed governance was a "phase 2" task after deployment was working
- Reframe: Google just productized governance as the deployment prerequisite — not the follow-on. The 86-89% pilot stall rate is a governance gap story, not a model story
- Consequence: Any team that can't answer three questions about every deployed agent (what it did, on whose authority, and whether the audit trail exists) is operating inside the 86% that never reaches production
- Core claim: The reason agent pilots stall isn't the model — it's the absence of the control infrastructure Google just made standard
