# Cognition Devin — $25B Valuation as Market Verdict on AI Consolidation Prediction

## Caption

Cognition just hit a $25B valuation.
Eight months ago it was worth $10.2B.

That isn't a funding story. It's the market pricing the answer to a prediction that turned out to be wrong.

The prediction: Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google would bundle autonomous coding agents into their existing platforms and absorb this market. Claude Code, Codex, Jules — done.

Cognition's $492M in annualized revenue says otherwise. Goldman Sachs, NASA, Mercedes-Benz, and Santander are paying for Devin while already having access to those bundled alternatives. They're not choosing it because it's the only option. They're choosing it because an independent agent not tied to one model vendor means accountability, flexibility, and the ability to route to the best capability — rather than whatever the lab decided to ship this quarter.

Google absorbed Windsurf's founding team. Cognition took Windsurf's product and customers. That's the pattern: model vendors absorb the talent, independent companies take the enterprise revenue.

Most infrastructure decisions get locked in early and rarely get re-examined. Goldman Sachs and NASA just made theirs.

Most founders I talk to assume their AI coding stack is a provisional choice they'll revisit when things stabilize. I think the window for treating autonomous coding infrastructure as provisional just closed. Where do you land?

- Consequence: The window for treating autonomous coding infrastructure as experimental or provisional is closing. Goldman Sachs and NASA are in production, not pilot. Founders who haven't deliberately evaluated the independent-vs-bundled question are on a default they may not have chosen on purpose.
- Core claim: The $25B valuation is the market betting that enterprises will pay a premium for autonomous coding infrastructure not tied to a single AI vendor — and Goldman Sachs and NASA are already proving that bet right.
