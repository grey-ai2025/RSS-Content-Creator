# HBS AI Job Bifurcation — Deskilling Precedes Displacement

## Caption

Harvard tracked nearly every US job posting from 2019 through early 2025. Routine-task roles fell 13%. Analytical, creative, and technical roles grew 20%.

The number everyone misses: a 7% drop in skill requirements inside the roles that survived.

The deskilling signal arrives before the layoff does. Finance and technology — the two sectors most people assume are safe — led the reductions.

Investment managers and financial analysts kept their roles. The structured, repetitive work inside those same sectors shrank. The difference: one category combines AI leverage with judgment. The other is judgment the AI already absorbed.

The hiring decision you make this quarter is a bet on which side of that 13%/20% split you're building into.

Name the role on your current team that most clearly combines AI leverage with judgment or hands-on expertise — the profile Harvard flagged as the 20% growth category.

## Strategy Brief

- Audience: Founders and hiring managers at 10-50 person companies making 2026 headcount decisions — people allocating budget across roles and assuming the categories they have always hired for still carry the same strategic value
- Reframe: The deskilling finding (7% drop in skill diversity inside routine roles) is the early-warning signal that precedes the headcount cut. Finance and technology — the sectors most people assume are safe — led the reductions. The routine knowledge roles surviving today are being simplified before they are eliminated, not preserved.
- Consequence: The hiring decision a founder makes this quarter is not about replacing a person with AI. It is about whether the role they are hiring for is in the 13% shrinking category or the 20% growing one. Same budget. Different return trajectory.
- Core claim: AI is bifurcating knowledge work, not eliminating it wholesale — and the deskilling of surviving routine roles is the warning signal that arrives before the layoffs do.
