# OpenRouter — The Single-Vendor Strategy Signal

## Caption

Google's growth fund led a $113M investment in a platform that routes traffic to OpenAI, Anthropic, and xAI. CapitalG doesn't make defensive investments. It makes platform bets.

OpenRouter's valuation doubled to $1.3B in 12 months. 8 million builders are routing AI tasks across 400+ models — not because they're philosophically opposed to vendor loyalty, but because they moved from pilot to production and discovered their single-vendor cost model was wrong.

Token volumes 5x between pilot and production. The gap between premium-model-for-everything and task-appropriate routing becomes a real line item fast.

CapitalG already ran this math. Most company AI stacks haven't.

Google just backed a router that sends traffic to its own competitors. What does that tell you about whether your AI vendor strategy is a deliberate architecture choice — or a default that survived from the pilot stage?

#AIInfrastructure #AgenticAI

---

## Strategy Brief

- Audience: Founders and technical operators at 10-50 person companies standardized on a single AI vendor (Claude for everything, or GPT-4 for everything), whose cost models and integration architecture assume a single-vendor approach
- Reframe: OpenRouter's 5x token growth is not a routing story — it's the market confirming that builders who moved from pilot to production have already resolved the vendor lock-in question toward interchangeability. Single-vendor "simplicity" is increasingly the strategy of builders who haven't gotten to production scale yet.
- Consequence: Token volumes 5x between pilot and production. The cost gap between paying premium-model pricing for every task vs. routing routine tasks to cheaper, faster models becomes a real line item. Companies that accept model interchangeability early run better cost curves as usage scales.
- Core claim: CapitalG's $113M bet on OpenRouter isn't a defensive investment — it's a platform bet that no single AI model wins the enterprise, and the companies that accepted model interchangeability run better cost curves than those still betting on one vendor.
