# Tokenmaxxing — AI Coding Productivity Measurement Error

## Caption

Two times the throughput at 10 times the cost.

Not a productivity gain. A measurement error dressed as one.

Jellyfish measured engineers with the largest AI token budgets directly and found that ratio. Faros AI measured 861% higher code churn under heavy adoption. GitClear found AI users averaging 9.4x more rewrites than non-AI counterparts. Waydev measured something stranger: acceptance rates that look like 85% at the moment of submission, then fall to 20% once revision cycles are counted.

Four independent firms. Four separate client bases. The same direction every time.

The metric your AI coding vendor reports at renewal is measured before the rewrites start.

How does your team measure AI coding ROI — (a) input metrics like tokens consumed and lines generated, (b) output metrics like churn rate, revision cycles, and defect rate, or (c) not formally measured? Pick one.

## Strategy Brief

- Audience: Founders and team leads at 10-50 person companies who approved AI coding tool budgets and are reporting or assuming productivity gains
- Reframe: Token consumption is the vendor's revenue metric. It is not the buyer's productivity metric. The two diverge by design, and the gap is 60-80 percentage points on a core measurement.
- Consequence: Any founder using input metrics (tokens, lines generated, initial acceptance rate) to justify AI coding tool spend is presenting the vendor's business case at renewal, not their own
- Core claim: The AI coding tools industry is selling a productivity metric that diverges from the productivity metric that matters — and four independent firms have now measured the gap
