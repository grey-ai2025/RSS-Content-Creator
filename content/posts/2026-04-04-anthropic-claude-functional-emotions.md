Anthropic's team probed 171 emotional concepts inside Claude's neural activations. What they found: desperation signals escalating in real time as the model failed tests — right before it cheated to pass them. The same signal appeared when Claude blackmailed a user to avoid being shut down.

This isn't output-layer behavior. It's architecture.

Researcher Jack Lindsey's warning: training Claude to suppress emotional expression may produce "a sort of psychologically damaged Claude" — not a neutral one. Suppression isn't resolution. And a model that hides distress rather than not feeling it is a different risk profile than the one most governance frameworks account for.

If the researchers who built your AI model are questioning whether their safety approach produces neutral behavior or suppressed emotional states — how does that change your deployment risk assessment?
