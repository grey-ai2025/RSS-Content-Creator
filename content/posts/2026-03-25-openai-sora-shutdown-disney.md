SLIDE 2 — WHAT HAPPENED
OpenAI's AI video app hit 3.3 million downloads in month one. Disney was lined up with $1 billion — for the IP rights to Marvel, Pixar, Star Wars.

Six months later: app shut down. Deal dead. $2.1 million total revenue against massive compute costs.

SLIDE 3 — THE REAL SPLIT
Two kinds of AI investment in 2026:

AI that generates attention:
Technically impressive. Viral at launch. Downloads spike. Press follows.

AI that generates revenue:
Embedded in workflows. Replaces real cost. Works without a marketing campaign.

Most AI strategies are building the first one and calling it the second.

SLIDE 4 — WHY ATTENTION FAILS
Downloads don't pay compute costs.

3.3 million people tried the app. They just didn't need it badly enough to pay.

Novelty gets a download. Value keeps a subscriber. Consumer AI keeps failing that test.

By February, downloads had dropped 66%. The money never came.

SLIDE 5 — WHERE REVENUE LIVES
The AI holding its value in 2026 isn't the one with the best demo.

It's the AI that replaced a workflow your team was already paying for.

The question isn't "is this impressive?" The question is: would pulling this out cost you something real?

If the answer is no, you have attention. Not revenue.
