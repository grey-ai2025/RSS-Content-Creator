# PayPal CEO Admits AI Pilot Mode Failed — $1.5B Price Tag on the Delay

## Caption

Running AI pilots is not an AI strategy.

PayPal's CEO said so on his Q1 earnings call — after years of pilots that never changed how the business works. The result: 4,500 planned cuts, $1.5 billion in projected savings, a new C-suite team built to redesign core processes from scratch.

His words: "This is not about adopting AI as a technology, where we have done many pilots. It's really about understanding how can we redesign the key processes."

Spotify's top developers stopped writing code six months ago. PayPal plans to get there in two to three years.

$1.5 billion is what the pilot phase cost.

What is the one core process AI would fundamentally redesign in your business — and what is stopping that commitment?

#EnterpriseAI #AIStrategy

---

## Strategy Brief

- Audience: Founders and operators at 10-50 person companies currently running AI pilots but not yet redesigning core workflows
- Reframe: Running pilots is not deliberation — it is postponement. PayPal's earnings call admission quantifies the cost of that distinction at $1.5 billion.
- Consequence: The "redesign the key processes" decision has a financial cost whether you make it proactively or are forced into it. PayPal chose the second path.
- Core claim: AI pilots are a postponement, not a strategy. PayPal just put the price tag on that distinction at $1.5 billion.
