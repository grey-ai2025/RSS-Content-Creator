SLIDE 2 — SETUP
Old restructuring: cut people to survive a downturn.
New restructuring: cut people to fund the AI that makes those people unnecessary.
Both show up in the same earnings call. One goes away when conditions improve. One doesn't.

SLIDE 3 — THE PATTERN
Oracle: 20,000–30,000 jobs cut to fund AI data centers.
Block: 4,000+ roles cited AI efficiency as the reason.
Now a platform with one of the largest consumer footprints in the world is reportedly evaluating cuts up to 20%.
→ This is no longer a warning sign. It's a pattern.

SLIDE 4 — THE LOGIC SHIFT
The framing is new. These aren't called restructurings.
They're called capital reallocation.
Labor budget → compute budget.
That language matters. It says: this trade is repeatable.

SLIDE 5 — THE TWO TYPES OF LEADER
𝐓𝐲𝐩𝐞 1: Watching from the outside, treating this as someone else's workforce story.
𝐓𝐲𝐩𝐞 2: Already asking — "What's our framework for this decision when it lands on our desk?"
The difference isn't company size. It's whether your org has thought through the trade before it's forced on you.

SLIDE 6 — THE GOVERNANCE GAP
Most organizations have a framework for hiring.
Almost none have a framework for deciding when AI makes a role redundant.
That gap is now a governance risk — not just an HR question.
→ Your board will ask. Do you have an answer?

SLIDE 7 — THE REAL QUESTION
This isn't: should we do what the platforms are doing?
It's: when the pressure arrives, will you be deciding — or just reacting?
The companies building frameworks now are the ones who'll make the call on their terms.
