# AI Boss Workforce Poll — Tool Use vs. Authority Acceptance

## Caption

𝟖𝟓% 𝐨𝐟 𝐩𝐞𝐨𝐩𝐥𝐞 𝐝𝐨𝐧'𝐭 𝐰𝐚𝐧𝐭 𝐚𝐧 𝐀𝐈 𝐛𝐨𝐬𝐬.
𝐂𝐨𝐦𝐩𝐚𝐧𝐢𝐞𝐬 𝐚𝐫𝐞 𝐛𝐮𝐢𝐥𝐝𝐢𝐧𝐠 𝐭𝐡𝐞𝐦 𝐚𝐧𝐲𝐰𝐚𝐲.

A Quinnipiac poll of 1,397 U.S. adults found only 1 in 7 would accept AI as their direct supervisor. Yet those same people use AI tools every day.

People trust AI as a tool. They don't trust it as authority.

"Help me do my job" — fine.
"Tell me how to do my job" — not fine.

Most agentic AI strategies cross that line. They don't just assist — they assign, evaluate, and decide.

That's not automation. That's management.

Even Gen Z — the group expected to embrace AI the most — shows the highest skepticism about its impact at work. The "digital native = AI native" assumption doesn't hold.

Companies focus on capability. Employees react to control.

Capability without trust stalls.

Before your next agentic rollout, ask your team one question: where do they draw the line between AI assistance and AI authority?

#FutureOfWork #AIAgents #AgenticAI #AILiteracy #PracticalAI #GreyAI

## Strategy Brief

- Audience: Business leaders and HR executives deploying agentic AI; team managers building AI-augmented workflows; organizational design professionals responsible for change management
- Reframe: Workers will enthusiastically use AI tools and simultaneously refuse to grant AI supervisory authority — these are separate psychological variables, not a continuum. Most enterprise AI roadmaps treat them as the same line.
- Consequence: Agentic AI deployments that assign tasks, schedule work, and evaluate outputs will encounter structured resistance from 85% of workers regardless of their tool adoption level. ROI projections built on assumed workforce acceptance are miscalibrated.
- Core claim: There is a growing and underappreciated gap between workers' willingness to use AI tools and their willingness to grant AI authority over their work — and enterprises are building agentic systems that cross that line without knowing the number.
