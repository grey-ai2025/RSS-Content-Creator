# OpenAI and Anthropic Build Competing PE Distribution Networks

## Caption

Blackstone just became an AI distribution channel. So did TPG, Brookfield, Advent, and Bain Capital.

Both OpenAI and Anthropic built joint ventures with major PE firms — simultaneously, with zero investor overlap between them. OpenAI raised $4B through its venture. Anthropic raised $1.5B through Blackstone, Goldman Sachs, and Hellman & Friedman.

The implementation model is not a SaaS license. Both ventures send "forward-deployed engineers" embedded directly in portfolio company workflows — on-site, not a support ticket.

Blackstone alone owns 250+ portfolio companies. The total consortium spans several thousand mid-market businesses.

That is not a sales motion. That is ownership-based distribution.

The enterprise AI market just split into two tiers: companies inside a PE consortium that get embedded implementation, and everyone else who gets the API docs.

If your company is PE-backed, does your investor's consortium position change how you think about your AI stack — or how you think about your cap table?

## Strategy Brief

- Audience: Founders and operators at PE-backed companies, plus enterprise buyers competing with PE-backed peers
- Reframe: AI vendor selection is not a procurement decision — it is a capital structure outcome
- Consequence: Two ownership-defined tiers now exist. Your tier may have been set by your investor, not your IT team
- Core claim: PE ownership networks are the most consequential AI distribution layer ever built — and whether your company is inside one determines what level of AI support you can access
