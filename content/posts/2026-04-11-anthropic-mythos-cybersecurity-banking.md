# Fed + Treasury Bank CEO Meeting — AI Systemic Risk Escalation

## Caption

The Fed Chair and Treasury Secretary just called America's largest bank CEOs into a room to discuss one AI model.

That is a systemic risk response. Not a technology briefing.

The model found CVE-2026-4747, a 17-year-old server exploit, without being trained to find it. $100M in access credits committed. Zero public availability. A company at $30B revenue withheld a product from public release. That is the capability signal.

Most CROs have a framework for AI operational risk. Almost none have a framework for this.

Where does AI risk governance sit at your institution: (a) technology committee, (b) enterprise risk committee, or (c) distributed — no single owner?

## Strategy Brief

- Audience: Bank CROs, enterprise risk officers, financial services technology leaders, board-level governance leads in regulated industries
- Reframe: AI risk in banking has been a technology committee conversation. A single model's demonstrated capability just moved it to a different room — and most institutions are not organized for where it landed.
- Consequence: Every financial institution whose AI governance sits at the technology committee level now has a federal-level precedent showing that regulators treat frontier capability as systemic infrastructure risk, not operational risk.
- Core claim: The Fed and Treasury meeting is not a policy signal — it is a capability threshold signal. AI has crossed from operational concern into institutional threat territory, and most governance frameworks were not designed to receive that kind of escalation.
