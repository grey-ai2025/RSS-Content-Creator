## Visual Direction Considered

**Option A: "The Glass Wall" — SELECTED**
Scene: A corporate boardroom, viewed from slightly behind and to the side of the executive table. A large presentation screen at the far end displays a clean, flawless AI demo — smooth charts, green status indicators, confident outputs. The boardroom glass wall is on the viewer's right. Through it, clearly visible but slightly out of focus: an open-plan production floor where roughly half the desks are empty — chairs pushed in tight, personal items cleared. At one surviving workstation, a single figure (seen from behind, anonymous) sits beneath a hanging fluorescent that flickers slightly, working through a tall stack of printed documents — agent outputs queued for executive sign-off. The executives at the table are not visible; only their empty chairs and the backs of their heads are implied, all attention on the screen. The camera sits at desk level, looking toward the screen, so both the demo and the floor beyond the glass are in the same frame.

Lighting: The demo screen is the primary light source — cool, clinical white-blue light washing the near side of the room. Through the glass, the production floor is lit by warm, slightly failing fluorescent overhead light — a different color temperature entirely. The lone worker is the only warm element in the frame.

Color treatment: The room is fully desaturated — dark charcoal walls, grey carpet, near-black furniture. The demo screen glows in cool white-blue. The single cyan accent lands on the flickering fluorescent tube above the lone worker — that one light, that one person, is the last mile the post is about.

Atmosphere: The viewer feels the cognitive dissonance before reading a word — a beautiful, confident screen on one side of the glass, a depleted floor on the other. The irony is architectural.

Text on image: 5 words — "The demo. Then the floor." — bottom-center, white, DM Sans Bold.

**Option B: "The War Room Map"**
A military-style operations room. Commanders in suits (not uniforms — corporate reframe) lean over a backlit tactical map covered in precise AI-generated overlays: clean projections, probability scores, optimal routing lines. Through a small grimy window at the back of the room: a chaotic, fog-filled landscape nothing like the clean map. The map is the demo. The landscape is production.

Why not selected: The military framing pulls the viewer away from the corporate AI context and requires more interpretive work. The Glass Wall is more immediate — it lives in the same world as the reader (a corporate office) and the two environments are visible simultaneously without metaphor translation.

**Option C: "The Signed Order"**
A close-up of a desk. Center frame: a document with "WORKFORCE REDUCTION — APPROVED" and a signature line freshly signed. Behind the document, slightly out of focus: a laptop showing a clean AI capability report with green checkmarks and impressive throughput numbers. In the extreme background: a coat abandoned on a chair — the only evidence of someone who was here and is now gone.

Why not selected: This is the aftermath — the decision already made and the consequences already felt. The Glass Wall shows the moment of the decision in progress, which has more tension. The post's argument is about the structural gap at the time of decision, not the reckoning after. The Glass Wall is the image of the decision being made in the wrong environment.
