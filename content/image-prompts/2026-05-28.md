## Visual Direction Considered

**Option A — The Neutral Switchboard (SELECTED)**
A 1960s–1970s telephone exchange room. Dense rows of patch cables — different colors, different carriers — all terminating into the same central routing panel. The routing infrastructure doesn't care which carrier wins. It handles everything. One cable rendered in cyan marks the active routing path. The image is the argument: the layer that connects competitors is more durable than any one of them.

**Option B — Stat Callout**
"$113M" at 220pt in white dominating the frame, with secondary text "The bet isn't on a model. It's on the layer below." Cyan on "layer below." Strong but loses the irony and the historical resonance. Falls back to a number rather than an idea.

**Option C — Concept Diagram**
Three input nodes (GPT-4 / Claude / Grok) with straight arrows feeding into a central ROUTING LAYER node in cyan, then a single arrow to PRODUCTION STACK. Clean architecture diagram. Useful for technical audiences but loses the emotional weight and scroll-stop power of the scene. Diagram as third option only.

**Rationale for Option A:** The switchboard scene carries the full argument — neutral infrastructure, competing inputs, one routing layer — without any labels. The historical parallel (telecom's neutral switching infrastructure outlasting every individual carrier) maps precisely onto CapitalG's bet that the routing layer outlasts any single AI model. The irony (Google's money backing a platform routing traffic to Google's competitors) is embedded in the image structure: competing cables, one routing board, no preference. The viewer feels the logic before they read the caption.

VISUAL CONCEPT:
A 1960s–1970s telephone exchange switchboard room, photographed from a low-angle wide shot. The dominant element is a dense wall of patch panel sockets with cables of different colors running from the left and right sides — competing carriers, competing lines — all terminating into the same central routing hardware. The cables converge toward a single illuminated routing panel at the center of the frame. The room is real, industrial, slightly aged — not a museum reconstruction. Shot as if from the perspective of the operator's station: you see the panel filling the frame, the converging cables as the visual subject, and the depth of the room receding into darkness behind it.

LIGHTING:
The light source is a single overhead industrial fixture directly above the central routing panel, casting a tight pool of warm tungsten light on the panel face and the cable termination points. The far ends of the cables — representing the competing carriers — fall off into shadow at the frame edges. The center is illuminated; the competitors are peripheral. The light quality is warm, slightly aged, the kind that reads as institutional permanence rather than modern tech glamour.

COLOR TREATMENT:
The dominant palette is desaturated — near-black backgrounds, aged cream and grey on the panel hardware, muted yellows and greens on the patch cables. One single cable, running from the left side of the frame through the routing panel and out the right, is rendered in #ADFBF6 cyan. This is the routing path — the one connection that carries everything. It is the only saturated element in the frame. The cyan cable visually names what the post argues: the routing path is the durable bet, not the endpoints.

ATMOSPHERE:

TEXT ON IMAGE:

RULES:

- The cyan cable is the ONLY saturated element in the entire image — everything else desaturated
- NO modern elements — no LED panels, no fiber optic cables, no digital displays. This is mid-century analog switching hardware
