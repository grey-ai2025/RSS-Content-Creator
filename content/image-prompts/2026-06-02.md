## Visual Direction Considered

**Option A: "The Teller Window" — SELECTED**
Scene: A 1970s bank teller's station shot at close range. The focal point is a rotary-dial telephone handset resting on the wooden counter, receiver slightly off-hook, as though mid-conversation. The teller's hand — visible only from the wrist down, no face — hovers over a transaction ledger, pen in hand, about to authorize something. A small brass name placard and a rubber stamp sit nearby. Nothing in the frame is digital. The authorization is happening entirely through the phone — the same channel as the request.
Lighting: A single overhead incandescent lamp casts a warm, directional cone of light directly onto the handset and the hovering pen, leaving the edges of the counter in deep shadow. The transaction ledger is partially lit — enough to see the open page but not the specific entries.
Color treatment: Near-monochrome warm sepia throughout — desaturated browns and tans. The single cyan accent falls on the telephone coil cord, tracing the only path between the requester and the authorization. It is a subtle, almost unsettling detail: the wire is the security layer.
Atmosphere: Quiet institutional trust being abused — the kind of mistake that feels obvious in hindsight.
Text: "One sentence. Irreversible." — 4 words on screen, rendered in white, bottom-center, small and understated.
