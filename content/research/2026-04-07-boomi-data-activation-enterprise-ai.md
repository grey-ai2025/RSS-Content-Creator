## Summary
Enterprise AI deployments are failing in 2026 — not because the models are wrong or agents can't reason, but because the data feeding those systems is fragmented, inconsistently labelled, and scattered across dozens of disconnected applications. Boomi, one of the largest enterprise integration platforms, is naming this gap "data activation" and positioning it as the foundational prerequisite for any agentic AI deployment to deliver real value. With 75,000 AI agents already running across its customer base and Gartner naming it a Leader in iPaaS for the twelfth consecutive year, Boomi's diagnosis carries significant weight. The company's CEO states plainly: "AI only delivers value when data is properly activated, trusted and governed first." The fix requires standardizing business definitions enterprise-wide before agents are deployed — not after.

## Key Data Points
- 75,000 AI agents currently running in production across Boomi's customer base
- 30,000+ global customers, including over 25% of the Fortune 500
- Gartner named Boomi a Leader in iPaaS for the twelfth consecutive year — with "AI-ready integration" now ranked as a strategic capability
- The failure mode: enterprise data exists abundantly in ERP systems, CRMs, and data lakes — but lacks shared context that lets agents treat data from different systems as reliably compatible
- Boomi introduced Meta Hub — a central system standardizing business definitions enterprise-wide
- New capabilities include real-time SAP data extraction via change data capture, plus enhanced audit trails and session logs for agent transparency
- CEO quote: "AI only delivers value when data is properly activated, trusted and governed first"

## Why It Matters
Most enterprise AI projects stall between pilot and production — and the reason is almost never the model. It is the data. Leaders approving AI budgets are authorizing agent deployments into environments where the underlying data is still fragmented and ungoverned. This story reframes the conversation: the ROI question for AI agents is actually a data governance question first. For CIOs, data architects, and any executive sponsoring an AI-first initiative in 2026, the implication is direct — building the data foundation is not a pre-project nice-to-have, it is the project.
