## Summary
Apple has agreed to pay $250 million to settle a false advertising class-action lawsuit accusing the company of overselling its Apple Intelligence features — specifically a promised AI overhaul of Siri that was advertised at the iPhone 16 launch in 2024 but never materialized. The settlement covers US customers who purchased any model of iPhone 15 or iPhone 16 between June 10, 2024 and March 29, 2025. Apple admitted no fault in the settlement. The case marks one of the first large-scale consumer accountability events in AI, where a company faces direct financial consequences for promising AI capabilities that did not ship on time. A WWDC 2026 announcement on enhanced Siri is expected in June — making the timing of this settlement unusually sharp.

## Key Data Points
- Settlement amount: $250 million common fund
- Eligible claimants: US buyers of iPhone 15 or iPhone 16 (any model), June 10, 2024 – March 29, 2025
- Payout per device: Presumptive $25, may increase up to $95 depending on claims volume
- This is one of the largest settlements Apple has ever reached
- The Better Business Bureau's National Advertising Division had already found that Apple's claim that Apple Intelligence was "available now" was misleading — the upgraded Siri was not available at launch
- In March 2025, Apple confirmed to Daring Fireball that enhanced Siri features would take "longer than we thought" and pulled a TV ad featuring a user asking Siri to recall a meeting contact
- This is Apple's second Siri lawsuit settlement in two years; in May 2025, Apple paid $95 million over claims Siri listened to private conversations
- Apple's statement: the company has "introduced dozens of features across many languages" but "resolved this matter to stay focused on doing what we do best"

## Why It Matters
This settlement is the clearest signal yet that AI overpromising carries direct financial and legal consequences — not just reputational ones. For founders, SMB operators, and team leads, the Apple case sets a precedent: marketing AI capabilities you haven't shipped exposes organizations to liability. The "available now" language that Apple used for features that weren't ready is the same category of claim many vendors make in B2B sales. Any operator currently evaluating AI tools — or building products with AI features — needs to understand that the gap between what a vendor promises and what they deliver is no longer just a customer satisfaction issue. It's a potential class-action. The settlement also frames the upcoming WWDC 2026 Siri announcement as a high-stakes accountability moment: Apple's AI credibility is now on trial in public, not just in court.
