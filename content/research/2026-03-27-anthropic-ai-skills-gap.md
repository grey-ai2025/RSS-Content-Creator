# Anthropic Research: AI Skills Gap Is Widening as Power Users Pull Ahead

**Title:** The AI Skills Gap Is Here, Says AI Company, and Power Users Are Pulling Ahead

**Source URL:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/25/the-ai-skills-gap-is-here-says-ai-company-and-power-users-are-pulling-ahead/

**Publication Date:** March 25, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI in education and workforce upskilling (#11); AI agents at enterprise scale (#1); Agentic AI / agentic systems (#3)

**Summary:**
Anthropic's fifth economic impact report, released at the Axios AI Summit in Washington D.C., finds that AI has not yet meaningfully eliminated jobs — but it is rapidly creating a two-tier workforce divided between early adopters who leverage AI as a strategic "thought partner" and newcomers who are barely using its capabilities. The company's head of economics, Peter McCrory, says there is "no larger material difference in unemployment rates" between workers in AI-exposed roles and those in less exposed ones — yet. But the data shows earlier Claude adopters are gaining significant advantages, using AI for sophisticated work tasks while later adopters use it for casual or one-off purposes. The report also reveals a geographic divide, with AI being used more intensely in high-income countries and U.S. cities with more knowledge workers. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has separately warned that AI could wipe out half of all entry-level white-collar jobs and push unemployment as high as 20% within five years.

**Key Data Points:**
- Entry-level job postings in the U.S. have fallen 35% since 2023, according to Revelio Labs data cited in related coverage
- Dario Amodei has warned AI could eliminate half of entry-level white-collar jobs and push unemployment to 20% within five years
- No material difference in current unemployment rates between AI-exposed and non-AI-exposed roles — but "displacement effects could materialize very quickly"
- Early Claude adopters are significantly more likely to use it for sophisticated work tasks (iteration, feedback, thought partnership) vs. casual use
- "Claude is used more intensely in high-income countries, within the U.S. in places with more knowledge workers, and for a relatively small set of specialized tasks and occupations"
- Anthropic's head of economics called for building a monitoring framework now "before it materializes so that we can catch it as it's happening and identify the appropriate policy response"
- Senator Mark Warner, also at the Axios AI Summit, said a VC told him he's "writing software investments down to zero" due to Claude's capabilities, and a major law firm said it is no longer hiring first-year associates

**Why It Matters:**
This research is significant because it comes from Anthropic itself — the company building the tools causing the disruption. It is one of the first systematic attempts to measure AI's real labor market impact with actual usage data rather than projections. For professional audiences, the skills gap finding is the most actionable insight: workers who learn to use AI as a strategic partner — not just a search engine — are pulling ahead of their peers right now, not in some speculative future. The message for organizations is that AI upskilling programs are not a nice-to-have; they are a competitive necessity that will determine which employees and companies compound their advantages and which fall permanently behind.
