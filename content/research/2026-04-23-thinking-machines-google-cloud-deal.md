# Summary
Mira Murati's Thinking Machines Lab — the secretive AI startup she founded after leaving OpenAI as CTO in February 2025 — has signed a multi-billion-dollar cloud infrastructure deal with Google, valued in the single-digit billions. The agreement gives Thinking Machines access to Google Cloud systems powered by Nvidia's newest GB300 GPUs, which offer a 2x improvement in training and serving speed over prior-generation hardware, plus infrastructure services for model training and deployment. This is the first time Thinking Machines has partnered with a cloud provider (previous partnerships were with Nvidia for chips). The deal was announced at Google Cloud Next 2026 and reflects Google's aggressive strategy to lock in fast-growing frontier labs before they commit to AWS or Azure — coming the same week Anthropic signed separate multi-billion-dollar cloud agreements with both Google and Amazon. Thinking Machines' primary product, Tinker, automates the creation of custom frontier AI models and relies on computationally expensive reinforcement learning — the exact workload Google Cloud is promising to support.

# Key Data Points
- Deal value: single-digit billions (exact figure not disclosed)
- Infrastructure: Google Cloud systems powered by Nvidia GB300 GPUs — 2x faster training and serving vs. prior generation
- First cloud provider partnership for Thinking Machines (previously only hardware deal with Nvidia)
- Deal is non-exclusive — Thinking Machines can use multiple cloud providers
- Mira Murati founded Thinking Machines in February 2025 after departing as OpenAI CTO
- Thinking Machines raised a $2 billion seed round at a $12 billion valuation in mid-2025
- First product: Tinker — automates creation of custom frontier AI models using reinforcement learning
- Timing: announced at Google Cloud Next 2026 (April 22-24, Las Vegas)
- Context: Anthropic signed separate multi-billion-dollar agreements with both Google (TPU capacity via Broadcom) and Amazon ($5B investment / $100B AWS commitment) in the same week
- Google strategy: lock in frontier labs early before they consolidate around AWS or Azure ecosystems

# Why It Matters
For founders evaluating which AI infrastructure bets will matter in 12-18 months, this deal illustrates where the competitive moat is actually forming: cloud providers are not just selling compute, they are signing frontier labs to preferential access deals that will shape which models are trained fastest and deployed cheapest. Thinking Machines is the most closely watched post-OpenAI startup — Murati's pedigree (she ran GPT-4 development) means its models will be enterprise-grade when they ship. The deal also signals that Google is mounting a serious counteroffensive against AWS's Anthropic investment by securing a different frontier lab in the same week. For anyone choosing a cloud strategy or selecting an AI vendor, the Google/AWS battle for frontier lab allegiance is the structural dynamic that will determine model availability, pricing, and capability curves over the next two years.
