# Summary
Amazon has made an additional $5 billion investment in Anthropic, bringing its total commitment to $13 billion — the largest external investment any AI lab has received from a single cloud provider. In return, Anthropic has committed to spending over $100 billion on AWS cloud services over the next decade, with access to Trainium2 through Trainium4 custom AI chips and up to 5 gigawatts of new computing capacity to train and run Claude. The deal mirrors Amazon's $50 billion investment in OpenAI made two months prior, establishing a pattern where hyperscalers are effectively pre-purchasing the AI infrastructure buildout by funding the labs most likely to generate compute demand at scale. Anthropic is currently fielding VC offers at an $800 billion-plus valuation as enterprise demand for Claude accelerates.

# Key Data Points
- New investment: $5 billion from Amazon
- Total Amazon investment in Anthropic: $13 billion
- Anthropic's cloud commitment: $100 billion on AWS over the next decade
- Chip access: Trainium2 through Trainium4 (Trainium4 not yet available); option on future Amazon chips
- Computing capacity: up to 5 gigawatts of new capacity to train and run Claude
- Comparison: Amazon invested $50 billion in OpenAI two months prior — both deals blend capital investment with cloud spending commitments
- Current Anthropic valuation in VC secondary markets: $800 billion or higher
- Strategic pattern: circular deal structure — Amazon funds Anthropic; Anthropic purchases Amazon infrastructure — effectively guaranteeing hyperscaler revenue from AI demand growth
- Context: Anthropic's revenue run rate surpassed OpenAI's ($30B vs. $24B) as of early April 2026, driven by enterprise Claude demand

# Why It Matters
For founders and operators evaluating AI vendors and infrastructure decisions, this deal signals a structural consolidation of the enterprise AI stack around two hyperscaler-backed labs (Anthropic/AWS and OpenAI/Azure). The $100 billion AWS commitment means Anthropic's infrastructure roadmap is locked to Amazon's chip development timeline — Trainium2 through Trainium4 — which directly determines Claude's capability trajectory and inference pricing for enterprise buyers. The $800B valuation context matters for anyone building on or integrating with Claude: this is not a startup risk profile anymore, it is a hyperscaler-backed infrastructure play. The circular investment pattern (fund the lab that buys your cloud) also reveals that the real competition in enterprise AI is now between AWS and Azure ecosystems, not between individual model providers.
