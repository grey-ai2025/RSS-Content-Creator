## Summary

Vercel, the web development infrastructure platform, has grown its annual recurring revenue from $100 million at the start of 2024 to $340 million by February 2026 — a more-than-3x increase driven almost entirely by the explosion in AI agent-generated apps. CEO Guillermo Rauch, speaking at the HumanX conference in San Francisco, disclosed that 30% of apps currently running on Vercel's platform were deployed by AI agents rather than human developers. Rauch positioned this as the beginning of a structural shift: as AI agents become more "prolific at deploying," Vercel's infrastructure becomes the landing zone for everything agents build — making its total addressable market effectively uncapped. The company was last valued at $9.3 billion in its September 2025 Series F ($300M, led by Accel) and is signaling IPO readiness, though Rauch declined to commit to a specific timeline. The Vercel data provides one of the most concrete revenue-level proofs yet that AI agents are not a future possibility — they are already generating measurable infrastructure demand and compounding business value in the present tense.

## Key Data Points

- Vercel ARR: **$100M at start of 2024 → $340M by end of February 2026** — more than 3x growth in approximately two years
- **30% of apps on Vercel's platform were deployed by AI agents** — not human developers, as of April 2026
- **Valuation**: $9.3 billion (September 2025 Series F, $300M, led by Accel); competes with Cloudflare and AWS for infrastructure
- CEO Rauch: **"When I started this company, only tens of millions of people could deploy. Now everybody in the world can create an app."**
- Rauch on total addressable market: **"The total addressable market of infrastructure has now grown, and it simply has no ceiling."**
- Rauch on IPO: **"The company's ready and getting more ready for it every day"** — no specific timeline given
- Also offers **v0**, a vibe-coding tool for creating websites and apps directly — the tool feeding agent-generated app creation
- Context: The broader IPO market is frozen — a software sector sell-off driven by AI disruption fears has paused most listings; Vercel, SpaceX, Anthropic, and OpenAI are the few expected blockbuster debuts remaining
- Rauch's framing of agent-driven software creation: **"All of that software... it needs to go somewhere, and we think it's going to be Vercel"** — positioning agents as the new developer class

## Why It Matters

The 30% figure is the clearest data point yet that AI agents are already functioning as active economic participants — building, deploying, and running software without human developers in the loop for each step. For a founder running a small team, this means the infrastructure cost of shipping software is collapsing: agents can now ship functional apps faster than any hiring cycle. For operators evaluating AI tools, the Vercel revenue trajectory answers the "is agentic AI real or hype?" question with a financial statement rather than a benchmark. The 3x ARR growth from agent-driven demand also reframes the AI hype correction narrative: the hype around AI capabilities may be oversold, but the hype around AI's economic impact on infrastructure is, if anything, undersold — the money is already flowing. Any professional who dismisses agentic AI as "not production-ready" is looking at the wrong metric.
