# Anthropic Research: AI Isn't Taking Jobs Yet, But a Skills Gap Is Opening Fast

**Source URL:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/25/the-ai-skills-gap-is-here-says-ai-company-and-power-users-are-pulling-ahead/

**Publication Date:** March 25, 2026

**Keyword Match:** generative AI productivity, AI agents, future of work

## Summary

Anthropic presented early research findings at the Axios AI Summit in Washington, D.C. showing that while AI has not yet begun causing widespread job displacement, a measurable skills divide is already forming between workers who use AI tools proficiently and those who do not. Power users — workers who have developed fluency with AI tools — are gaining a competitive edge over their peers, creating a performance gap that Anthropic researchers believe will widen significantly as AI capabilities improve. The research comes as Senator Mark Warner, speaking at the same event, cited a major law firm that has stopped hiring first-year associates because AI now handles much of that work — indicating that displacement, while not yet statistical, is already being experienced at the hiring level for specific roles. The Anthropic findings add nuance to the polarized AI employment debate: the story is not yet "AI takes all the jobs," but it is already "AI favors those who know how to use it," and that distinction is becoming more consequential with each capability improvement.

## Key Data Points

- Source: Anthropic internal research, presented at the Axios AI Summit, Washington D.C., March 2026
- Finding: AI has not yet materially displaced jobs at a measurable statistical level
- Finding: "Power users" — experienced AI tool users — are pulling ahead of peers in measurable output and performance metrics
- The gap between power users and non-users is already visible and described as growing
- Context data from same event: entry-level job postings in the U.S. down 35% since 2023 (Revelio Labs)
- Context: a major law firm told Sen. Warner it has stopped hiring first-year associates due to AI
- Anthropic's framing focuses on inequality risk: skill-based AI advantage could entrench new workplace hierarchies
- Research raises questions about who bears responsibility for AI upskilling: employers, workers, or government
- Dovetails with the Harvard Business School GenAI study finding AI boosts output but cannot replace expertise

## Why It Matters

The "power user pulling ahead" pattern is more consequential for most professionals than the headline "AI takes jobs" narrative, because it is already happening, it is already creating performance differentials that affect promotions, hiring, and compensation, and it requires a response now rather than in some hypothetical future. For leaders managing teams, the Anthropic data is a direct challenge: if some portion of your workforce is extracting significantly more output using AI and another portion is not using it at all, you have a productivity inequality problem inside your own organization that will compound over time. For individuals, the message is unambiguous: the question is no longer whether to develop AI proficiency but how quickly the gap becomes difficult to close. This story is also significant because it comes from Anthropic — one of the leading AI developers — which means it reflects data from actual Claude usage patterns, not survey responses about intention.
