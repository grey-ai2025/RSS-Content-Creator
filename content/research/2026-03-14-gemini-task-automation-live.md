# Gemini's Task Automation Is Live — and It's Wild

**Source URL:** https://www.theverge.com/tech/893820/gemini-task-automation-samsung-s26-google-pixel-10

**Publication Date:** March 12, 2026

**Keyword Match:** Gemini task automation, agentic AI

## Summary

Google's Gemini task automation feature has rolled out in beta on the Galaxy S26 Ultra, allowing the AI to use apps on the user's behalf inside a virtual window — ordering food, booking rides, and navigating menus without human input at each step. The Verge's reviewer tested it live: Gemini booked an Uber to the airport by asking for clarification on the destination, then autonomously completing the steps before pausing for final human approval. In a second test, Gemini scrolled through Starbucks' full menu, identified a flat white, and correctly decided to order the croissant warmed — without being told. The system stops before final purchase confirmation, keeping humans in the loop on consequential actions. The feature is launching first for food delivery and rideshare apps, with broader app support expected to follow.

## Key Data Points

- Live in beta on Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra as of March 12, 2026
- Google Pixel 10 also scheduled to receive the feature
- Operates in a virtual window — users can watch Gemini navigate the app in real time
- Users can intervene or stop the automation at any point
- Gemini asks clarifying questions before acting (e.g., which airport, which terminal)
- Final purchase/confirmation step always requires human approval
- Launching initially with Uber and food delivery apps; broader rollout planned
- Described by The Verge reviewer as "weird watching your phone use itself"

## Why It Matters

This is the most concrete consumer demonstration of agentic AI to date — not a demo, not a promise, but a live feature ordering coffee and calling cars on a real device. For years, AI assistants have claimed they would handle tasks; Gemini is now doing it. The human-in-the-loop design (pausing before final confirmation) is also a signal about how agentic AI will be governed at the consumer layer: autonomous enough to be useful, constrained enough to stay trusted. Every enterprise thinking about AI agent deployment can learn from how Google has scoped and staged this rollout.
