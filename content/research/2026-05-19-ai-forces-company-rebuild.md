## Summary
Fast Company contributor Enrique Dans argues that the core problem with enterprise AI adoption is not how companies are using AI in their processes — it is that their processes were never designed for AI in the first place. Bolting AI onto legacy workflows produces marginal gains at best and organizational chaos at worst. The article frames the AI transformation moment as a fundamental architectural challenge: companies built around human-speed, human-scale operations are being asked to run at machine speed without redesigning the underlying structure. The author contends that only organizations willing to rebuild their operating model around AI — not add AI to their existing model — will capture meaningful competitive advantage. This aligns with and extends the pattern identified in the Writer.com report (only 23% of enterprises report meaningful ROI from AI agents) by naming the root cause: structural mismatch, not insufficient tools.

## Why It Matters
For founders and SMB operators, this article names the most common mistake in AI deployment: treating it as a feature added to the existing company rather than a reason to reconsider how the company is structured. A 10-person services firm that automates its old quoting process is not the same as a 10-person firm that redesigns its delivery model to leverage AI at every handoff. The first gets marginal time savings. The second gets a structurally different cost base. The practical implication: the companies that are winning with AI are not the ones who bought the most tools — they are the ones who redesigned how work flows through the organization. For a founder running a team, this means auditing not just "what AI tools do we have" but "what does our operating model assume about human time, human attention, and human bottlenecks" — because those assumptions are now optional constraints, not fixed ones.
