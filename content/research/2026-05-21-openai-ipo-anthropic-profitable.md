# OpenAI Targets September IPO — Anthropic Hits First Profitable Quarter in the Same Week

## Summary

OpenAI is preparing to file IPO paperwork confidentially "within days or weeks," targeting a public debut by September 2026 and working with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley. The path cleared this week after Elon Musk lost his lawsuit against OpenAI — a legal battle that had threatened the company's structure and finances. In the same week, Anthropic told investors it expects its first profitable quarter in Q2 2026, projecting approximately $10.9 billion in revenue — more than doubling its prior quarter — driven by surging Claude adoption among professionals, small businesses, and law firms. NYT DealBook reported that SpaceX, OpenAI, and Anthropic are now in a three-way race to test public market appetite for AI companies, with SpaceX having already filed its S-1 at an $80 billion valuation.

---

## Key Data Points

- OpenAI targeting a **September 2026 IPO**; IPO paperwork confidential filing expected "within days or weeks"
- Musk's lawsuit against OpenAI was unanimously dismissed by jury on May 18, removing the last major legal obstacle
- OpenAI reportedly valued at **$500 billion** in its most recent private round
- Banks working on OpenAI IPO: **Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley**
- Anthropic projecting **$10.9 billion in Q2 2026 revenue** — more than double the prior quarter
- Anthropic's first profitable quarter ever expected in Q2 2026
- Anthropic's growth driven by professionals, small business owners, and law firms adopting Claude
- Note: Anthropic's profitability may not persist full year due to large scheduled compute costs ($1.25 billion/month paid to xAI for compute, per SpaceX S-1 filing)
- SpaceX S-1 filed at **$80 billion valuation** — the largest IPO in history if completed

---

## Why It Matters

Three of the most valuable AI companies in the world are now preparing to enter public markets within months of each other. For founders and business operators, this has two immediate implications. First, the IPO process will force OpenAI and Anthropic to publish financial disclosures that reveal actual revenue, burn rates, and business model mechanics — information that has been entirely opaque. Every enterprise buyer negotiating AI contracts will have leverage data they don't have today. Second, the race to public markets signals that private AI valuations are being locked in now. Founders building AI-enabled products need to understand that the competitive landscape is about to be recalibrated by public market pricing, investor scrutiny, and the kind of financial discipline that comes with quarterly earnings calls. If OpenAI's IPO succeeds at $500B+, it validates the entire AI infrastructure investment cycle and likely accelerates enterprise AI adoption spending.
