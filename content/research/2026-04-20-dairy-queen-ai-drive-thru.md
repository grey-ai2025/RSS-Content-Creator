# Dairy Queen Rolls Out AI Voice Agents in Drive-Thrus Across the US and Canada

**Source URL:** https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/913928/dairy-queen-ai-drive-thru-presto

**Publication Date:** April 17, 2026

**Keyword Match:** Agentic commerce (Priority Cluster #5); Agentic AI as a fait accompli (Priority Cluster #1)

## Summary

Dairy Queen is becoming the latest major fast food chain to deploy AI voice agents in its drive-thrus across the US and Canada, using technology built by Presto — the same AI company already operating in Carl's Jr., Hardee's, Taco John's, and Fazoli's. Following a successful test last year, Dairy Queen is moving to a wide rollout in select franchise locations. The AI chatbot handles order-taking and is specifically designed to upsell customers with additional menu items. Presto's system achieves approximately 90% order accuracy. The announcement comes as AI drive-thru adoption has spread across the quick-service restaurant industry, with Wendy's (Google-powered), McDonald's (IBM pilot, since ended), and Burger King all having run or actively running their own versions. The technology represents a fully autonomous commercial transaction — an AI agent executing a revenue-generating interaction with zero human involvement.

## Key Data Points

- Dairy Queen partnering with Presto for AI voice chatbot in drive-thrus (US and Canada)
- Presto's system achieves ~90% order accuracy
- Presto also operates in: Carl's Jr., Hardee's, Taco John's, Fazoli's
- Goal: speed up service and "encourage customers to add more food to their orders" (upsell)
- Tested last year; now moving to broad franchise rollout
- Wendy's, McDonald's (ended pilot), Burger King are also running or have run AI drive-thrus
- Bloomberg reported in 2023 that Presto's "AI" drive-thrus include human workers in countries like the Philippines as backup — transparency concern remains live
- Burger King is also deploying AI inside employee headsets to monitor "friendliness"

## Why It Matters

For SMB operators in retail, food service, hospitality, or any customer-facing business, the Dairy Queen deployment is the clearest mainstream proof point that AI agents are already handling commercial transactions at scale — not in labs, not in Fortune 500 pilot programs, but in drive-thrus serving everyday customers. The model is: AI agent takes the order, upsells, and routes to fulfillment. That same pattern — autonomous agent handles inbound, qualifies, and upsells — is already available to small operators through tools that cost less per month than a part-time employee. The question is no longer whether this is real. It is: how does your business's customer interaction stack compare to what a fast food franchise is running?
