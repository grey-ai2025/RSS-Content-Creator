## Summary

**Note: This file covers a major new development on a story previously documented in `2026-04-11-anthropic-mythos-cybersecurity-banking.md`. What's new: the narrative has reversed. Where the original story reported that Treasury Secretary Bessent and Fed Chair Powell convened bank executives to warn them about Mythos's systemic risk, Bloomberg now reports that same meeting included officials encouraging banks to actively test Mythos to detect vulnerabilities. Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, Bank of America, and Morgan Stanley are reportedly already testing the model. UK financial regulators are separately discussing Mythos risk. The story has shifted from "regulators are alarmed" to "regulators are simultaneously alarmed and promoting adoption" — a contradiction that defines the current AI governance moment.**

Within the same week that the US Treasury and Federal Reserve convened major bank CEOs to discuss the systemic risks of Anthropic's Claude Mythos Preview, Bloomberg reports that Trump administration officials used that same meeting to encourage banks to test Mythos for defensive vulnerability detection. This creates a direct and unresolved contradiction at the regulatory level: the model is too dangerous to release publicly, the Department of Defense has labeled Anthropic a supply-chain risk, the company is in active litigation with the Trump administration — and simultaneously, the Treasury Secretary is encouraging the nation's largest banks to adopt it. JPMorgan Chase is already an official launch partner; Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, Bank of America, and Morgan Stanley are now testing it. UK financial regulators are in separate discussions about Mythos risk.

## Key Data Points

- **Bloomberg report**: Bessent and Powell's meeting with bank CEOs included encouragement to actively test Mythos for vulnerability detection — not just a warning session
- Banks now confirmed testing Mythos: **JPMorgan Chase** (official launch partner), **Goldman Sachs, Citigroup, Bank of America, Morgan Stanley**
- **UK financial regulators** are also in discussions about the risks Mythos poses (Financial Times)
- Contradiction: Anthropic is simultaneously in **active litigation against the Trump administration's DoD** over the supply-chain risk designation — while Trump's Treasury official is pushing banks to adopt Anthropic's flagship model
- Anthropic's stated reason for limiting Mythos access: the model is "too good at finding security vulnerabilities" — a concern about proliferation to bad actors
- Anthropic is **not planning general availability** for Mythos Preview — only vetted organizations with safety commitments can access it
- Alternative interpretation flagged by TechCrunch: restricting Mythos may be a "smart enterprise sales strategy" rather than purely a safety decision — creating scarcity and driving high-value institutional deals

## Why It Matters

The most important signal here is not the model's capabilities — it's what the contradictory regulatory response tells every enterprise buyer and security leader. In a single week: the US government declared Anthropic a national security risk, a federal court partially overturned that designation, Trump's Treasury promoted Anthropic's most powerful model to the nation's largest banks, and UK regulators opened a parallel risk discussion. For any executive responsible for AI vendor strategy or enterprise security posture, this is the clearest possible signal that the rules governing AI in regulated industries are being written in real time — by people who are themselves uncertain about the right answer. The personal-stakes angle is direct: if you are in financial services, fintech, or any regulated industry, your competitors are already testing capabilities your regulators haven't decided are safe yet. The window between "early adopter access" and "broad availability" is the only window where defensive advantage exists.
