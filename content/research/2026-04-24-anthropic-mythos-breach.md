## Summary
Anthropic's Claude Mythos — a model the company had restricted from public release on the grounds that its cybersecurity capabilities were too dangerous — was accessed by a small group of unauthorized users who found it by making an educated guess about its online location, according to Bloomberg. The breach was not a sophisticated exploit: the group combined insider knowledge from a previously disclosed data breach at a third-party company (Mercor) with a standard URL-guessing technique that has been a known attack vector for 20 years. Security researchers describe the failure as "entirely imaginable" and note Anthropic should have anticipated it, particularly since the Mercor breach was already public before Mythos launched. The incident is especially damaging because Anthropic built its brand on being the AI safety-first lab — and Mythos had been framed as a "watershed moment for security" capable of finding vulnerabilities in "every major operating system and web browser."

## Key Data Points
- Unauthorized access to Mythos occurred via "educated guess about the model's online location" — not a sophisticated exploit
- Group used leaked information from Mercor (AI training data company breached before Mythos launch) + insider access from contract work
- Security researcher Lukasz Olejnik: breach was "entirely imaginable" — a standard hacking technique the industry has dealt with for 20 years
- Anthropic CAN log and track model use — evidently wasn't monitoring closely enough for a model billed as highly dangerous
- Unauthorized users reportedly did NOT use Mythos for cybersecurity tasks (which could have caused real harm)
- Anthropic claimed Mythos found vulnerabilities in "every major operating system and web browser"
- Mozilla CTO: Mythos found 271 security bugs in Firefox 150 — described it as equal to "world's best security researchers"
- This is the second Mythos security incident: its existence was previously revealed through an "unsecured data trove" before launch
- RUSI researcher Pia Hüsch: "The fact that this has been accessed through unauthorized means so quickly, and through such an unsophisticated attempt, is really a humiliation for them."
- Anthropic is investigating; NSA and US agencies reportedly have authorized access despite Anthropic's "supply chain risk" designation

## Why It Matters
Businesses evaluating whether to deploy AI tools from vendors who claim "safety-first" positioning now have a concrete data point: Anthropic's own security practices failed to protect a model it described as dangerously powerful — through a completely foreseeable attack vector. For founders and operators, this story reframes the governance question from "what can the AI do to us?" to "can we trust the vendors building these tools to protect access to them?" Any organization considering using Claude Mythos or similar enterprise AI deployments should be auditing vendor security practices, not just model capabilities. The breach also undercuts Anthropic's competitive differentiation at a moment when OpenAI just released GPT-5.5 — the safety-first narrative just took a reputational hit.
