# If You're Giving a Commencement Speech in 2026, Maybe Don't Mention AI

**Source URL:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/17/if-youre-giving-a-commencement-speech-in-2026-maybe-dont-mention-ai/

**Publication Date:** May 17, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI backlash and adoption gap; Eric Schmidt / AI commencement backlash

**Summary:**
Multiple commencement speakers at US universities were booed by graduating students in May 2026 when they pivoted to AI optimism in their speeches. Former Google CEO Eric Schmidt was booed at the University of Arizona when he told graduates "You will help shape artificial intelligence." Real estate executive Gloria Caulfield drew the same reaction at the University of Central Florida after declaring AI "the next industrial revolution." Notably, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang addressed AI at Carnegie Mellon with no similar pushback. A Gallup poll cited in coverage shows only 43% of Americans aged 15-34 believe it's a good time to find employment locally — down from 75% in 2022 — framing the backlash as economic anxiety, not technophobia. The booing is being read across tech media as a cultural inflection point: AI optimism from executives now reads to young audiences as willful blindness to job market disruption.

**Key Data Points:**
- Eric Schmidt booed at University of Arizona commencement for AI comments
- Gloria Caulfield (real estate executive) booed at University of Central Florida for calling AI "the next industrial revolution"
- Jensen Huang addressed AI at Carnegie Mellon with no notable pushback (context: engineering audience)
- Gallup poll: only 43% of Americans aged 15-34 believe it's a good time to find employment locally (down from 75% in 2022)
- The pattern is consistent enough that TechCrunch's headline explicitly advises against mentioning AI in commencement speeches
- Context from prior week: NYT ran "The Villain of This Year's Commencement Speeches: A.I." — aligns with trending keyword already in SEO file
- Prior student groups at University of Arizona called for Schmidt's removal before his speech over an unrelated lawsuit, amplifying the adversarial context

**Why It Matters:**
For founders and team leads, this backlash is a direct signal about where the public narrative on AI is heading. The same employees you're trying to get to adopt AI tools are in the same cohort as students booing Schmidt. If your internal AI adoption messaging sounds like an executive commencement speech — "AI is the next industrial revolution, embrace it" — you're triggering the same rejection. The 43% employment confidence stat is the real data point: AI adoption resistance isn't irrational fear, it's people doing math about their own economic position. Messaging that skips the "here's what changes for you" step and jumps straight to "AI is an incredible opportunity" will now actively backfire. This is the audience psychology behind the 79% enterprise adoption challenge stat.
