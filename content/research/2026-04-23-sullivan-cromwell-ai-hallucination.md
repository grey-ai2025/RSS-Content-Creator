Sullivan & Cromwell, One of America's Most Elite Law Firms, Submitted a Court Filing Full of AI Hallucinations

# Summary
Sullivan & Cromwell — one of the oldest and most prestigious law firms in the United States, currently representing President Trump in multiple appeals — apologized to a federal bankruptcy judge after submitting a court filing that contained approximately three dozen errors generated by AI, including "hallucinations" that fabricated case citations. A partner at the firm, Andrew Dietderich, wrote to Judge Martin Glenn on April 18 acknowledging the errors, which spanned three pages. Opposing counsel discovered the errors. Some were clerical; a significant number involved AI-generated citations of imagined passages from real cases — a failure mode now recurring across the legal profession despite high-profile warnings. This comes weeks after lawyers in an Oregon winery case were fined $110,000 for citing AI-generated bogus case law. The legal profession is experiencing a structural reckoning: AI handles voluminous research efficiently but consistently produces authoritative-sounding legal falsehoods.

# Key Data Points
- Firm: Sullivan & Cromwell — one of the oldest and most prestigious law firms in the US
- Current clients: representing President Trump in several appeals including his 2024 criminal conviction
- Error count: approximately three dozen errors across three pages of a court filing
- Nature of errors: AI-generated "hallucinations" including fabricated passages from real case citations; also some non-AI clerical errors
- Discovery: identified by opposing counsel, not by S&C's own review
- Forum: US Bankruptcy Court in Manhattan (Judge Martin Glenn)
- Context: an Oregon winery case just weeks prior saw lawyers fined $110,000 for submitting AI-generated bogus case law
- Pattern: the legal profession has received repeated high-profile warnings about AI hallucinations in legal filings; errors continue

# Why It Matters
The key signal here is not that AI made errors — it is that a firm of Sullivan & Cromwell's caliber and resources made errors that were caught by the other side, not caught internally. If one of the most sophisticated legal organizations in the world has not implemented review processes sufficient to catch AI hallucinations before filing, this exposes a structural truth: AI output verification is an organizational capability, not just a tool setting. For any founder using AI to produce documents, contracts, proposals, or reports that will be read and acted upon by another party, this is a direct risk story. The phrase "hallucinations that fabricated case citations" maps directly to any professional context where AI produces plausible-sounding sources, statistics, or attributions that don't exist. The professional liability exposure is not theoretical — the courts are already fining lawyers for it.
