# Anthropic Could Raise $50B at a $900B Valuation — Nearly Doubling in 3 Months

**Source URL:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/29/sources-anthropic-could-raise-a-new-50b-round-at-a-valuation-of-900b/

**Publication Date:** April 29–30, 2026

**Keyword Match:** Google-Anthropic $40B investment; agentic AI; AI agents enterprise adoption

## Summary

Anthropic is fielding pre-emptive offers to raise approximately $50 billion at a valuation between $850B and $900B, according to sources familiar with the matter. The company's board is expected to decide on terms in May. This would nearly triple the $380B valuation from its February funding round and push Anthropic close to OpenAI's $852B post-money valuation. The fundraising frenzy is being driven by explosive revenue growth — Anthropic's annual run rate has reportedly surged from roughly $9B at end of 2025 to approximately $40B today, with products like Claude Code and Cowork leading the charge.

## Key Data Points

- Pre-emptive offers value Anthropic at $850B–$900B
- Potential raise: ~$50B
- February 2026 valuation: $380B — meaning valuation has more than doubled in under 3 months
- Current annual revenue run rate: ~$40B (up from ~$9B at end of 2025)
- Investor demand so intense that one institution prepared to commit $5B cannot get a meeting with the CFO
- Growth drivers: Claude Code, Cowork (desktop agent), enterprise expansion in finance, life sciences, and healthcare
- Google's previously announced $40B commitment to Anthropic is the largest single AI funding deal in history

## Why It Matters

For founders and team leads, this is the strongest signal yet that the AI tools they are evaluating today — Claude, Claude Code, Cowork — are backed by a company with a near-trillion-dollar mandate and the deepest enterprise adoption in the market. The $40B revenue run rate means Anthropic is not a startup curiosity; it is enterprise infrastructure. Founders choosing between AI tool vendors need to factor in the staying power these funding rounds imply. It also signals that the "pilots to production" narrative is now backed by real revenue at scale — not projection.
