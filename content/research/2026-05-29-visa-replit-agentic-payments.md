## Summary
Visa has made an undisclosed investment in Replit and is exploring integrations that would allow developers — and the AI agents they build — to accept and make payments directly within the Replit platform. The partnership centers on Visa Intelligent Commerce (Visa's AI payments suite) and the Visa Trusted Agent Protocol, which gives AI agents a verifiable identity so payment systems can confirm the agent's intent and the human principal behind it. Replit simultaneously launched self-serve enterprise access at up to $200,000 contract value, no salesperson required. The Visa-Replit deal lands one day after Robinhood launched agentic trading, and alongside Google's Universal Commerce Protocol announcement — all within the same week. More than 1,000 Visa employees already use Replit for prototyping. Replit itself is valued at $9 billion following a March 2026 raise, up from $3 billion just six months prior.

## Key Data Points
- Visa investment in Replit: undisclosed amount
- Visa Trusted Agent Protocol: a system allowing AI agents to identify themselves (intent + customer details) so payments are verified before execution
- Visa Intelligent Commerce: the suite Replit developers will be able to integrate for AI-powered payments
- More than 1,000 Visa employees use Replit for prototyping and development
- Replit self-serve enterprise tier: contracts up to $200,000, no sales call required; includes SSO, audit logs, advanced permissions
- Replit valuation: $9 billion (March 2026), up 3x from $3 billion (September 2025) — six months apart
- Replit net revenue retention: 300% in some enterprise cases
- Context: Robinhood agentic trading launched May 27; Google Universal Commerce Protocol also announced this week — all targeting the same agentic payment layer

## Why It Matters
The Visa-Replit deal is significant because it comes from the payments infrastructure side, not just the AI tooling side. Visa is the world's largest payment network. When Visa builds a protocol specifically to identify and verify AI agents making payments, it is acknowledging that agent-initiated transactions are not a future scenario — they are already happening at scale. The Trusted Agent Protocol is the key detail: the problem it solves (how does a payment system know an AI agent is acting on legitimate human authority?) is the same accountability gap that regulators and CFOs are asking about right now. The self-serve $200K enterprise tier at Replit is also a structural signal: enterprise AI procurement is decentralizing — teams are now authorizing six-figure AI infrastructure contracts without procurement cycles.

## Why It Matters for Founders and Operators
This story is directly relevant for founders building any product that involves AI agents taking actions with financial consequences — from expense automation to customer payment handling. The Visa Trusted Agent Protocol defines what "safe agentic payment delegation" looks like: the agent identifies its intent, shares the human principal's context, and the payment network verifies before executing. That is the architecture every business needs to build if it is deploying AI in any payment-adjacent workflow. For developers specifically, Replit at $9 billion with 300% NRR is evidence that the vibe-coding + agentic tooling market is not a bubble — it is a genuine accelerant that enterprises are paying for and not churning out of. The ability to move from idea to production without a sales conversation is a meaningful threshold change in enterprise software procurement.
