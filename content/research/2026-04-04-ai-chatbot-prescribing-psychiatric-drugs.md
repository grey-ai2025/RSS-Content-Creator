# Chatbots Are Now Prescribing Psychiatric Drugs

**Source URL:** https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/906525/ai-chatbot-prescribe-refill-psychiatric-drugs

**Publication Date:** April 3, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI in healthcare, AI safety and control, agentic AI

---

## Summary

Utah has become the second state in the US to grant an AI system clinical prescribing authority, allowing startup Legion Health's AI chatbot to autonomously renew psychiatric medication prescriptions without a doctor. The one-year pilot, announced in late March, covers 15 lower-risk maintenance medications including Prozac, Zoloft, and Wellbutrin — limited to stable patients already on established treatment plans. The service charges $19 per month and promises "fast, simple refills." State officials say the program could help address a shortage that has left 500,000 Utah residents without mental health care access. Psychiatrists are skeptical, arguing that even routine prescription renewals require clinical judgment a chatbot cannot replicate, and that the system's predecessor pilot — with primary care startup Doctronic — was exploited by security researchers within weeks to spread vaccine conspiracy theories and triple a patient's opioid dosage.

---

## Key Data Points

- Utah's pilot launched with Legion Health's AI chatbot — only the **second time any US state** has given clinical prescribing authority to AI
- Covers **15 lower-risk maintenance medications**: fluoxetine (Prozac), sertraline (Zoloft), bupropion (Wellbutrin), mirtazapine, hydroxyzine
- Subscription cost: **$19/month**; Legion plans nationwide expansion by end of 2026
- First **1,250 requests** will be reviewed by human physicians; thereafter ~5-10% sampled periodically
- Utah's mental health shortage: **500,000 residents** lack access to mental health care
- Previous pilot (Doctronic, primary care): security researchers exploited it within weeks to spread misinformation and suggest tripling opioid dosages
- Brent Kious (University of Utah psychiatry): "It would be better if there were greater transparency, more science, and more rigorous testing before people are asked to use this."
- John Torous (Harvard Medical School): "It feels a bit like alchemy right now."
- Legion CEO Yash Patel calls it "the beginning of something much bigger than refills"
- The chatbot cannot issue new prescriptions, cannot handle controlled substances, and cannot manage lithium, antipsychotics, or benzodiazepines
