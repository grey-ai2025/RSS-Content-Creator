## Summary

Anthropic's most capable AI model, Claude Mythos Preview, has already identified thousands of zero-day vulnerabilities across every major operating system and browser — and the company's response was not to release it, but to quietly deploy it to the organizations responsible for keeping the internet secure. The initiative is called Project Glasswing, and it involves 11 major launch partners including AWS, Apple, Microsoft, Google, CrowdStrike, and JPMorganChase, with $100 million in usage credits committed. The model's cybersecurity capabilities emerged not by design but as a downstream consequence of general improvements in code, reasoning, and autonomy — a fact that has alarmed U.S. regulators. Treasury Secretary Bessent and Fed Chair Powell separately convened the CEOs of America's largest banks — including Bank of America, JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, and Citigroup — to discuss the systemic risks Mythos poses to the financial system, in what represents the first time a single AI model has triggered a government-level convening of financial leadership.

## Key Data Points

- Claude Mythos Preview identified a **27-year-old bug in OpenBSD** and a **17-year-old remote code execution flaw in FreeBSD** (CVE-2026-4747) enabling complete server compromise
- The model can chain **three, four, or five vulnerabilities in sequence** to produce sophisticated exploits — described by Anthropic researcher Nicholas Carlini
- **Project Glasswing launch partners**: AWS, Apple, Broadcom, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Google, JPMorganChase, Linux Foundation, Microsoft, Nvidia, Palo Alto Networks — plus 40+ additional organizations
- **$100 million in Mythos Preview usage credits** committed by partners; **$4 million in direct donations** to open-source security organizations
- Anthropic confirmed: "We do not plan to make Claude Mythos Preview generally available due to its cybersecurity capabilities" — concern centers on proliferation to actors lacking safety commitments
- **Treasury Secretary Bessent and Fed Chair Powell** convened CEOs of Bank of America, JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, and Citigroup to discuss systemic risk
- Anthropic's revenue run rate has now reached **$30 billion**, passing OpenAI's $24 billion — enterprise demand drove the surge, with 1,000+ businesses now spending $1M+ annually on Claude
- Model capabilities emerged as a "downstream consequence" of improvements in general code, reasoning, and autonomy — not intentional cybersecurity design

## Why It Matters

This is not an AI safety hypothetical. A model that can find 27-year-old bugs in production operating systems in hours has already changed the threat landscape — and the fact that America's top financial regulators had to call an emergency meeting of major bank CEOs tells every enterprise security leader, CIO, and risk officer exactly what stakes are on the table. For executives, the personal-stake angle is direct: the attack surface of every enterprise technology stack is about to be re-evaluated by AI systems far faster than any human team. The governance question — who gets access to these capabilities, under what terms, and with what oversight — is now a boardroom and regulatory conversation, not just a researcher debate. Companies that are not already integrating AI-powered defensive security will be running years behind AI-enabled attackers.
