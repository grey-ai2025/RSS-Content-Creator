## Summary
Microsoft has completed a significant leadership reorganization that consolidates its entire workplace AI stack — LinkedIn, Office, Teams, Copilot, and agents — under two executives reporting directly to CEO Satya Nadella. Ryan Roslansky, who runs LinkedIn, is now also leading Office and Teams under a new "Work Experiences Group." Charles Lamanna, one of Microsoft's fastest-rising leaders, will head the "Copilot, Agents, and Platform" (CAP) team covering Microsoft 365, Dynamics 365, SharePoint, and OneDrive. The trigger was the retirement of Rajesh Jha, a 35-year Microsoft veteran who led Windows, Office, Copilot, and Microsoft 365. Jeff Teper becomes executive VP of apps and agents under Lamanna. The structural message is clear: Microsoft is unifying its workplace products around AI agents as the central organizing layer, not treating Copilot as a separate feature layer bolted on top. The change goes into effect this week, with the new structure fully in place by June 30.

## Key Data Points
- Ryan Roslansky (LinkedIn CEO) now leads Office, Teams, and LinkedIn under a new "Work Experiences Group"
- Charles Lamanna now leads "Copilot, Agents, and Platform" (CAP) — covering M365, Dynamics 365, BizChat, SharePoint, OneDrive
- Jeff Teper named executive VP of apps and agents under Lamanna
- Kirk Koenigsbauer named president of Data Platform and Growth under Lamanna
- Perry Clarke becomes CTO of Application Systems, responsible for systems architecture "across M365 and Copilot, and how that composes efficiently with model families, Azure cloud, and silicon"
- Trigger: Rajesh Jha (35-year Microsoft veteran, head of experiences and devices) announced retirement in March 2026; departs June 30, 2026
- Lamanna and Roslansky will report directly to Satya Nadella starting June 30
- Microsoft simultaneously offering voluntary retirement to US employees whose age + years of service equals 70+, potentially thinning Windows and Office legacy headcount
- Roslansky appointed Daniel Shapero as new LinkedIn CEO the prior week (now reports to Roslansky)
- Microsoft Surface and Windows remain under Pavan Davuluri, separate from the AI work stack

## Why It Matters
Microsoft is not reorganizing around a product. It is reorganizing around AI agents as the new operating layer of work. Putting LinkedIn, Office, Teams, Copilot, and agent infrastructure under two leaders — with agent-and-platform getting its own dedicated division — is a structural bet that enterprise work will happen through AI agents before it happens through apps. For founders and team leads already using Microsoft 365, this matters because Copilot and agent capabilities across Teams, Word, Excel, and LinkedIn will now be engineered as one coherent system rather than stitched-together features. The voluntary retirement program, layered on top of this, signals that Microsoft is also trimming legacy skill sets in favor of AI-native product teams. This is the org-chart rewrite happening in plain sight at the most widely used enterprise software company in the world.
