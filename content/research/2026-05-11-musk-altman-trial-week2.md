## Summary
Week 2 of the Musk v. Altman trial turned the tables on Musk's narrative. OpenAI president Greg Brockman testified that Musk himself pushed for a for-profit structure in 2017, demanding majority equity and board control — and "stormed around the table" when refused. Shivon Zilis, former board member and mother of four of Musk's children, testified that Musk tried to recruit Altman to lead an AI lab inside Tesla, and asked colleagues "to send a list of top OpenAI people to poach." Brockman's personal diary entries were also exposed, showing he once wrote about wanting to become a billionaire — undermining the purely mission-driven narrative. A trial outcome against OpenAI could derail its anticipated trillion-dollar IPO, while Musk's own xAI/SpaceX prepares a $1.75 trillion public offering.

## Key Data Points
- Brockman's testimony: Musk demanded majority equity and board control over OpenAI in 2017
- Musk reaction when refused equal shares: "stormed around the table" and left with a Tesla painting
- Zilis testimony: Musk asked colleagues to compile a list of "top OpenAI people to poach"
- Musk sent threatening messages before trial: "By the end of this week, you and Sam will be the most hated men in America"
- Brockman's diary exposed: wrote about wanting to become a billionaire (cited by Musk as evidence mission was abandoned)
- Brockman holds stakes in multiple companies with OpenAI business ties — conflict of interest raised
- Trial at stake: OpenAI's anticipated trillion-dollar IPO; Musk's xAI/SpaceX at $1.75 trillion valuation
- Underlying legal question: whether OpenAI's nonprofit-to-for-profit conversion was a breach of founding mission

## Why It Matters
The Musk v. Altman trial is the first major legal test of how frontier AI labs can govern themselves and change their corporate structure. For founders who rely on OpenAI's APIs and products — or who are deciding whether to build on OpenAI vs. alternatives — the trial's outcome has direct implications. A ruling that restricts OpenAI's for-profit conversion could freeze its product roadmap and capital raising ability. More broadly, the trial is surfacing the governance questions every AI-adjacent company will eventually face: who controls the mission, who gets the equity, and what happens when a nonprofit tool becomes a trillion-dollar commercial platform?
