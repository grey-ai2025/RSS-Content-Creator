# Title
Meta Is Recording Employees' Keystrokes and Mouse Movements to Train Its AI Agents

# Publication Date

# Summary
Meta has deployed a tool called Model Capability Initiative (MCI) on US-based employees' computers that records mouse movements, clicks, keystrokes, and occasional screenshots across work-related apps and websites. The stated purpose is to generate training data for Meta's AI agents — teaching them how to interact with computers the way humans do, so they can automate the kinds of tasks Meta employees currently perform. The initiative is part of Meta's Agent Transformation Accelerator (ATA) program, announced internally by CTO Andrew Bosworth, whose memo stated the company's goal: "The vision we are building towards is one where our agents primarily do the work and our role is to direct, review and help them improve." Meta says the data will not be used for performance assessments and that safeguards are in place for sensitive content. The move highlights a fundamental reality of building capable AI agents: models need massive datasets of real human computer behavior, not just text.

# Why It Matters
For founders and team leads thinking about deploying AI agents inside their own organizations, this story makes the training data gap visible. Meta — with billions in AI investment and thousands of engineers — is resorting to keylogging its own workforce to get the kind of real-world computer interaction data that agents need to function usefully. Two implications are direct: first, the "agents do the work" vision that Bosworth describes is the explicit endgame that every major tech company is building toward; second, the data collection required to get there involves a degree of workplace surveillance that will generate employee trust questions any company deploying agents internally will eventually face. The simultaneous layoff context sharpens the stakes: companies are collecting data on how employees work while building systems designed to replace the humans producing that data.
