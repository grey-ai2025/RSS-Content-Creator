# A New $100M+ PAC Is Being Built to Push Trump's AI Agenda Through the 2026 Midterms

**Source URL:** https://www.nytimes.com/2026/03/29/business/trump-artificial-intelligence-pac-midterms.html

**Publication Date:** March 29, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI governance and regulation (#3); Generative AI in enterprise (#7)

## Summary

A new political action committee called Innovation Council Action has been formed with the explicit goal of advancing Trump's AI policy agenda through the 2026 midterm elections. The group plans to spend at least $100 million and will be led by a former administration official. The PAC's formation follows David Sacks' departure as the White House's AI and Crypto Czar, creating a vacuum in the administration's formal AI policy leadership. The move signals that pro-AI deregulation forces are pivoting to legislative strategy — attempting to elect or retain members of Congress who will support lighter-touch AI governance, resist state-level regulation efforts like Colorado's AI law, and keep the federal playing field favorable to industry incumbents. This development sits alongside the White House's recently released AI framework and strong legislative momentum in Congress around AI, making AI governance a defining political fault line heading into the midterms.

## Key Data Points

- Innovation Council Action plans to spend at least $100 million on AI-aligned political messaging and candidate support
- The PAC will be led by a former Trump administration official
- The PAC's formation follows David Sacks' formal departure as White House Special Advisor on AI and Crypto
- Colorado's AI law faces active repeal debate — a key battleground the PAC is expected to weigh in on
- The White House released a long-awaited AI governance framework in the same week, signaling competing regulatory visions
- U.S. Department of Labor simultaneously launched a "Make America AI-Ready" initiative, creating a parallel workforce policy track

## Why It Matters

AI governance is no longer a policy debate contained to tech industry conferences — it is now an explicit electoral campaign issue with nine-figure funding behind it. For enterprise technology leaders, this has direct implications: the regulatory environment for AI in the United States is about to be shaped as much by midterm election outcomes as by agency rulemaking. Companies building AI strategies in 2026 must now factor in the real possibility that the federal governance framework shifts significantly depending on who controls Congress after November. The PAC's presence also signals that Silicon Valley's political strategy for AI has shifted from White House access (via figures like Sacks) to legislative majority-building — a longer-term, more structural approach to shaping the rules of the game.
