# Nvidia Will Spend $26 Billion to Build Open-Weight AI Models

**Source URL:** https://www.wired.com/story/nvidia-investing-26-billion-open-source-models/

**Publication Date:** March 11, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI-native architecture, agentic AI, AI agents in production

## Summary

Nvidia has committed $26 billion over five years to develop open-weight AI models, according to a 2025 financial filing confirmed by company executives to Wired. The move represents a major strategic shift: Nvidia is evolving from a chipmaker with a software stack into a frontier AI lab capable of competing with OpenAI and DeepSeek. Simultaneously, Nvidia released Nemotron 3 Super, its most capable open-weight model to date at 128 billion parameters, which the company claims outperforms GPT-OSS and ranks first on PinchBench — a new benchmark specifically testing AI agents' ability to control OpenClaw. The investment is strategically designed to strengthen Nvidia's position at the center of global AI infrastructure: its models are tuned to its own hardware, incentivizing customers to buy Nvidia chips to run Nvidia models. The move also serves a geopolitical purpose — creating a high-quality US-made alternative to widely-adopted Chinese open models from DeepSeek, Alibaba, and others that many Western startups currently build on.

## Key Data Points

- $26 billion committed over five years to open-weight AI model development (per SEC filing, confirmed by Nvidia executives)
- Nemotron 3 Super: 128 billion parameters, Nvidia's largest open-weight model to date
- Scores 37 on the Artificial Intelligence Index vs. GPT-OSS's 33; ranked #1 on PinchBench (OpenClaw agent control benchmark)
- Nvidia also completed pretraining a 550-billion-parameter model (not yet released publicly)
- Bryan Catanzaro, VP of applied deep learning research: "Nvidia is taking open model development much more seriously"
- Models are tuned to Nvidia hardware — a strategic lock-in mechanism
- Chinese open models (DeepSeek, Alibaba Qwen, Moonshot AI, MiniMax) currently dominate developer adoption in the West
- A new DeepSeek model is rumored to have been trained entirely on Huawei chips — potentially the catalyst for US government funding of open models
- Nathan Lambert (Allen Institute for AI): "The US government should also fund open models"

## Why It Matters

Nvidia building frontier AI models changes the competitive map. This is not just about open source as a philosophy — it is about who controls the stack that the world's AI ecosystem runs on. If open-weight Chinese models dominate developer adoption globally, Nvidia's chip hegemony becomes fragile over time. By building US-made open models tuned to its hardware, Nvidia is hedging its infrastructure position and giving Western developers a credible domestic alternative. For enterprise technology leaders, Nemotron 3 Super's top ranking on the OpenClaw agent benchmark signals that Nvidia is positioning itself as the infrastructure layer for agentic AI — not just for training, but for inference and agent deployment.
