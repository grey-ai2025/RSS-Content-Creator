# Google Commits Up to $40 Billion in Anthropic — The Biggest AI Bet Yet

**Source URL:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/24/google-to-invest-up-to-40b-in-anthropic-in-cash-and-compute/

**Publication Date:** April 24, 2026

**Keyword Match:** Anthropic Mythos model, AI chip supply constraints, Agentic AI and workflow automation

**Summary:**
Google is committing up to $40 billion to Anthropic in a tiered deal — $10 billion immediately at a $350 billion valuation, with a further $30 billion contingent on performance targets. The investment combines cash and compute: Google Cloud is providing 5 gigawatts of computing capacity over five years, on top of an earlier agreement for 3.5 additional gigawatts of TPU-based capacity beginning in 2027. The deal follows Anthropic's controversial limited release of Mythos, its most powerful model to date, and comes just days after Anthropic secured a separate $5 billion commitment from Amazon. The move signals that the AI compute race has reached a scale where even trillion-dollar companies are locking in infrastructure years in advance to avoid being left behind.

**Key Data Points:**
- Google is investing up to $40B in Anthropic — $10B immediate, $30B contingent on milestones
- Valuation set at $350 billion, making Anthropic one of the most valuable private companies in the world
- Google Cloud provides 5 gigawatts of compute over 5 years, plus 3.5 GW of TPU capacity starting 2027
- Anthropic recently secured a separate $5B from Amazon and a CoreWeave infrastructure deal
- The investment follows Anthropic's limited release of Mythos, its frontier cybersecurity-focused model
- Mythos was already accessed by unauthorized users despite restricted distribution

**Why It Matters:**
For founders and SMB operators, this deal marks a turning point: the AI tools they rely on — Claude, Anthropic's APIs — are now backed by a compute war that will define which models survive and improve. A $40B commitment means Anthropic is not going anywhere, and future Claude models will be dramatically more capable as compute scales. It also signals that the cost of building frontier AI is diverging sharply from what most companies can afford independently — meaning cloud-delivered AI becomes more critical, not less, for smaller teams trying to compete with enterprise-scale intelligence.
