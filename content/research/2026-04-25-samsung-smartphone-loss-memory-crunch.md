# Samsung Faces First-Ever Smartphone Loss as AI Devours the World's Memory

**Source URL:** https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/04/samsung-may-be-bracing-for-first-ever-annual-loss-in-smartphone-business/

**Publication Date:** April 24, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI chip supply constraints, Apple AI hardware roadmap

**Summary:**
Samsung's mobile experience division head TM Roh has warned company leadership that 2026 could mark the first net loss on smartphones in Samsung's history — not because of weak sales, but because the AI infrastructure race is consuming the world's DRAM and NAND memory supply. The AI era has roughly doubled the cost of memory and storage components used in phones, with RAM set to account for more than a third of the total cost of building a budget phone by mid-2026. Critically, Samsung's semiconductor division is simultaneously smashing records, earning an estimated $38 billion in profit in Q1 2026 — more than seven times its Q1 2025 net — because AI data centers are the ones buying all that memory. The crisis is structural: DRAM production in 2027 is projected to fall 40% short of expected demand even with best-case production increases.

**Key Data Points:**
- Samsung MX head TM Roh warned internally that Samsung could post its first-ever net loss on smartphones in 2026
- AI has roughly doubled the cost of memory and storage components used in smartphones
- RAM will account for more than a third of the cost of building a budget phone by mid-2026 (Counterpoint Research)
- Samsung Semiconductor earned ~$38B profit in Q1 2026 — over 7x its Q1 2025 net
- Nvidia's Vera AI CPU (replacing Grace) will have up to 1.5 TB of LPDDR5x memory — one server rack consumes enough RAM for 4,600 Galaxy S26 Ultra phones
- DRAM production in 2027 projected to fall 40% short of expected demand (Nikkei Asia)
- Motorola raised budget phone prices by up to 50% due to memory shortage
- Samsung raised Galaxy Z Flip 7 and Z Fold 7 prices by $80; some tablets by $100

**Why It Matters:**
This story matters beyond Samsung. For founders and team leads managing hardware budgets — whether buying phones for staff, laptops, or servers — the AI memory crunch is now inflating costs across all computing categories. Budget phones are no longer budget. Laptops are more expensive. And the supply squeeze is set to run through at least 2027. The underlying cause is a structural shift: the biggest buyers of memory are no longer consumer electronics companies but AI data center operators. Small businesses are competing with trillion-dollar infrastructure builds for the same chips, and losing.
