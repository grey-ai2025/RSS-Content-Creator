## Summary

OpenAI's chief revenue officer Denise Dresser sent a four-page internal memo to employees on April 13, viewed in full by The Verge, outlining the company's enterprise strategy and its direct competitive framing against Anthropic. The memo's central thesis is that the AI market has shifted from model capability to platform lock-in: OpenAI is no longer competing on raw intelligence but on multi-product adoption, agent infrastructure, and deployment support. Dresser explicitly attacks Anthropic's compute strategy, accuses the company of inflating its revenue run rate by $8 billion using gross-up accounting, and frames the competitive dynamic as "a platform war" where "single-product companies" lose. The memo also discloses a strategic Amazon partnership through the "Amazon Stateful Runtime Environment" designed to move OpenAI beyond stateless model access into persistent, memory-enabled agents running inside enterprise AWS environments. This is the clearest articulation yet of OpenAI's vision of becoming "operating infrastructure" — not a product vendor.

## Key Data Points

- **"The market is as competitive as I have ever seen it"** — Denise Dresser, OpenAI CRO, April 13 memo
- OpenAI claims Anthropic's stated **$30B run rate is inflated by ~$8 billion** due to accounting treatment that grosses up rev share with Amazon and Google
- **Multi-year, multi-product, nine-figure enterprise deals** are rising at OpenAI; Dresser cites this as validation of the platform strategy
- OpenAI's new model internally codenamed **"Spud"** cited as the intelligence foundation — described as "our smartest model yet" with stronger reasoning and more reliable production output
- **Amazon Stateful Runtime Environment** partnership: enables memory, context, and continuity across interactions — moving toward long-running, multi-step agents inside AWS environments
- OpenAI's five strategic priorities: (1) Win model layer for work, (2) Win agent platform layer with "Frontier," (3) Expand via Amazon, (4) Sell full AI-native stack, (5) Own deployment via "DeployCo"
- **"Agents are the market now"** — Dresser writes "The market has moved from prompts to agents. That shift is a massive opportunity for us."
- On Anthropic: **"Their story is built on fear, restriction, and the idea that a small group of elites should control AI"** — OpenAI positioning itself as "democratic AI" vs. Anthropic's "elite access" model
- **"30% of apps on Vercel already come from agents"** (referenced in context — separate Vercel data cited same day at HumanX conference confirming the agent-creation trend OpenAI is targeting)
- OpenAI describes its current biggest constraint as **"not demand — it is capacity"**, with talent as a top Q2 priority

## Why It Matters

For any founder, operator, or professional who uses or buys AI tools, the memo reframes the entire decision. This isn't a race to pick the best model — it's a race to pick the winning platform before your tools, data, and workflows are locked into one ecosystem. Dresser's framing is explicit: OpenAI wants multi-product adoption to make switching costs prohibitive. The announcement of the Amazon Stateful Runtime Environment is particularly significant for SMB operators — it means OpenAI's agents will run persistently inside AWS environments with memory and context, which is fundamentally different from the stateless API interactions most businesses currently use. Any founder or team lead evaluating AI vendor strategy in Q2 2026 is now choosing not just a model but an operating infrastructure. The Anthropic run rate dispute also matters: if OpenAI's analysis is correct, Anthropic's enterprise growth story — the primary reason many organizations chose Claude — is materially overstated. The memo is the clearest possible signal that 2026's AI decisions are about moats, not benchmarks.
