# 15% of Americans Say They'd Be Willing to Work for an AI Boss, According to New Poll

**Source URL:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/30/ai-work-boss-supervisor-us-quinnipiac-poll/

**Publication Date:** March 30, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI workforce readiness; AI and jobs; agentic AI reliability

## Summary

A Quinnipiac University poll of 1,397 American adults conducted March 19-23, 2026 finds that only 15% of Americans would accept a job where their direct supervisor is an AI program that assigns tasks and sets schedules. The finding lands amid a broader wave of polling data showing that AI adoption is rising while public trust is declining. The same Quinnipiac research series found 7 in 10 Americans believe AI will cut jobs, with Gen Z the most pessimistic about the technology's impact on employment. The data collectively paints a picture of a workforce that is adopting AI tools under pressure while remaining deeply skeptical about AI authority over human workers.

## Key Data Points

- 15% of Americans say they'd be willing to work for an AI program as direct supervisor
- 85% of Americans are therefore unwilling to report to an AI boss — a striking majority resistance
- The poll surveyed 1,397 American adults between March 19-23, 2026 (Quinnipiac University)
- A separate question in the Quinnipiac series found 7 in 10 Americans believe AI will reduce jobs
- Gen Z respondents reported the highest levels of pessimism about AI's impact on the workforce
- The poll sits alongside rising AI adoption rates — creating a paradox where usage grows while acceptance of AI authority does not

## Why It Matters

For business leaders deploying AI agents and automation in the workplace, this data is a critical reality check. Introducing AI into supervisory or evaluative roles — even indirectly through algorithmic task assignment or scheduling — is likely to face significant worker resistance. At the same time, the 15% willing minority suggests a real segment of the workforce, likely in tech-forward roles, that is open to AI management. The generational divide, with Gen Z most skeptical, challenges the assumption that younger workers will naturally embrace AI authority. Organizations building agentic systems that interact with or direct human workers need to account for this trust gap now, not after deployment.
