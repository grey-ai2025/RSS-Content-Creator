# Databricks Launches Lakewatch and Acquires Two Startups to Build AI-Native Security Platform

**Source URL:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/24/databricks-buys-two-startups-lakewatch-antimatter-siftd-ai-security/

**Publication Date:** March 24, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI governance and security, agentic AI, AI agents, generative AI ROI

## Summary

Databricks has entered the enterprise security market with a new product called Lakewatch — a Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) platform that uses AI agents powered by Anthropic's Claude to detect, investigate, and respond to threats across the data infrastructure Databricks already manages for thousands of enterprises. To build it, the company made two acquisitions: Antimatter, a security startup focused on building a "data control plane" for safe enterprise agent deployment, and SiftD.ai, a two-person outfit that had only launched its interactive human-agent notebook product in November 2025. Both acquisitions were acqui-hires funded by Databricks' $5 billion war chest from its February close. The move is notable because it signals that Databricks — which already sits at the center of enterprise data pipelines — is now extending its surface area to include the security layer, creating a vertically integrated platform where data storage, AI inference, and threat monitoring coexist.

## Key Data Points

- Lakewatch performs classic SIEM tasks (threat detection, investigation) using AI agents powered by Anthropic's Claude
- Databricks closed a $5 billion funding round last month, providing capital for aggressive M&A
- Antimatter raised $12 million led by New Enterprise Associates in 2022; was working on a "data control plane" to let enterprises deploy agents securely while protecting sensitive data
- SiftD.ai had only launched its product in November 2025 — an interactive notebook for human-agent collaboration; effectively a sub-5-person team
- SiftD's co-founder Steve Zhang was previously chief scientist at Splunk, creator of the Search Processing Language
- Antimatter had fewer than 50 employees; SiftD was even smaller — both were acqui-hires
- Andrew Krioukov (Antimatter founder) is now leading the Lakewatch team inside Databricks
- Databricks confirmed it is "always looking to what's next" and actively pursuing further acquisitions
- Connection to keywords: Databricks released its Agentic AI Security Framework (DASF v3.0) earlier this week, and Lakewatch is the commercial product built on top of it

## Why It Matters

Databricks' move into AI-native security is the clearest signal yet that the next frontier in enterprise AI governance is not policy documents or model audits — it is infrastructure. By building a SIEM that runs on the same data layer enterprises use for analytics and AI training, Databricks is betting that security and AI operations will converge. For enterprise technology leaders, this matters because it illustrates where the agentic AI security conversation is heading: away from perimeter defenses and toward agents that monitor other agents from inside the data stack. The acquisitions of Antimatter and SiftD also reveal that Databricks is moving to own the full stack of what it calls "agentic AI security" — data, inference, and now threat detection — a vertical integration play that will put it in direct competition with legacy SIEM providers like Splunk (where one of Lakewatch's key engineers previously worked).
