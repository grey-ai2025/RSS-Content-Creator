# Illinois Passes America's Strongest AI Safety Bill — Third-Party Audits Now Required

**Source URL:** https://www.wired.com/story/illinois-pass-major-ai-safety-law-pritzker/

**Publication Date:** May 28, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI regulation / AI safety legislation (Trending Topic #3); AI governance / AI agent security (Trending Topic #7)

---

## Summary

The Illinois House passed SB 315, which Governor JB Pritzker has pledged to sign, creating the first US law requiring frontier AI companies (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind) to have their safety practices independently audited by third parties — rather than self-certifying compliance. The bill goes further than California and New York's existing AI laws, which require companies to publish safety incident reports but allow them to "grade their own homework." Under SB 315, AI labs must adhere to their own stated safety commitments and have that adherence verified by an independent auditor — likely the Big Four accounting firms or specialized AI evaluation organizations. Notably, both OpenAI and Anthropic publicly endorsed the bill. The legislation passed 110-0 and comes as Congress has failed to pass any meaningful federal AI safety law, leaving states as the primary regulatory battleground.

---

## Key Data Points

- **Illinois SB 315** passed the House **110-0** — unanimous, bipartisan vote
- Governor Pritzker committed to **signing the bill**; it becomes law upon signature
- Requires **independent third-party safety audits** for frontier AI labs — the first such requirement in the US
- Goes beyond California and New York laws (which require self-reported safety incident disclosures)
- **OpenAI and Anthropic both endorsed** SB 315; trade groups representing Google, Apple, Amazon, and a16z opposed it
- Likely auditors: Big Four accounting firms (Deloitte, EY, KPMG, PwC) or AI Evaluator Forum members (METR, Transluce, Averi)
- Chamber of Progress (Google/Apple/Amazon affiliate) objected: law "would force companies to expose sensitive systems to untested auditors"
- State rep Daniel Didech: "Laws like this create a world where it's more likely for the federal government to pass something"
- Backdrop: Trump canceled a planned AI safety executive order last week, calling it an innovation "blocker"
- Congress has passed **no meaningful federal AI safety legislation** to date

## Why It Matters

For founders and SMB operators building AI-powered products or services in Illinois — or selling to Illinois-based enterprises — this law creates a new compliance tier that will cascade down the value chain. If frontier model providers must now pass third-party audits, they will embed those audit requirements into their API terms and enterprise agreements. That means any business building on Claude, GPT, or Gemini will eventually face questions from customers and partners about whether their AI usage meets the safety standards their model provider is now legally required to document. More immediately, this is the clearest signal yet that state-level AI compliance is not a future concern — it is arriving now, and businesses that haven't mapped their AI usage to safety and governance frameworks are behind. The 110-0 vote suggests this is not a partisan edge case; it is the emerging consensus about what responsible AI deployment looks like.
