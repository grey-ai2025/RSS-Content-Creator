## Summary

OpenAI has acquired Hiro Finance, an AI-powered personal financial planning startup backed by Ribbit Capital, General Catalyst, and Restive. The deal, announced April 14, is structured as an acquihire — Hiro will shut down its consumer product on April 20 and delete all user data by May 13, with founder Ethan Bloch and approximately 10 employees joining OpenAI. Hiro's product allowed users to input their financial data (salary, debts, monthly costs) and model what-if scenarios to support financial decisions; it was specifically trained to nail financial math with a verification option. This is not OpenAI's first financial acquisition — the company already markets ChatGPT as a tool for business finance teams. Read in context with OpenAI's leaked internal memo (same day, The Verge), which explicitly outlines a "full AI-native stack" strategy and positions ChatGPT as the "front door for knowledge work," the Hiro acquisition signals that financial planning is becoming a native ChatGPT capability — not a third-party integration. The acquisition also carries a competitive subtext: Bloch had built his own OpenClaw-based trading agent called "RoboBuffett," and the acquihire may partly be an effort to attract enterprise users who currently prefer Claude for financial agentic workflows.

- Hiro will **shut down April 20**; all user data deleted by **May 13**
- Founder Ethan Bloch previously sold **Digit** (digital savings bank) to Oportun for **$230 million** in 2021 — this is his third successful exit
- Bloch built **RoboBuffett** — his own OpenClaw-based auto-trading AI agent — signaling deep expertise in agentic financial workflows
- OpenAI already markets **ChatGPT for business finance teams** and has a prior undisclosed financial app acquisition
- Context: OpenAI's leaked Q2 memo (same day) states its strategy is to be "a platform company with multiple entry points and one integrated enterprise offering" — financial planning is a clear new entry point

## Why It Matters

For any founder, operator, or professional who uses AI for financial planning, forecasting, or business modeling, this acquisition is a direct signal: ChatGPT is moving from general-purpose assistant to a purpose-built financial intelligence layer. Hiro's specific capability — modeling financial scenarios with verified math — is exactly the gap that has prevented most small business operators from trusting AI with finance work. If OpenAI ships this as a native ChatGPT capability, the competitive dynamic shifts for every fintech app, financial planning tool, and CFO-as-a-service offering in the market. The Ribbit/General Catalyst backing also tells the story: this wasn't a failed startup — it was a validated fintech product acquired for its talent and IP before it could grow into a standalone competitor. For founders running sub-50-person companies who currently rely on spreadsheets, a CFO consultant, or a basic accounting tool for financial modeling, this is a preview of what ChatGPT may offer natively by end of 2026.
