# Anthropic's Claude AI Can Now Respond With Charts, Diagrams, and Other Visuals

**Source URL:** https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/893625/anthropic-claude-ai-charts-diagrams

**Publication Date:** March 12, 2026

**Keyword Match:** Claude visual output, agentic AI

## Summary

Anthropic has launched a new capability for Claude that allows the AI to generate custom charts, diagrams, and interactive visualizations inline during a conversation — not in a side panel, but directly in the chat thread. Claude determines on its own whether a visual would be useful based on conversational context, then generates it automatically; users can also request visuals explicitly. One example shows Claude producing an interactive periodic table when discussing chemistry; another shows structural diagrams for an architecture question. The visuals are dynamic: users can click inside them for additional information and ask Claude to revise them. The feature rolls out to all Claude users immediately and is enabled by default. The launch puts Claude directly in competition with ChatGPT and Gemini, both of which launched similar interactive visualization features this week.

## Key Data Points

- Rolling out to all Claude users as of March 12, 2026; on by default
- Visuals appear inline in conversation, not in the Artifacts side panel
- Unlike Artifacts (persistent, shareable), inline visuals are conversation-scoped and update as the chat progresses
- Interactive: users can click elements inside visuals for more information
- Example use cases: periodic table, structural/architectural diagrams, data charts
- Users can ask Claude to modify visuals after generation
- OpenAI launched a similar math/science visualization feature in ChatGPT the same week
- Google Gemini also launched interactive educational images the same week — a three-way simultaneous move by the major AI labs

## Why It Matters

AI is no longer just a text layer — it is becoming the presentation layer. Claude generating inline interactive visuals signals that the major AI models are now competing on data storytelling, not just language quality. For professionals who use AI to analyze data, explain concepts, or prepare presentations, this changes the workflow. The fact that OpenAI and Google launched near-identical features in the same week is not coincidence — it suggests a shared belief among AI labs that visual output is the next major interface battleground. For enterprise buyers evaluating AI tools, visual reasoning capability is now a selection criterion.
