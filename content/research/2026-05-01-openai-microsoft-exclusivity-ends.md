# Here's How the New Microsoft and OpenAI Deal Breaks Down

**Source URL:** https://www.theverge.com/tech/921210/microsoft-openai-partnership-divorce-notepad

**Publication Date:** April 30, 2026

**Keyword Match:** OpenAI ends Microsoft exclusivity; AI agents / agentic AI; $700 billion AI infrastructure capex

---

## Summary

Microsoft and OpenAI have restructured their partnership in what amounts to an amicable but meaningful separation. The core change: OpenAI models are no longer exclusive to Microsoft Azure. Businesses can now access OpenAI models through Amazon Bedrock and Google Cloud — not just Azure. This ends the arrangement that had made Azure the sole enterprise channel for running OpenAI models in production. The deal was confirmed alongside OpenAI's Q1 revenue miss and reshuffles how the largest AI vendor in the world distributes its products.

The restructuring also gives OpenAI more autonomy to pursue its own infrastructure and cloud relationships as it prepares for an anticipated IPO. Microsoft retains a revenue share arrangement and preferred cloud partner status for some workloads, but no longer has exclusivity over where OpenAI's models run. For the enterprise cloud market, the implication is clear: the thesis that "Microsoft owns OpenAI's distribution" is no longer operative.

## Key Data Points

- OpenAI models are now available on Amazon Bedrock and Google Cloud, ending Azure-only exclusivity
- Microsoft retains a revenue share arrangement and preferred partner status but loses exclusivity
- OpenAI reported Q1 revenue below internal targets, adding pressure to diversify distribution channels
- OpenAI is preparing for a potential IPO, making broader distribution strategically important for growth narratives
- The deal reflects ongoing tension over AI infrastructure: OpenAI has strained against relying solely on Microsoft's compute capacity
- Amazon's AWS and Google Cloud both confirmed OpenAI model availability as part of their enterprise AI offerings
- The AI Weekly newsletter framed it as: "The 'Microsoft owns OpenAI's distribution' thesis is dead — and Satya didn't even get a press release."

---

## Why It Matters

This is a direct vendor-choice story for any founder or team lead who uses or is evaluating OpenAI's models. Until this week, running OpenAI in production at scale effectively meant committing to Azure. That constraint is now gone. Teams already on AWS or Google Cloud can now access OpenAI models natively through those platforms — without an Azure dependency. For operators who chose alternatives like Anthropic's Claude or Google's Gemini specifically to avoid Azure lock-in, this changes the calculus. It also signals that the AI model market is moving toward multi-cloud availability, which puts more bargaining power in the hands of businesses: they can pick their preferred cloud vendor and still access frontier models. The longer-term implication is pricing competition — when OpenAI competes for AWS and GCP workloads alongside Anthropic and Google's own models, inference costs are more likely to decrease than if Azure had maintained monopoly distribution.
