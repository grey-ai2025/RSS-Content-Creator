## Summary
Sierra, the enterprise AI agent company founded by Bret Taylor (former Salesforce co-CEO and OpenAI board chairman), raised $950 million from Tiger Global and GV, pushing its valuation above $15 billion and total capital to over $1 billion. Sierra builds autonomous agents that handle high-stakes enterprise customer interactions — mortgage refinancing, insurance claims, returns processing, and fundraising campaigns. The company's ARR grew from $100 million in November 2025 to $150 million by February 2026, a 50% revenue jump in three months. Sierra's newest product, Ghostwriter, lets users describe a business need in plain language and the system autonomously creates and deploys specialized agents. The raise signals that investors believe agentic AI for enterprise customer interactions is no longer a bet — it is a proven market.

## Key Data Points
- $950M raised from Tiger Global and GV; total capital now exceeds $1 billion
- Post-money valuation: above $15 billion
- ARR: $100M in November 2025, growing to $150M by February 2026 — 50% growth in 3 months
- Customer base: grown from 4 design partners to serving more than 40% of the Fortune 50
- Founded by Bret Taylor (former Salesforce co-CEO, former OpenAI board chairman)
- Ghostwriter product: plain-language agent creation and deployment, "agent as a service"
- Use cases include mortgage refinancing, insurance claims processing, returns management, and nonprofit fundraising campaigns
- Uber was cited as an early customer that quickly exhausted AI deployment budgets, underscoring the upfront investment cost

## Why It Matters
For founders and SMB operators, Sierra's trajectory sets the benchmark for what "enterprise-grade AI agents" looks like — and how fast that bar is moving. A company growing ARR 50% in three months, backed by $1B, serving 40%+ of Fortune 50 is establishing the competitive standard for autonomous customer interaction. Any SMB building customer service, sales, or operations workflows now competes (or integrates) with systems capitalized at this scale. The Ghostwriter product is the most relevant signal for smaller operators: the trend is toward plain-language agent creation, meaning the barrier to deploying your own agents is dropping — but so is the time window before competitors do the same.
