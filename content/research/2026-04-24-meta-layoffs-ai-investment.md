## Summary
Meta announced it will lay off approximately 10% of its global workforce — roughly 8,000 people — in May 2026, while simultaneously closing around 6,000 open roles. The cuts are explicitly framed as an offset to Meta's accelerating AI investment: the company plans to spend $115 billion to $135 billion in capital expenditures in 2026, nearly double the $72.22 billion spent in 2025. Chief People Officer Janelle Gale described the move as "running the company more efficiently to allow us to offset the other investments we're making." The layoffs affect Meta at an overall company level following earlier cuts in 2026 to recruiting, social media, sales, and Reality Labs teams. Reuters has reported that further cuts are planned for the second half of 2026.

## Key Data Points
- ~8,000 employees cut (approximately 10% of Meta's global workforce)
- ~6,000 additional open roles being closed
- Affected employees notified May 20, 2026
- Meta's 2026 capital expenditure plan: $115 billion–$135 billion (up from $72.22 billion in 2025 — a near-doubling)
- Additional cuts planned for H2 2026 per Reuters
- Earlier 2026 cuts: hundreds in recruiting/social media/sales, ~10% of Reality Labs division
- March 2026 Reuters report: Meta was considering laying off 20% "or more" of the company
- Janelle Gale (Chief People Officer): "This is not an easy tradeoff and it will mean letting go of people who have made meaningful contributions."
- Meta's AI investment is framed as supporting "Meta Superintelligence Labs efforts and core business"
- Meta is simultaneously building a $1 billion+ data center in Tulsa, Oklahoma

## Why It Matters
Meta's decision is the clearest public illustration of the CFO math now playing out inside large companies: human headcount is being directly converted into AI infrastructure spending. The 8,000 jobs cut aren't a reaction to poor performance — they're a deliberate reallocation of capital toward AI at a scale that dwarfs what any SMB can match. For founders and team leads, the signal is direct: the largest companies in the world are running the same "cost of human vs. cost of AI" calculation that every 10-50 person company is beginning to face — and they're making the call publicly. The question isn't whether to have this conversation; it's whether you're having it proactively or reactively.
