# EU Parliament Votes to Delay AI Act Deadlines, Backs Ban on Nudify Apps

**Source URL:** https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/901315/eu-ai-act-delays-ban-nudify-apps

**Publication Date:** March 26, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI governance and accountability, AI identity and access security

## Summary

The European Parliament has voted by a large majority to delay key compliance deadlines under the EU AI Act — the bloc's flagship AI regulation — while simultaneously backing proposals to ban nudify apps. High-risk AI systems, those posing a "serious risk" to health, safety, or fundamental rights, will now face a compliance deadline pushed back to December 2027; systems under sector-specific rules such as medical devices or toys would have until August 2028. Rules requiring watermarking of AI-generated content, originally due in August 2026, are now proposed to be delayed until November 2026. The nudify app ban follows widespread European outrage over Grok's sexualized deepfakes earlier this year. The vote creates additional uncertainty for businesses operating in Europe, as the parliament cannot unilaterally change European law — it must now negotiate the final text with the European Council, making it unclear whether changes can be legally implemented before the original August deadline.

## Key Data Points

- High-risk AI system compliance deadline: pushed from August 2026 to December 2027 — a 16-month delay
- Sector-specific AI systems (medical devices, toys, etc.): deadline proposed at August 2028
- AI content watermarking deadline: delayed from August 2026 to November 2026
- Vote passed by a "large majority" in the European Parliament
- Nudify app ban backed in the same vote; no technical details yet on implementation
- Ban exempts AI systems with "effective safety measures preventing users from creating such images"
- EU has previously missed its own guidance publication deadlines, adding to business uncertainty
- Parliament must still negotiate with the European Council (27 member states) before changes become law
- Background: EU launched investigation into X/Grok over sexualized deepfakes earlier this year

## Why It Matters

The EU AI Act delay is a significant signal for every enterprise with European operations: the world's most comprehensive AI regulatory framework is running behind schedule, and the goalposts are moving again. For compliance teams and AI product leaders, this buys more time but does not reduce the ultimate compliance burden — it only defers it. The simultaneous nudify app ban vote reveals the EU's political priorities: while governance timelines slip on high-risk systems, explicit harms like non-consensual deepfakes are being targeted with urgency. For professional audiences tracking AI governance globally, this is a reminder that the regulatory environment remains in flux and that organizations that have built their AI governance roadmaps around August 2026 dates now need to revise them. The EU's inconsistency also hands ammunition to critics who argue the AI Act is too complex to implement on any realistic schedule.
