## Summary
At Google Cloud Next '26, Google announced the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform — the first major cloud provider to make agentic AI governance a native product feature rather than an add-on. The platform assigns each agent a cryptographic identity and deploys an Agent Gateway to oversee all agent-to-data interactions, centralizing control over what agents can access and do. Despite this, a new OutSystems survey of 1,879 IT leaders (April 2026) reveals a massive confidence-vs-reality gap: 97% of organizations are exploring agentic AI strategies and 49% describe themselves as "advanced or expert," but only 36% have centralized governance and just 12% use a centralized platform to manage AI sprawl. Gartner data makes the production picture even starker: only 17% of organizations have actually deployed agents, and of those, only 11-14% of pilots reach genuine production scale. Gartner also forecasts that 40%+ of agentic AI projects could face cancellation by 2027 due to unclear value and weak governance. The article frames Google's move as a signal to the market that governance infrastructure needs to be a first-class concern — not an afterthought bolted on after deployment.

## Why It Matters
For any founder or operations lead who has started deploying AI agents — or is being pressured to — this data should recalibrate expectations. Nine out of ten organizations that start an agent pilot never reach production. The failure mode isn't the model — it's the absence of governance infrastructure that can answer: who controls this agent, what can it access, and how do we stop it if it goes wrong? Google's announcement signals that serious agent deployment requires enterprise-grade governance tooling from day one, not a bolt-on after the fact. For SMBs operating without an IT security team, this maps directly to a build-vs-buy decision: building agents on platforms that provide native governance (like Google's new platform) rather than standing up raw API agents with no oversight layer.
