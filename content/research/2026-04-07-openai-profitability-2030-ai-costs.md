## Summary
New financial disclosures reveal that OpenAI does not expect to reach profitability until at least 2030, with surging compute costs outpacing rapid revenue growth. The same reporting confirmed that Anthropic is in a similar financial position, burning through capital at scale. The revelations land alongside AI Weekly's report that $6 billion in OpenAI shares sat unsold on secondary markets the same week, and that the company's COO Brad Lightcap was simultaneously reshuffled into a "special projects" role. Taken together, the picture that emerges is of two leading AI labs locked into a capital-intensive race where the economics of frontier model development remain deeply unresolved — even at massive scale and revenue. For enterprise buyers who have built AI-first strategies around these vendors, the profitability timeline carries direct implications for pricing, continuity, and long-term vendor stability.

## Key Data Points
- OpenAI projects profitability no earlier than 2030 — even as it scales revenue aggressively
- Anthropic is also on a long road to profitability, burning substantial capital alongside rapid growth
- AI Weekly reported $6 billion in OpenAI shares sat unsold on secondary markets during the same week
- OpenAI COO Brad Lightcap reshuffled into "special projects" role simultaneously with these disclosures
- OpenAI's AGI deployment CEO Fidji Simo went on medical leave during the same period — significant leadership churn at the top
- Context: global AI companies raised a record $297 billion in Q1 2026 alone (NYT, April 1) — capital is available, but burn rates remain extreme
- OpenAI's Stargate project represents a planned $500 billion infrastructure bet despite no clear path to profitability
- AI Weekly previously noted that OpenAI pulled the plug on Sora, writing off $15 million per day in inference burn, signaling aggressive prioritization trade-offs

## Why It Matters
Enterprises and investors are pricing AI vendor relationships as if the market has already sorted itself out. It has not. The two most prominent frontier AI labs — the ones whose models underpin a growing share of enterprise workflows, agent deployments, and AI-first strategies — remain deeply unprofitable with no near-term resolution. For technology leaders, this is a vendor concentration risk that belongs in the risk register: what happens to your AI-first strategy if your primary vendor restructures, raises prices sharply, or pivots its model offerings to chase profitability? For buyers who have built critical workflows on ChatGPT or Claude, 2030 is not a comfortable distance. These disclosures make the case for architecture decisions that preserve optionality across vendors.
