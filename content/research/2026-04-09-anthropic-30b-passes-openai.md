# Anthropic Hits $30B Revenue Run Rate, Surpassing OpenAI

**Source URL:** https://aiweekly.co/issues/481
**Publication Date:** April 9, 2026
**Keyword Match:** AI infrastructure and cloud spend, AI agents

## Summary

Anthropic's annual revenue run rate has reached $30 billion, overtaking OpenAI's $24 billion — a reversal that few predicted even six months ago. The acceleration was driven entirely by enterprise demand, not consumer product growth. The number of businesses spending $1 million or more annually on Claude more than doubled in under two months, a metric that signals institutional commitment rather than experimentation. Anthropic also locked in 3.5 gigawatts of next-generation TPU compute via Google and Broadcom to sustain this growth, reinforcing that AI infrastructure investment is an enterprise arms race. The shift comes as OpenAI faces internal tension over its IPO timeline, with its CFO reportedly saying the planned 2026 IPO is "too aggressive" after projecting a $14 billion loss.

## Key Data Points

- Anthropic's revenue run rate: $30 billion (as of early April 2026)
- OpenAI's revenue run rate: $24 billion — now second in the enterprise AI market
- Anthropic grew from $9B to $30B run rate in approximately four months
- Number of enterprise customers spending $1M+ annually on Claude: more than doubled in under two months
- Anthropic secured 3.5 gigawatts of next-gen TPU compute via a Google/Broadcom deal
- OpenAI's CFO Sarah Friar reportedly told colleagues the Q4 2026 IPO timeline is "too aggressive"
- OpenAI projects a $14 billion loss and $600 billion in cumulative spending
- OpenAI's CFO has reportedly been excluded from key financial meetings after breaking ranks
- OpenAI closed $122 billion in funding at an $852 billion post-money valuation just over a week ago
- Anthropic's growth is powered by enterprise Claude deployments, not consumer ChatGPT-style products

## Why It Matters

The revenue reversal between Anthropic and OpenAI is the single most consequential competitive shift in enterprise AI this year. For technology leaders, procurement teams, and investors, the message is direct: the company that built its brand on safety and governance is now the market leader by revenue — and it got there through enterprise adoption of AI agents and Claude integrations, not by winning the consumer chat war. For executives currently evaluating or renegotiating AI vendor relationships, this data point changes the reference architecture. The doubling of million-dollar enterprise customers in under two months is the fastest indicator yet that agentic AI is moving from pilot to production inside large organizations. OpenAI's IPO uncertainty adds further urgency: the AI market's financial structure is more volatile than its valuations suggest, and vendor dependency decisions made today will carry long-term weight.
