# AI Hype vs. Profit: The Missing Step Nobody Has a Plan For

**Title:** The missing step between hype and profit

**Source URL:** https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/04/27/1136456/the-missing-step-between-hype-and-profit/

**Publication Date:** April 27, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI agents cost vs. human labor; agentic AI enterprise adoption

**Summary:** MIT Technology Review argues that the AI industry has mastered Steps 1 and 3 — building the technology and promising transformation — but has no credible plan for Step 2: profitable real-world deployment. Using the South Park "underpants gnomes" analogy, the piece shows that companies are racing to deploy AI agents before understanding how to restructure workflows around them. A Mercor study tested AI agents from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind on 480 workplace tasks and found that every agent failed to complete most of its assigned duties. The article calls for better real-world evaluation methods, transparency from model makers about actual capabilities, and deep workflow redesign — not just bolting AI onto existing processes.

**Key Data Points:**
- A Mercor study tested AI agents across 480 workplace tasks; every agent tested (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind) failed to complete most of its assigned duties
- Anthropic's own job impact research is based on task-level performance assessments, not actual workplace deployment results
- LLMs lack the strategic judgment needed to replace knowledge workers end-to-end; they fail when integrated into existing workflows without redesign
- The gap between AI benchmark performance and real-world economic output is the defining business risk of 2026
- Successful AI adoption requires "tearing up and rebuilding existing workflows" around the technology — not point-in-place substitution

**Why It Matters:** Every founder and team lead evaluating AI investments needs to understand this gap before signing contracts or staffing down. The ROI math breaks if you deploy AI agents into unchanged workflows. This piece provides the corrective lens: the question isn't "can AI do this task in a demo?" — it's "does our workflow let AI operate without constant human rescue?" That is a decision that changes hiring plans, vendor selection, and implementation timelines for any 10–50 person company.

**Related Themes:** AI ROI, agentic AI deployment, enterprise AI adoption failure modes, AI agents cost vs. human labor, workflow transformation
