# Senator Warner Proposes Taxing Data Centers to Fund AI Job Loss Transition

**Source URL:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/26/a-pound-of-flesh-from-data-centers-one-senators-answer-to-ai-job-losses/

**Publication Date:** March 26, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI data center energy and regulation, generative AI productivity, future of work

## Summary

Senator Mark Warner (D-VA) has proposed taxing data centers to generate revenue specifically directed toward helping workers displaced by AI — a policy idea gaining urgency as public anger toward both AI and data center construction intensifies. Speaking at the Axios AI Summit in Washington, Warner described growing evidence of real-world AI displacement: a venture capitalist told him he is writing software investments down to zero partly due to Claude's capabilities, and a major law firm told him it is no longer hiring first-year associates because AI handles much of that work. Unlike colleagues Bernie Sanders and Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, who proposed a full data center moratorium the same day, Warner argues against stopping construction entirely — framing a moratorium as ceding ground to China — and instead wants to extract a "pound of flesh" from the industry to fund retraining programs, nursing workforce development, and AI upskilling. The proposal comes as AI holds a lower public approval rating than ICE in a recent NBC News poll, and as Virginia debates repealing $2 billion per year in data center tax breaks.

## Key Data Points

- Entry-level job postings in the U.S. have declined 35% since 2023, per Revelio Labs data cited in the article
- A VC told Warner he is writing software startup investments "down to zero" in part due to Anthropic Claude's capabilities
- A major law firm told Warner it has stopped hiring first-year associates because AI now handles much of the work
- NBC News poll: 46% of registered voters view AI negatively vs. 26% positively — lower public approval than ICE
- Virginia's data center tax breaks cost the state and localities nearly $2 billion per year in lost revenue
- Henrico County, Virginia already used data center tax revenue to launch an affordable housing project
- Sanders/AOC introduced a companion data center moratorium bill the same day; Warner explicitly opposes it
- Warner's tax proposal has not yet been introduced as formal legislation as of March 26

## Why It Matters

The Warner proposal reframes the AI infrastructure debate in a way that is likely to gain more policy traction than a moratorium: instead of trying to stop AI, it asks who pays for its social costs. For business leaders and professionals, this signals that data centers — and by extension the enterprises that depend on AI infrastructure — may face new tax liabilities tied explicitly to workforce disruption. The framing also marks a shift in political tone: AI is no longer simply a growth story in Washington; it is a jobs story, and the industry's implicit social contract with workers is now being demanded in writing. The 35% drop in entry-level job postings is the kind of concrete data point that will energize this policy conversation regardless of whether Warner's specific bill advances.
