## Summary

Stanford University's Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence released its 2026 AI Index today — the most comprehensive annual measurement of where AI actually stands. The headline: AI is advancing faster than jobs, regulators, and the benchmarks designed to test it can keep up. AI is now used by more than half of the world's population, adopted faster than the PC or the internet, and embedded in 88% of organizations. But the same report documents that employment for early-career software developers (ages 22–25) has dropped nearly 20% since 2022, a third of organizations expect AI to shrink their workforce in the coming year, and the benchmarks used to evaluate AI progress are so flawed that one popular math benchmark has a 42% error rate. The US and China are now nearly tied on model performance, competing less on capability and more on cost and reliability.

## Key Data Points

- **88% of organizations** now use AI; **4 in 5 university students** use it — adoption has outpaced the personal computer and the internet
- **AI is used by more than half of all people on Earth** — within three years of going mainstream
- Employment for **software developers ages 22–25 has fallen nearly 20%** since 2022 (Stanford Digital Economy Lab, 2025)
- **A third of organizations** expect AI to shrink their workforce in the coming year, particularly in service operations, supply chain, and software engineering (McKinsey 2025)
- AI boosts productivity **14% in customer service** and **26% in software development** — but gains don't appear in tasks requiring judgment
- A popular AI math benchmark has a **42% error rate** — strong benchmark scores no longer reliably predict real-world performance
- SWE-bench Verified (software engineering benchmark) saw top AI scores jump from **~60% in 2024 to nearly 100% in 2025**
- AI data centers globally now draw **29.6 gigawatts** — enough to power all of New York State at peak demand
- **73% of AI experts** say AI will have a positive impact on how people do their jobs; only **23% of American adults** agree — the biggest gap in the survey
- Among all countries, **Americans trust their government least** to regulate AI appropriately
- The EU AI Act's first prohibitions (predictive policing, emotion recognition) took effect; US moved toward deregulation; US states passed a record **150 AI-related bills** in 2025

## Why It Matters

For any founder, manager, or individual contributor, the Stanford report provides the clearest data-grounded answer to "what is actually happening with AI and jobs." The 20% drop in junior software developer employment is the most concrete labor-market signal published to date — and the 33% of organizations planning workforce reductions gives anyone in a service, operations, or coding role a direct reason to pay attention. The gap between expert optimism (73%) and public pessimism (23%) on AI's job impact also frames the internal culture challenge every manager faces when deploying AI tools: your team almost certainly does not share the optimism of the people building these systems. The governance dimension is equally concrete — a third of the world's AI data centers sit in the US, nearly every leading AI chip is made by one company in Taiwan, and the benchmarks companies use to decide which AI to buy have a 42% error rate. The data argues for AI-first skill development and skepticism about vendor benchmark claims in equal measure.
