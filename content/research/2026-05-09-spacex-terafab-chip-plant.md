## Summary
SpaceX has filed plans with Grimes County, Texas for a semiconductor fabrication plant called "Terafab," with an initial investment of at least $55 billion and a potential total investment of $119 billion if all phases are built. The plant will be run jointly by SpaceX and Tesla and will manufacture AI chips for both companies, targeting use in AI, robotics, and space-based data centers. Intel has been named as a design and fabrication partner, saying its chip manufacturing capabilities will help Terafab "produce 1 TW/year of compute to power future advances in AI and robotics." This is the largest single private investment in a US semiconductor plant announced in the AI era — exceeding Intel's Ohio plant and TSMC's Arizona expansion. The announcement came the same week SpaceX agreed to provide Anthropic with full access to the Colossus 1 supercomputer in Memphis, confirming that Musk's infrastructure plays are converging into a vertically integrated AI compute stack.

## Why It Matters
The Terafab announcement closes the loop on a critical competitive dynamic: Musk is building the full vertical stack for AI compute — chips (Terafab), data centers (Colossus), and model partnerships (Anthropic) — without running a public cloud. For founders and operators evaluating AI vendor choices, this matters because it signals that compute access is concentrating into a handful of vertically integrated players. The "compute tier your AI runs on is not in your contract" problem gets more acute as Terafab-class infrastructure comes online: organizations that signed AI vendor agreements in 2023-2024 have no visibility into the compute tier their workloads will run on once this manufacturing capacity reshapes supply. Practically: if your current AI vendor's pricing or availability changes in 2027-2028, Terafab-powered alternatives will exist, and the infrastructure battle will have already been decided.
