# OpenAI Kills Sora, Cuts Science Team, and Bets Everything on Enterprise AI

**Source URL:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/17/kevin-weil-and-bill-peebles-exit-openai-as-company-continues-to-shed-side-quests/

**Publication Date:** April 17, 2026

**Keyword Match:** Agentic AI as a fait accompli (Priority Cluster #1); AI model competitive landscape (SEO Keyword #9)

## Summary

OpenAI has shed two of its most prominent leaders — Kevin Weil (head of OpenAI for Science) and Bill Peebles (creator of Sora) — as part of a deliberate strategic pivot away from what the company calls "side quests" toward enterprise AI and its forthcoming "superapp." Sora, the video generation tool, was shut down last month after burning an estimated $1 million per day in compute costs. The science initiative (OpenAI for Science) is being absorbed into existing research teams. A third executive, Srinivas Narayanan (CTO of enterprise applications), also announced his departure. The exits mark the most visible evidence yet that OpenAI has concluded that consumer moonshots and research prestige are less valuable than enterprise revenue and the agentic AI platform race.

## Key Data Points

- Kevin Weil (head of OpenAI for Science) and Bill Peebles (creator of Sora) both departed April 17

- OpenAI for Science launched in October 2025, shut down within 6 months
- OpenAI's new focus: enterprise AI and a forthcoming consumer "superapp" integrating Codex
- Backdrop: Anthropic's revenue run rate hit $30B in March 2026, passing OpenAI's $24B
- OpenAI just released GPT-Rosalind (biology-tuned LLM) the day before Weil's departure
- Competitor Claude Code's success is directly driving OpenAI's Codex investment acceleration

## Why It Matters

For founders and team leads, OpenAI's pivot is a strategic signal with direct operational implications. The world's most-watched AI company is concluding that enterprise tooling and agentic infrastructure — not research moonshots — is where the durable value sits. That means the tools available to business operators are about to get significantly more powerful and better integrated. It also confirms the category dynamic: the race is now between enterprise-grade AI coding agents and agentic platforms, not between chatbots. Any business still treating AI as a peripheral experiment should notice that the companies building these tools have decided the enterprise is the primary battleground — and that battle is already underway.
