# Tech CEOs Are Apparently Suffering from AI Psychosis

## Title
Tech CEOs Are Apparently Suffering from "AI Psychosis"

## Summary
Box CEO Aaron Levie coined "AI psychosis" to describe how executives who demo AI prototypes extrapolate those results into company-wide automation decisions — without understanding the complexity of the last mile of actual work. The piece catalogs hard data that contradicts the optimism driving mass layoffs: a UC Berkeley meta-analysis found no robust relationship between AI adoption and aggregate productivity gain, MIT researchers say agents won't perform at 80–95% competence until 2029, and Harvard Business Review warns that mass AI delegation simply moves the bottleneck to executives who must approve everything agents produce. In the first five months of 2026 alone, 115,430 tech workers have been laid off — nearly matching all of 2025 — with AI productivity gains cited as justification by most companies. The piece uses ClickUp CEO Zeb Evans as the defining case study: he laid off 22% of his staff after deploying 3,000 internal AI agents, claiming this would create a "100x org."

## Key Data Points
- 115,430 tech layoffs in the first five months of 2026 across 152 companies — nearly matching all of 2025 (124,636 across 275 companies)
- ClickUp CEO laid off 22% of staff after deploying ~3,000 AI agents internally
- UC Berkeley California Management Review meta-analysis (October 2025): "no robust relationship between AI adoption and aggregate productivity gain"
- NBER research: AI adoption improved productivity, but "perceived productivity gains are larger than measured productivity gains"
- MIT researchers: AI agents can't do human-quality work yet on many tasks; competence projected at 80–95% by 2029
- Harvard Business Review: mass AI delegation shifts bottleneck to executives who must approve all agent output — creates organizational chaos
- Aaron Levie (Box CEO): "CEOs are uniquely prone to AI psychosis because they're sufficiently distant from the last mile of work that still has to happen"
- Levie's advice: CEOs should use AI heavily enough to personally understand both the upside and "the real work"

## Why It Matters
The gap between what a CEO sees in a demo and what actually ships in production has never been wider. Levie's "AI psychosis" framing is useful precisely because it names a specific cognitive failure: confusing the happy path with the production path. For founders making workforce decisions based on AI capability promises, the data is a direct warning. Companies that laid off talent to fund agents are discovering that agents haven't reached the quality threshold needed for autonomous work, and they've destroyed the institutional knowledge that made the company function. The Harvard bottleneck finding is particularly sharp: if everyone produces more but all approvals still route through the same humans, output doesn't increase — it backs up.

## Relevance to B2B Decision-Makers
Any founder or team lead considering restructuring headcount around AI agents needs to pressure-test whether their AI deployment is above the MIT competence threshold for their specific tasks. The productivity paradox data (NBER) suggests even companies where AI is "working" may be overestimating real gains. The ClickUp example shows what happens when the decision is made at the CEO level without understanding the operational reality. The key question for SMB operators: are you measuring AI impact on actual outcomes (revenue, customer satisfaction) or proxy metrics (tasks completed, tokens consumed)?
