## Summary

Hark, the AI lab founded by serial entrepreneur Brett Adcock (previously Figure.AI and Archer Aviation), has raised a $700 million Series A round at a $6 billion valuation — before releasing any product publicly. The round was led by Parkway Venture Capital with participation from AMD Ventures, ARK Invest, Brookfield, Intel Capital, Qualcomm Ventures, and Salesforce Ventures. Hark is building what it describes as a "universal AI interface with the digital world" targeting everyday users, not just developers. The company plans to release multimodal models this summer, followed by custom hardware devices. Adcock self-funded the launch with $100 million of his own capital in late 2025 and has grown the team to 70 people operating a proprietary Nvidia B200 GPU data center.

## Why It Matters

A $700 million Series A at a $6 billion valuation with no public product is either the most confident bet in AI consumer hardware since the Vision Pro, or it signals that investors believe the next major AI platform — the one that captures everyday users, not just power users — has not been built yet. For founders, this is a market-gap signal: the investor thesis is that enterprise AI is saturated with capital, developer-facing AI tools are crowded, and the underserved market is a consumer AI interface that "normal people" actually want to use. Hark's backer list is unusually hardware-heavy (AMD, Intel Capital, Qualcomm), which combined with Adcock's robotics background suggests the "custom hardware device" roadmap is the real bet, not just another chatbot. For team leads evaluating AI tools, the emerging pattern is that the most-funded bets in 2026 are about AI that acts in the physical world and through persistent personal hardware — not browser tabs.
