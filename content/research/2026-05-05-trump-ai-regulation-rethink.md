## Summary
The Trump administration is reconsidering its light-touch approach to AI regulation, with federal officials debating mandatory pre-release government review of frontier AI models. Anthropic's Mythos model — which triggered emergency responses from central banks and intelligence agencies globally — is cited as the catalyst for the shift. David Sacks (White House AI czar) and Dario Amodei (Anthropic CEO) are both named as key players in the policy discussion. The concern is no longer about innovation risk but about national security: a powerful model reaching adversaries before the government can assess it. This represents a meaningful reversal from the administration's earlier deregulatory posture on AI.

## Key Data Points
- Anthropic's Mythos model triggered emergency responses from central banks and intelligence agencies across multiple countries after release
- The NSA separately tested Mythos Preview for cybersecurity vulnerabilities
- Microsoft, xAI, and Google have already agreed to share frontier AI models with the US government for security review
- GPT-5.5 matched Mythos in independent cybersecurity benchmarks, suggesting the capability concern is not model-specific
- White House AI czar David Sacks is reportedly involved in the policy rethink alongside Dario Amodei
- A prior Axios report noted a draft policy that would let agencies procure models even if the developer's safety evaluation flagged them as high-risk — this new discussion signals a harder line

## Why It Matters
For founders and SMB operators deploying frontier AI models, government pre-release vetting changes the procurement timeline and vendor relationship. If a model is withheld pending government review, the tools they have roadmapped may arrive later — or in restricted form. For any business building on top of Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google models, the regulatory status of the underlying model becomes a supply-chain risk. This also signals that AI governance is moving from voluntary (developer-led safety cards) to mandatory (pre-release government sign-off), which changes how enterprise AI contracts and compliance policies will need to be written.
