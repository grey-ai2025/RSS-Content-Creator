## Summary

At Nvidia's Q1 2026 earnings call, Jensen Huang identified a brand-new $200 billion total addressable market: CPUs purpose-built for AI agents. Nvidia introduced Vera in March — described as "the world's first CPU purpose-built for agentic AI" — and has already generated $20 billion in Vera sales in 2026. Huang's argument is structural: while GPUs handle the "thinking" in AI systems, agents rely on CPUs to execute tasks. As billions of AI agents come online, each needs its own CPU infrastructure. Nvidia posted $81.6 billion in Q1 revenue (beating estimates of $78.86 billion), guided Q2 at $91 billion, and disclosed that its holdings in private startups nearly doubled in one quarter — from $22 billion to $43 billion in stakes — as the company bets on the broader AI ecosystem.

## Why It Matters

Nvidia's identification of a brand-new $200B market specifically for AI agent CPUs is the clearest signal yet from the infrastructure layer that the "agent era" is not a feature — it's a separate compute paradigm. For founders building products on top of AI agents, the Vera CPU signal matters for two reasons. First, it confirms that hyperscalers are investing heavily in infrastructure specifically designed for agents running at scale, which means the cost-per-agent-action will fall significantly over the next 18-24 months — making agentic workflows more economically viable for SMBs. Second, Nvidia's $43 billion startup portfolio means the company is now one of the most active AI investors on the planet. If you're building in the agentic stack, Nvidia's investment appetite is a new funding channel to track. The Q1 earnings also confirm that enterprise AI infrastructure spending is not slowing — $91 billion guided for Q2 is acceleration, not plateau.
