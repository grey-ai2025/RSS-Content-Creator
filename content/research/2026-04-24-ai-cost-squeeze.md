## Summary
After years of subsidized access to AI tools, the economics are inverting: AI labs are under mounting pressure to return capital to investors who have poured hundreds of billions into data centers, and the cost is starting to flow downstream to businesses. The Verge's deep investigation reveals that to hit a bare-minimum 7% return on invested capital, major AI providers collectively need to earn close to $7 trillion in AI-driven revenue through 2029 — roughly $2 trillion per year by end of period. Token consumption would need to grow 50,000-100,000x from current levels to reach that target. Meanwhile, Anthropic has already restricted third-party tools like OpenClaw unless users pay premium prices; OpenAI has introduced in-platform advertising; and enterprise plans have shifted from flat-rate to token-based billing. Companies interviewed report token usage costs climbing significantly year-over-year, with some evaluating a shift to open-source models to manage expenses.

## Key Data Points
- Gartner estimates $6.3 trillion in capital investment in AI data centers between 2024–2029
- To hit 7% ROIC (bare minimum to avoid write-downs): providers need ~$7 trillion in cumulative AI-driven revenue through 2029 (~$2 trillion/year by end of period)
- To hit "historic returns": ~$8.2 trillion needed over same period
- Token consumption would need to grow 50,000–100,000x from current levels to hit $2T/year target
- OpenAI has made $600 billion in spending commitments through 2030 (down from originally planned $1.4 trillion)
- Gartner predicts no more than 2 large language model providers per regional market will survive market consolidation
- Google processed 1.3 quadrillion tokens in October; all providers combined: ~100–200 quadrillion/year — still far short of sextillion-level target
- One company (Eve, legal AI) reports 100x year-over-year growth in token usage, now spending 25–30% of usage on premium reasoning models
- Aaron Levie (Box CEO): "The use cases have exploded, and we're out of capacity."
- OpenAI and Anthropic enterprise plans have both shifted to token-based (not flat-rate) billing
- Open-source models now used by companies as cost pressure relief — "what open source is really doing is it's putting pressure on these companies to make their cheaper models cheaper"
- Anthropic restricted OpenClaw (viral AI agent tool) unless users pay extra — cited "subscriptions weren't built for these usage patterns"

## Why It Matters
Every founder currently running AI tools on a flat-rate or lightly used subscription is living in borrowed time. The structure of AI pricing is shifting from "cheap to try" to "expensive to scale" — and the companies best positioned are the ones that already know which tasks demand premium models and which can run on cheaper alternatives. The "stegosaurus paradox" (Gartner's framing): AI labs need the entire global economy to become their food source to survive, not just the tech market. For a 10-50 person company, this is the moment to do an AI budget audit: map your token usage, identify which workflows require frontier models, and start evaluating open-source or cheaper tier alternatives before the pricing pressure arrives with urgency.
