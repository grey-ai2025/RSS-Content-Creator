## Summary
A Harvard Business School-led study by Suraj Srinivasan (HBS, Philip J. Stomberg Professor of Business Administration) with co-authors Wilbur Xinyuan Chen (Hong Kong University of Science and Technology) and Saleh Zakerinia (Ohio State University) analyzed nearly all US job vacancies from 2019 through March 2025. The headline finding: generative AI is not driving wholesale job elimination — it is bifurcating the market. Postings for occupations dominated by structured, repetitive tasks fell 13%, while postings for analytical, technical, and creative roles grew 20%. The skill requirements within automation-prone occupations also dropped 7%, meaning the surviving routine roles are being deskilled, not preserved. The largest reductions concentrated in finance and technology — sectors typically considered "knowledge work." The researchers used ChatGPT to categorize over 19,000 job tasks across 900+ occupations to build augmentation scores. Their conclusion: human-AI collaboration, not displacement, is the operative mechanism transforming labor markets, and firms that treat AI purely as a cost-cutting tool will miss the bigger opportunity in augmentation-prone roles.

## Key Data Points
- 13% decline in job postings for occupations with structured, repetitive tasks (high automation potential)
- 20% growth in employer demand for analytical, technical, or creative roles (high augmentation potential)
- 7% reduction in skill requirements (skill diversity) for automation-prone occupations
- Study period: 2019 through March 2025 — covers the pre- and post-ChatGPT periods
- Dataset: nearly all US job vacancies
- Methodology: OpenAI's ChatGPT used to categorize over 19,000 job tasks across more than 900 occupations
- Largest reductions concentrated in finance and technology sectors

## Why It Matters
For founders, hiring managers, and operators staffing teams in 2026, this is the first large-N, all-US-vacancies dataset that quantifies the bifurcation people have been describing anecdotally for two years. The number that should reset assumptions: the 7% drop in skill requirements inside automation-prone roles. That is not a story about jobs disappearing — it is a story about jobs being deskilled before they disappear, which removes the wage premium long before the headcount reduction shows up. The other reset: finance and technology lead the reductions. The "knowledge work is safe" mental model is wrong. The premium is moving to roles that combine AI-leverage tasks with judgment, social skills, and hands-on technical expertise — and those postings are up 20%. For a small business owner, the implication is hiring strategy: the same headcount budget now buys very different output if it's pointed at augmentation-prone roles instead of routine knowledge-work roles. For an employee in finance or tech: the deskilling signal is the warning that arrives before the layoff.

## Methodology Note
The study acknowledges its scope is short-term US labor market impacts; effects on other regions and long-term impacts remain uncertain as adoption scales. The augmentation scores were constructed based on the proportion of exposed-vs-unexposed tasks within each occupation, not on direct observation of AI adoption inside firms.
