# Defense Official Reveals How AI Chatbots Could Be Used for Targeting Decisions

**Source URL:** https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/03/12/1134243/defense-official-military-use-ai-chatbots-targeting-decisions/

**Publication Date:** March 12, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI in military and geopolitics, AI governance and data readiness

## Summary

A Defense Department official speaking on background to MIT Technology Review disclosed that the US military may use generative AI systems — potentially including ChatGPT and Grok — to rank and prioritize target lists, with human review of results before action. The disclosure comes as the Pentagon faces scrutiny over a strike on a girls' school in Iran that killed more than 100 children, with a preliminary investigation pointing to outdated targeting data as a contributing factor. The military already runs a long-standing "big data" targeting initiative called Maven, which uses computer vision to analyze drone footage and identify targets. Generative AI is now being layered on top of Maven as a conversational interface that can analyze data and make prioritization recommendations faster — though the official said generative AI systems are fundamentally less battle-tested than the older AI powering Maven and harder to verify. Anthropic's Claude was reportedly used in operations in Iran and Venezuela before being banned from Pentagon use following the Anthropic-DoD legal dispute; OpenAI and xAI (Grok) have since reached classified-use agreements with the Pentagon.

## Key Data Points

- Defense Department official confirmed AI chatbots may be used to rank and prioritize target lists — with human vetting of results
- The Pentagon's Maven system (in operation since 2017) uses computer vision to process drone footage and identify targets
- Generative AI adds a conversational chatbot layer to accelerate data analysis and target prioritization
- OpenAI's ChatGPT and xAI's Grok have both reached classified-use agreements with the Pentagon
- Anthropic's Claude was reportedly used in Iran and Venezuela operations; now banned from federal use following DoD dispute
- A preliminary Pentagon investigation found outdated targeting data partly responsible for the Iran school strike that killed 100+ children
- The official declined to quantify speed improvements from generative AI given required human double-checking
- GenAI.mil, launched in December 2025, gives millions of service members non-classified access to generative AI for tasks like contract analysis and presentation writing

## Why It Matters

The use of generative AI in military targeting is no longer hypothetical. A Defense Department official has now described a specific architecture: AI chatbots ranking target lists, humans reviewing recommendations, and speed as the stated operational benefit. This disclosure raises critical questions that professional audiences in tech, policy, and ethics are actively debating: What does "human in the loop" mean when the AI is generating the ranked list and humans are under pressure to act fast? Who is accountable when a generative AI model — known to hallucinate and difficult to audit — contributes to a targeting decision? The answer will shape AI governance frameworks far beyond the military.
