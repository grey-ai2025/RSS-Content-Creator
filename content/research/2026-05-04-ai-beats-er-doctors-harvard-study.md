# Harvard Study: AI Diagnosed Emergency Room Patients More Accurately Than Two Human Doctors

**Source URL:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/03/in-harvard-study-ai-offered-more-accurate-diagnoses-than-emergency-room-doctors/

**Publication Date:** May 3, 2026

**Keyword Match:** Generative AI ROI and enterprise adoption maturity, AI fluency

**Summary:**
A peer-reviewed study published in Science by researchers at Harvard Medical School and Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center found that OpenAI's o1 model achieved higher diagnostic accuracy than two internal medicine attending physicians when assessing 76 real emergency room cases. The o1 model reached the exact or a very close diagnosis 67% of the time at initial triage, compared to 55% and 50% for the human physicians. Researchers were careful to note this does not mean AI is ready for clinical deployment — the study calls for prospective trials and flagged significant limitations including the text-only format and lack of accountability frameworks. An emergency medicine specialist also questioned the methodology, noting that internal medicine doctors are not ER specialists, making the comparison imperfect. The study was published as part of a broader examination of LLM performance across multiple medical contexts.

**Key Data Points:**
- o1 model accuracy at initial ER triage: 67% (exact or very close diagnosis)
- Physician 1 accuracy: 55%; Physician 2 accuracy: 50%
- o1 "either performed nominally better than or on par" with both physicians across all diagnostic touchpoints
- Study sample: 76 real ER patient cases
- Study published in Science by Harvard Medical School + Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center
- Limitation: AI worked from text records only; no physical exam data; no patient interaction
- No formal accountability framework exists for AI diagnostic errors
- Critique from ER specialist: internal medicine attendings are not ER practitioners — specialty mismatch weakens the comparison

**Why It Matters:**
For founders and operators building in professional services, healthcare, or any high-stakes domain, this study represents a data point that the AI-as-expert-advisor model is not just hype — it is measurably competitive with credentialed humans in controlled conditions. The accountability gap (no framework for AI diagnostic error) is the business-critical detail: the capability is there, the governance infrastructure is not. Any founder considering AI for professional advisory use cases — legal, medical, financial — should be tracking both the performance benchmarks and the liability question simultaneously. This story also reinforces the broader GPT-5.5 and agentic capability narrative running this week.
