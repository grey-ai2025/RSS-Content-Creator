# Regulators Flag Dangerous Control Gaps as Enterprises Deploy AI Agents

**Source URL:** https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/ai-agent-governance-control-gaps/

**Publication Date:** April 30, 2026

**Keyword Match:** agentic AI; EU AI Act high-risk deadline; AI agents enterprise adoption

## Summary

Australia's financial regulator APRA conducted a targeted review of large regulated entities in late 2025 and found that while AI adoption is now universal, governance and risk management practices remain dangerously immature. The regulator found boards failing to independently scrutinize AI risk, institutions lacking basic inventories of deployed AI tools, and security architectures that were never designed for non-human AI agents. The review comes as banks and superannuation trustees rapidly expand AI in both internal and customer-facing operations — and as the EU's August 2, 2026 Annex III compliance deadline approaches with no trilogue deal in sight.

## Key Data Points

- AI was in active use across 100% of APRA-reviewed entities
- Most entities still building fundamental AI risk management frameworks — despite widespread deployment
- Boards found over-reliant on vendor materials rather than independent risk analysis
- Missing: AI tool inventories, defined ownership, and model performance tracking systems
- Identified attack vectors: prompt injection, insecure AI integrations, identity/access gaps for non-human agents
- Vendor concentration risk flagged: some institutions have no viable exit strategy if a single AI provider fails
- APRA warning: treating AI risk "in the same terms as other technologies" fails to account for AI's unpredictability
- New standards in development: FIDO Alliance and CIS are building authentication frameworks specifically for AI agents

## Why It Matters

This story lands hardest for founders and operators who have already deployed AI agents in customer-facing or operational workflows without formalizing oversight. The APRA finding that boards are relying on vendor materials rather than independent analysis is a warning for any SMB: if you're trusting your AI provider's safety claims without independent review, you're in the same position as the big banks the regulator just flagged. The prompt injection and non-human agent access control issues are not theoretical — any business using AI agents that browse the web, read emails, or access internal systems faces the same attack surface. Practical question: does your team have an inventory of every AI tool in use, who owns it, and what access it has?
