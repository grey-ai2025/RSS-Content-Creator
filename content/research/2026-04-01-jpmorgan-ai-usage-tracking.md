# JPMorgan Is Now Tracking Whether Its Engineers Use AI — And It Could Affect Their Reviews

**Title:** JPMorgan begins tracking how employees use AI at work

**Source URL:** https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/jpmorgan-begins-tracking-how-employees-use-ai-at-work/

**Publication Date:** March 30, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI in HR and workforce; AI accountability and governance; generative AI moving from hype to pragmatism

## Summary

JPMorgan Chase has begun tracking how its approximately 65,000 engineers and technologists use AI tools as part of their regular workflow, with managers monitoring adoption frequency. According to reporting from Business Insider, that usage data may also influence employee performance reviews — making AI utilization a measurable job performance metric for the first time at one of the world's largest financial institutions. Employees are encouraged to use tools including ChatGPT and Claude Code for tasks like coding, document review, and routine analysis. The bank's internal system categorizes workers as either "light users" or "heavy users" based on adoption. This move represents the clearest signal yet from a major enterprise that AI adoption is no longer optional — it is an expectation baked into how performance is defined.

## Key Data Points

- JPMorgan Chase tracking AI usage across ~65,000 engineers and technologists
- Tools in use include ChatGPT and Claude Code for coding, document review, and routine tasks
- Workers categorized internally as "light users" or "heavy users" based on adoption frequency
- AI usage data "may also influence performance reviews" — per Business Insider reporting
- Managers actively monitoring adoption patterns across teams
- The bank has been among the most aggressive large financial institutions in mandating enterprise AI adoption
- Key unresolved tension: whether higher AI usage should mean more output in the same time, or whether the metric itself is the goal
- Financial institutions must balance efficiency mandates with regulatory compliance requirements for all AI-assisted work

## Why It Matters

JPMorgan making AI usage a performance metric is a watershed moment in the enterprise AI adoption story. It signals the end of the "optional tool" era and the beginning of the "baseline competency" era — the same shift that happened with spreadsheets in the 1990s and with email in the 2000s. For HR leaders and executives, this raises an immediate strategic question: is your organization still treating AI adoption as voluntary? For employees, it reframes the risk calculation entirely — not using AI is no longer neutral. For the broader professional community watching how enterprise AI mandates evolve, JPMorgan's move at 65,000 people will set a precedent that competitors feel compelled to match.
