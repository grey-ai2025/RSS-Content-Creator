# Microsoft's Copilot Health Connects Your Medical Records and Wearables to AI

**Source URL:** https://www.theverge.com/tech/893594/microsoft-copilot-health-launch

**Publication Date:** March 12, 2026

**Keyword Match:** agentic AI, AI governance and data readiness

## Summary

Microsoft launched Copilot Health, a sandboxed section of its Copilot assistant designed for health-related queries — connecting to medical records from over 50,000 US hospitals and healthcare organizations, lab test results, and more than 50 wearable devices including Apple Watch, Oura, and Fitbit. The product joins a rapidly crowding field: OpenAI launched ChatGPT Health in January, Amazon expanded its Health AI agent in early March, and Anthropic's Claude is positioning itself as "HIPAA-ready." Microsoft explicitly states Copilot Health is not a diagnostic tool but a health data interpreter — helping users understand lab results, find doctors by specialty and insurance, and track wearable data over time. A notable governance gap: Copilot Health is not currently HIPAA-compliant, unlike ChatGPT for Healthcare and Amazon's offering, though Microsoft's VP of Health AI says HIPAA compliance is not legally required for direct consumer products using personal data.

## Key Data Points

- Connects to medical records from 50,000+ US hospitals via HealthEx; lab results via Function
- Compatible with 50+ wearables (Apple Watch, Oura, Fitbit, others)
- Chats in Copilot Health are isolated from general Copilot and not used for model training
- Users can delete health data or disconnect data sources at any time
- Responses include citations and "expert-written answer cards from Harvard Health"
- ISO 42001 certified (international AI systems responsibility standard)
- Not HIPAA-compliant at launch — unlike ChatGPT for Healthcare, Amazon Health AI, and Anthropic Claude for Healthcare
- Microsoft VP of Health AI: "HIPAA is not required for a direct-consumer experience like this when you're using your own data"
- OpenAI, Amazon, Anthropic, and now Microsoft have all launched health AI products within a 2-month window

## Why It Matters

Four major AI companies have now launched health data products within two months of each other. The race to become the default AI health assistant is accelerating — and the governance gaps are widening in parallel. Microsoft's decision to launch without HIPAA compliance is a signal about how AI companies are interpreting the boundary between "consumer tool" and "medical product." For enterprise buyers in healthcare and adjacent sectors, this raises a critical question: when AI companies can change their data privacy policies at any time, what does it actually mean to share health records with an AI? The simultaneous multi-company launch is also a case study in how fast AI product categories move from novel to crowded.
