## Summary
A MIT Technology Review Insights research piece exposes a critical readiness gap at the heart of enterprise AI strategy: 85% of organizations say they want to become fully agentic within three years, but 76% acknowledge their current operations and infrastructure cannot support that transition. The piece introduces a framework called Agentic Business Transformation (ABT) built on three pillars — technology stack redesign, workforce redesign, and new performance metrics. The core argument is that organizations are failing because they layer AI agents on top of existing processes rather than redesigning those processes around agents. PwC's Prasun Shah describes the mistake as "adding sticky tapes to parts of an operating model that is breaking." The report concludes that real value from agents only emerges through enterprise-wide systems transformation, not incremental tool deployment.

## Key Data Points
- 85% of organizations want to adopt agentic AI within three years
- 76% say their current operations and infrastructure cannot support this transition
- Potential gains from proper transformation: 30–50% acceleration in business processes; 25–40% reduction in low-value work
- McKinsey: 75% of current jobs will require redesign or upskilling by 2030
- One enterprise tripled ROI after switching from output metrics to outcome metrics
- PwC's Prasun Shah: organizations are "adding sticky tapes to parts of an operating model that is breaking"
- Three ABT pillars: (1) technology stack — agents as connective tissue, not add-ons; (2) workforce redesign — hybrid human-AI teams; (3) performance metrics — shift from output to outcomes
- Traditional hierarchies must evolve to accommodate trust, explainability, and status dynamics of AI teammates

## Why It Matters
The 85%/76% gap is the defining number in enterprise AI right now. Nine in ten organizations have the ambition to go agentic; three in four can't actually do it with what they have. This is not a capability problem with AI — it is an organizational problem with humans who have not redesigned the systems AI needs to operate in. The "built backwards" frame from the SEO keywords file is exactly what this research surfaces: adding an AI agent to a broken workflow does not fix the workflow; it accelerates the breakage. For founders and operators, the actionable insight is that agent ROI depends on whether you redesign the process the agent will run, not just the tool you deploy.

## Relevance to B2B Decision-Makers
SMB operators facing this 85%/76% split have an advantage over enterprise: they can redesign faster. A 10–50 person company can restructure workflows around agents in weeks; a 10,000-person enterprise takes years. The metric shift is the most immediately actionable insight: if your team is measuring AI success by tasks completed or prompts generated (output), you're measuring the wrong thing. Measuring AI success by customer satisfaction improvement, revenue per head, or time-to-decision (outcomes) is what correlates to real ROI. The tripled-ROI data point from the report validates the switch.
