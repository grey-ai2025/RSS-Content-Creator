## Summary
Both Anthropic and OpenAI have simultaneously launched joint ventures with major private equity and asset management firms to distribute enterprise AI services through their portfolio companies. Anthropic closed $1.5 billion from Blackstone, Goldman Sachs, and Hellman & Friedman, while OpenAI is raising $4 billion through "The Development Company" with TPG, Brookfield, Advent, and Bain Capital. The core logic: PE firms collectively own thousands of mid-market companies, giving the AI labs a ready-made distribution channel without traditional enterprise sales cycles. The ventures deploy "forward-deployed engineers" to implement AI tools directly within portfolio company workflows. This marks a fundamental shift — AI is no longer sold as software; it is embedded as a service through existing investment relationships.

## Key Data Points
- Anthropic's venture: $1.5B from Blackstone, Goldman Sachs, and Hellman & Friedman (each committing $300M); Apollo, General Atlantic, GIC, Leonard Green, and Sequoia also participating
- OpenAI's "The Development Company": raising $4B against a $10B valuation; investors include TPG, Brookfield, Advent, and Bain Capital
- No overlap between the two ventures' investor bases — each lab has a distinct PE distribution network
- Blackstone alone owns 250+ portfolio companies; total PE consortium spans several thousand businesses
- Both ventures use "forward-deployed engineers" embedded with individual clients — not a SaaS license, but a service deployment model
- OpenAI recently raised $122B (valuing it at $852B); Anthropic finalizing $50B round at ~$900B valuation
- Enterprise sales motion now starts with "Is your sponsor part of the OpenAI consortium?" rather than "What's your tech stack?"

## Why It Matters
For a founder or SMB operator, this changes who controls AI access for a large segment of the market. If your business is PE-backed or owned by a firm in one of these consortiums, your AI vendor may effectively be chosen for you. For everyone else, this concentrates premium AI deployment support inside specific ownership networks — those outside the network get the standard API, not the forward-deployed engineer. It also confirms the Service-as-Software thesis: both labs are now selling outcomes and implementation, not just model access. The pricing and support structures that smaller businesses have relied on are diverging sharply from what PE-backed enterprises receive.
