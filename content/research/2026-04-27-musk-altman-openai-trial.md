# Musk vs. Altman: A High-Stakes AI Clash Goes to Court

**Source URL:** https://www.nytimes.com/2026/04/27/business/dealbook/musk-altman-ai-trial.html

**Publication Date:** April 27, 2026

**Keyword Match:** Musk vs. Altman / OpenAI trial (#1 trending topic)

## Summary

The civil trial between Elon Musk and OpenAI officially begins today in Oakland, California, with jury selection underway. Musk is seeking more than $150 billion in damages and a complete restructuring of OpenAI, arguing he was defrauded when the nonprofit he co-founded and funded shifted toward a for-profit model after he departed. Sam Altman is the #1 trending US search term today, and the witness list includes Altman, Musk, Satya Nadella (Microsoft CEO), and other key figures from the early days of OpenAI. The outcome could reshape how AI companies are structured, how nonprofit-to-profit conversions are governed, and which organization controls the most powerful AI systems on earth. The broader AI industry is watching closely: if Musk wins, it could force OpenAI to unwind its corporate transformation and potentially halt or complicate its path to IPO.

## Key Data Points

- Musk is seeking **more than $150 billion** in damages — one of the largest damage claims in tech legal history
- Trial is being held in **Oakland, California** with jury selection beginning April 27, 2026
- Witness list includes **Sam Altman, Elon Musk, Satya Nadella (Microsoft), Greg Brockman, Jared Birchall, Shivon Zilis, and Mark Zuckerberg**
- Musk's core legal argument: OpenAI was founded as a nonprofit to benefit humanity, and its shift to a for-profit structure defrauded him as a founding donor
- OpenAI has received major investments from **Google ($40B) and Microsoft**, making the trial's outcome a multi-trillion-dollar business question
- Sam Altman is **#1 trending US search term** on April 27 — this is a dominant public conversation topic
- DeepSeek is also listed among the organizations referenced in court filings, signaling how global the competitive stakes are
- If Musk prevails, OpenAI could be forced to completely restructure its corporate governance

## Why It Matters

For founders and SMB operators, the Musk-Altman trial is not just celebrity drama — it is the most consequential legal test of how AI companies are allowed to evolve. Virtually every founder considering building on or partnering with OpenAI, or any AI company that may eventually seek investor capital or restructure, has a stake in how courts interpret the nonprofit-to-for-profit conversion argument. The trial will also surface internal communications and strategy documents from OpenAI's early days, likely exposing how major AI decisions were actually made. The outcome will affect AI pricing, access, governance norms, and who controls the dominant AI platforms that millions of businesses now depend on.
