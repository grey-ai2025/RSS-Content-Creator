# States Plow Ahead With A.I. Regulation, Defying Trump

**Source URL:** https://www.nytimes.com/2026/03/30/technology/trump-states-ai-gavin-newsom-california.html

**Publication Date:** March 30, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI regulation and federal policy; AI governance and enterprise scaling

## Summary

States across the U.S. are moving forward with their own AI guardrails despite President Trump ordering them to stand down. California Governor Gavin Newsom signed an executive order on March 30 requiring safety and privacy protections for AI companies holding state contracts — a direct counter to the White House National Policy Framework released March 20, which recommended federal preemption of state AI laws. Utah, Texas, and a growing number of other states are also advancing their own AI legislation. The moves set the stage for a significant federal-state standoff over who controls the rules governing AI deployment in the United States.

## Key Data Points

- California's executive order mandates safety and privacy guardrails for all AI vendors contracting with the state government
- The White House National Policy Framework for AI was released March 20 and includes preemption recommendations intended to block state-level AI regulation
- States ranging from California to Utah are defying the federal directive by advancing their own AI oversight measures
- Governor Newsom is simultaneously pushing back against the federal framework while positioning California as a de facto national AI regulator by proxy
- The Anthropic-Pentagon dispute — in which a judge blocked the Pentagon's supply chain risk designation on March 26 — is running in parallel as a second front in the federal AI governance battle
- More than 700 state AI bills have been introduced across U.S. legislatures in 2026, according to prior reporting

## Why It Matters

Enterprise leaders and legal teams need to understand that AI governance is now a dual-track compliance challenge: federal deregulation is proceeding alongside a proliferation of state-level rules that may directly affect vendor contracts, data handling, and deployment decisions. For any organization contracting with state governments — or operating in regulated industries — the California executive order is a leading indicator of standards that other states may adopt. The tension between federal preemption and state experimentation will shape AI procurement and risk strategy for years.
