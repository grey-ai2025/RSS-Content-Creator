# Okta CEO: AI Agents Need Their Own Identity Layer — Or Enterprise Security Fails

**Title:** Okta's CEO is betting big on AI agent identity

**Source URL:** https://www.theverge.com/podcast/902264/oktas-ceo-is-betting-big-on-ai-agent-identity

**Publication Date:** March 30, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI agents / agentic AI; AI accountability and governance; cybersecurity and AI

## Summary

Okta co-founder and CEO Todd McKinnon argues that the rapid proliferation of AI agents inside enterprise environments has created an urgent identity crisis — literally. Every AI agent operating inside a company needs to be authenticated, permissioned, and auditable, just like a human employee, but most companies are deploying agents without any formal identity infrastructure underneath them. McKinnon frames the threat bluntly: running agents without a proper identity layer is "naive," and companies that do so are creating attack surfaces and accountability gaps they cannot see. Okta is positioning itself as the identity provider for the agentic era, extending its core platform from human identity management to AI agent identity management. McKinnon also introduced the concept of the "SaaSpocalypse" — the scenario where AI agents replace or bypass traditional SaaS applications entirely, disrupting the existing enterprise software stack.

## Key Data Points

- Okta CEO Todd McKinnon: every AI agent needs authentication, permissioning, and an audit trail — the same infrastructure required for human employees
- McKinnon called it "naive" to deploy AI agents without a formal identity layer in place
- Okta is extending its core identity platform to cover non-human identities, specifically AI agents
- The "SaaSpocalypse" concept: AI agents that execute tasks end-to-end may render traditional SaaS applications obsolete — McKinnon says it would be naive NOT to prepare for this scenario
- Without proper agent identity, organizations cannot answer basic audit questions: which agent took which action, with what permissions, and when
- AI agents operating with human-level credentials (as many enterprises currently do) represent a compounding security and compliance risk
- Identity for AI agents is becoming an enterprise governance issue, not just a security issue

## Why It Matters

Most of the public conversation about AI agents focuses on what they can do. Okta's CEO is drawing attention to a gap that most enterprise AI deployments have not addressed: what governs them. As companies accelerate agent deployment in 2026, the question of agent identity — who (or what) authorized this action, what did it have access to, and is there an audit trail — is moving from theoretical to urgent. For compliance officers, CISOs, and anyone building enterprise AI strategy, this is the accountability infrastructure question the Accenture/Wharton "intelligence may be scalable but accountability is not" framing points directly at. Okta is positioning itself at the center of this problem, but the underlying issue applies to every enterprise deploying agents regardless of vendor.
