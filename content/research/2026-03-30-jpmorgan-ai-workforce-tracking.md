# JPMorgan Is Tracking How 65,000 Employees Use AI — and It May Affect Their Performance Reviews

**Source URL:** https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/jpmorgan-begins-tracking-how-employees-use-ai-at-work/

**Publication Date:** March 30, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI workforce upskilling (#6); Generative AI in enterprise (#7)

## Summary

JPMorgan Chase has begun requiring approximately 65,000 engineers and technologists to use AI tools as a standard part of their workflow — and managers are now actively monitoring how often staff engage with those tools. According to Business Insider, employees are being categorized as either "light users" or "heavy users," and AI tool usage may now influence performance reviews. The tools being tracked include ChatGPT and Claude Code, primarily applied to coding, document review, and workflow automation tasks. The move signals a structural shift in how one of the world's largest financial institutions treats AI competency: no longer optional, but a baseline expectation with measurable accountability. All AI outputs still require human verification before use in client-facing or decision-making contexts, but the pressure to engage is now embedded into the performance management system.

## Key Data Points

- Approximately 65,000 JPMorgan engineers and technologists are subject to the new AI usage mandate
- Employees are classified as "light users" or "heavy users" based on tracked AI tool engagement
- AI tool usage may now directly influence individual performance reviews at JPMorgan
- Tools being tracked include ChatGPT and Claude Code
- Primary use cases: coding assistance, document review, and routine workflow automation
- All AI-generated outputs still require human verification before deployment in regulated or client-facing contexts
- JPMorgan is one of the largest financial institutions globally, managing trillions in assets and employing over 300,000 people worldwide

## Why It Matters

This is one of the clearest signals yet that AI adoption in large enterprises is moving from voluntary experimentation to institutional mandate. JPMorgan is not asking employees if they want to use AI — it is tracking whether they do, and tying that to career outcomes. For enterprise leaders watching the AI-readiness gap widen, this sets a precedent: the question is no longer whether to deploy AI, but how to build accountability structures around adoption. It also raises important workforce questions — particularly around fairness, surveillance, and what "good AI use" actually looks like when measured. For professionals in finance, tech, and beyond, this is a sign that AI proficiency is rapidly becoming a career-critical skill evaluated at the management level, not just a personal choice.
