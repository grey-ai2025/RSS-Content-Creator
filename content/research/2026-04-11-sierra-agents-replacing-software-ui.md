## Summary

Bret Taylor, co-founder and CEO of enterprise AI startup Sierra and former co-CEO of Salesforce, made a direct prediction at the HumanX conference in San Francisco this week: traditional click-based software interfaces are being replaced by natural language, and the transition is already underway. Sierra launched a product called Ghostwriter — an agent that builds other agents — which allows users to describe a task in plain language and have a specialized agent autonomously created and deployed to execute it. Taylor's argument is grounded in enterprise reality: most enterprise software is used infrequently and awkwardly, and the barrier between what workers need and what complex SaaS tools require is enormous. His example: a Nordstrom implementation was deployed in just four weeks. The broader implication is that enterprises will stop buying software and start buying outcomes — agents as a service, not apps.

## Key Data Points

- Sierra launched **Ghostwriter**, an "agent as a service" tool that autonomously creates and deploys specialized agents based on natural language descriptions
- Sierra reached **$100 million ARR in under 21 months** after founding — among the fastest enterprise SaaS trajectories ever recorded
- The company was last valued at **$10 billion** following a **$350 million round** led by Greenoaks Capital in September 2025
- Taylor's Nordstrom example: an AI agent implementation completed in **just four weeks** using Ghostwriter
- Taylor cited Workday as the canonical example of underused enterprise software: workers sign in at onboarding and for open enrollment, never otherwise
- Qualifier: experts and investors note that "AI agent implementation is far from autonomous" — companies like Sierra and Harvey still require "forward-deployed" engineers to constantly update and fine-tune customer agents
- Taylor was formerly co-CEO of Salesforce and chairman of the board of OpenAI

## Why It Matters

For enterprise operators, IT leaders, and software buyers, Taylor's argument is a direct challenge to the SaaS status quo. If agents replace the interfaces of complex software, it reshapes the entire enterprise software market — pricing models, vendor relationships, IT headcount, and how organizations think about software procurement. The $10 billion valuation on $100M ARR confirms this is not a concept pitch; it is a live market transition. For individual professionals, the personal-stake angle is concrete: the skills required to navigate and procure enterprise tools are shifting from interface fluency to outcome specification. How you describe what you need is becoming more valuable than knowing how to use the tool.
