## Summary
During its Q1 2026 earnings call, PayPal CEO Enrique Lores announced the company is "becoming a technology company again" — a direct admission that it has not fully embraced AI internally while competitors raced ahead. Lores announced a new "AI transformation and simplification" team reporting directly to him, tasked with redesigning the company's key processes function by function using AI. Combined with planned layoffs of approximately 20% of its workforce (over 4,500 jobs over two to three years), the move is expected to generate at least $1.5 billion in cost savings. The announcement is notable because PayPal's admission of AI lag came the same week TechCrunch noted that Spotify's top developers "haven't written a line of code since December" due to AI — underscoring how wide the enterprise AI adoption gap has become. PayPal's Q1 revenue was $8.4 billion, up 7% year-over-year, but weak Q2 guidance sent the stock lower after earnings.

## Key Data Points
- $1.5 billion in projected cost savings over two to three years from AI adoption and restructuring
- ~20% of PayPal's workforce to be cut: more than 4,500 jobs over two to three years (Bloomberg)
- AI to be deployed across: software development, customer service, support operations, and risk management
- CEO created a new C-suite team reporting directly to him for AI transformation
- PayPal's stock is down over 80% from its 2021 high — CEO's stated priority is to "maximize shareholder value"
- Spotify's best developers have not written code since December 2025 due to AI — PayPal is now "catching up"
- PayPal Q1 2026 revenue: $8.4 billion, +7% YoY (beat); Q2 guidance weak
- CEO direct quote: "This is not about adopting AI as a technology, where we have done many pilots... It's really about understanding how can we redesign the key processes... this is what will drive significant savings."

## Why It Matters
PayPal's CEO admission that the company is only now "becoming a technology company again" after years of AI pilots is a landmark corporate admission — the pattern that drives the highest-engagement LinkedIn posts in the benchmark data. For founders and SMB operators, this story carries two direct stakes: first, every business that processes payments through PayPal is now watching its vendor race to cut costs via AI while reducing its human customer service and support workforce; second, the "redesign the key processes" framing is precisely the decision that SMB owners face in 2026 — not whether to try AI pilots, but when to commit to redesigning how the business actually runs. The Spotify comparison embedded in the article sharpens the urgency: companies that moved on AI 6 months ago are already in a different competitive position. PayPal's restructuring is a case study in what happens when a large organization delays that decision.
