# KPMG: Inside the AI Agent Playbook Driving Enterprise Margin Gains

**Source URL:** https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/kpmg-inside-ai-agent-playbook-enterprise-margin-gains/

**Publication Date:** April 1, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI agents, agentic AI, generative AI for enterprise

---

## Summary

KPMG's first quarterly Global AI Pulse survey has exposed a stark gap between what enterprises are spending on AI and what they are actually getting back. Despite organizations planning to spend a weighted average of $186 million on AI over the next 12 months, only 11% have deployed and scaled AI agents that produce enterprise-wide business outcomes. The report identifies a clear performance split: AI leaders — companies that redesign processes before deploying agents — outperform their peers by 20 percentage points on meaningful business value delivered. The core finding, as articulated by KPMG's Global Head of AI, is blunt: "spending more on AI is not the same as creating value." The survey spans organizations globally and reveals that the gap between high performers and everyone else comes down to a single key factor — whether the business redesigned its workflows first or simply layered AI tools on top of existing processes.

---

## Key Data Points

- Organizations plan to spend a weighted average of **$186 million on AI** over the next 12 months
- Only **11% have deployed and scaled AI agents** producing enterprise-wide outcomes
- **64%** of all organizations report AI is delivering meaningful business value
- **82%** of AI Leaders report meaningful business value vs. **62%** of others — a 20-point gap
- AI Leaders in IT/Engineering: **75%** using agents for code development (vs. 64% peers)
- AI Leaders in Operations: **64%** deploying agents for supply-chain work (vs. 55% peers)
- Regional agent deployment leaders: ASPAC 49%, Americas 46%, EMEA 42%
- Steve Chase, Global Head of AI at KPMG: "The first Global AI Pulse results reinforce that spending more on AI is not the same as creating value."
- Key differentiator: Leaders redesign processes first, then deploy agents — vs. majority who layer tools onto existing workflows
