# Illinois Just Passed America's Strongest AI Safety Law — Third-Party Audits for OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google

## Title
Illinois Lawmakers Just Passed America's Strongest AI Safety Bill

May 28, 2026

## Summary
The Illinois House passed SB 315 on May 28, requiring frontier AI companies including OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google to have their safety practices independently verified by third-party auditors — making it the nation's strictest AI accountability law. Governor JB Pritzker confirmed he will sign it. Unlike California and New York's AI laws (which require companies to self-report safety incidents and publish guardrail information), Illinois requires an outside auditor to verify whether AI labs are actually following their own safety commitments. Both OpenAI and Anthropic publicly endorsed the bill. The law emerges against a backdrop of federal inaction: Congress has passed no meaningful AI safety legislation, President Trump has been rolling back Biden-era AI rules, and Trump canceled a planned AI executive order last week. Illinois is now effectively setting the de facto national AI safety standard.

## Key Data Points
- SB 315 requires third-party auditors to verify AI labs' adherence to their own stated safety standards
- Expected auditors: Big Four accounting firms (Deloitte, EY, KPMG, PwC) and specialized evaluators from the AI Evaluator Forum (METR, Transluce, Averi)
- Governor Pritzker has confirmed he will sign the bill
- California and New York have existing AI safety laws — Illinois' is the first to require independent verification
- OpenAI and Anthropic both endorsed SB 315 publicly (notable given AI labs' usual resistance to regulation)
- Chamber of Progress (Google, Apple, Amazon, Andreessen Horowitz as partners) opposed the bill, calling it "all liability and no standards"
- Federal context: Trump canceled AI safety executive order last week, citing competitive concerns with China
- Wired quote: "We're in a situation where the AI companies grade their own homework" — Scott Wisor, Secure AI Project

## Why It Matters
This is the first US law requiring AI companies to prove — not just claim — they are following their own safety standards. The practical implication is that every AI lab deploying frontier models will now face structured external scrutiny in Illinois, and other states will likely follow the template. For business leaders, the significance is twofold: (1) The regulatory window for self-governance is narrowing; companies that have not built formal AI governance frameworks are now behind the curve. (2) The fact that Anthropic and OpenAI endorsed this law is a signal — they are betting that codified safety standards create a moat against smaller competitors who cannot afford compliance. Illinois is setting a standard that may become the national floor when federal legislation eventually arrives.

## Why It Matters for Founders and Operators
Founders and operators using AI tools in customer-facing products need to ask a new question: if your AI vendor's safety claims are now subject to independent audit in Illinois, what does that mean for the representations you've made to your own customers? The accountability chain doesn't stop at the frontier AI lab — it extends to every business deploying AI in production. Companies that have already documented their AI governance practices (data usage, model decisions, escalation paths) are in a structurally better position when clients, investors, or regulators ask the same question the Illinois auditors will be asking frontier labs. This law is the first draft of what AI governance compliance will look like everywhere within 2-3 years.
