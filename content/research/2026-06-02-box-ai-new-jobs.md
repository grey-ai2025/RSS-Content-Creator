## Summary
While tech companies including Meta, Cloudflare, Block, and others have been cutting headcount and citing AI as the justification, Box — the Silicon Valley enterprise content management company — is moving in the opposite direction. CEO Aaron Levie told the New York Times that Box expects to employ more people, not fewer, because of AI — and has already created 13 entirely new job categories that did not exist before, including AI architects and AI solutions managers. The story arrives alongside a companion piece on the same day asking whether AI-driven layoffs are genuine productivity gains or convenient cover for cuts that were coming anyway. Taken together, the two articles capture the full fork in the road that founders are currently navigating: deploy AI to replace headcount, or deploy AI to expand what your existing team can do and create new specializations.

## Key Data Points
- Box created 13 new job types due to AI, including: AI Architect, AI Solutions Manager, and other AI-adjacent roles not previously defined
- CEO Aaron Levie (Box) cited in NYT as a company expecting headcount to grow, not shrink, as a result of AI
- Same-day NYT companion piece: "Is AI Replacing Tech Workers or Providing an Excuse for Job Cuts?" examines Meta, Cloudflare, Block layoffs framed as AI-driven
- Mark Zuckerberg (Meta) and Matthew Prince (Cloudflare) both cited as executives attributing cuts to AI efficiency gains
- ClickUp cut 22% of workforce in 2026, replacing workers with AI agents
- AI Weekly (Issue #497, May 29): "Wikipedia editors organizing a strike over Wikimedia layoffs; Amazon employees gamed internal AI ranking into uselessness; Chinese courts began enforcing a framework that bars AI-justified layoffs"
- Microsoft researchers previously published a list of the 40 jobs most exposed to AI, including teachers
- Context: 2026 tech layoffs already nearly matching all of 2025 totals, per TechCrunch

## Why It Matters
This story gives founders the most practically useful mental model for the AI workforce question: it is not binary. The companies cutting headcount while citing AI are mostly using AI as political cover for downturns — not systematically replacing roles. The companies actually benefiting from AI are adding new categories of work that didn't exist before. For a 10-50 person company, the Box model is the more instructive one: what new roles does AI enable you to create, and which existing roles can be elevated? The question is not "how many people can AI replace?" but "what work can AI make possible that you couldn't afford before?" The 13 new job categories Box created offer a practical naming convention for conversations with your board or leadership team about how AI reshapes your org chart.
