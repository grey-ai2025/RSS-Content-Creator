## Summary
Google DeepMind, Microsoft, and Elon Musk's xAI have agreed to allow the US government to review new AI models before they are released publicly. The agreement is with CAISI — the Commerce Department's Center for AI Standards and Innovation — which will conduct "pre-deployment evaluations and targeted research to better assess frontier AI capabilities." CAISI has now performed 40 such reviews to date; OpenAI and Anthropic signed earlier versions of this agreement in 2024 and have renegotiated their partnerships to align with Trump's AI Action Plan. The White House is also reportedly considering an executive order that would formalize a process bringing tech executives and government officials together to oversee new AI models before release. This represents a meaningful shift: AI governance is moving from voluntary self-assessment to structured pre-release government review.

## Key Data Points
- Three new signatories: Google DeepMind, Microsoft, xAI
- Existing signatories (from 2024): OpenAI, Anthropic — both have renegotiated to align with Trump's AI Action Plan
- CAISI (Center for AI Standards and Innovation, within the Commerce Department) is the reviewing body
- 40 pre-deployment evaluations have been completed to date
- White House is considering an executive order to further formalize government oversight of AI model releases (reported by NYT)
- CAISI director Chris Fall statement: "Independent, rigorous measurement science is essential to understanding frontier AI and its national security implications."
- Scope of reviews: "pre-deployment evaluations and targeted research to better assess frontier AI capabilities"
- Context: The AI Weekly newsletter flagged a separate White House move to let agencies procure frontier models even if developers' own safety evaluations flag them as high-risk — creating a tension between tightening vendor review and loosening agency guardrails

## Why It Matters
For founders, SMB operators, and team leads evaluating which AI vendors to trust with sensitive business data or customer interactions, this story signals a structural shift in who validates AI model safety. Pre-deployment government review means that enterprise-grade AI vendors are now operating under a scrutiny layer that did not exist 18 months ago — which changes the calculus on vendor selection. A model reviewed and cleared by a federal standards body is categorically different from a model that is self-certified. For businesses in regulated industries (finance, healthcare, legal services), this signals that the compliance landscape for AI tools is actively consolidating around a federal review framework. For operators not in regulated industries, the practical question becomes: if the US government is now reviewing AI models before release, what does your own internal review process look like before you deploy one?
