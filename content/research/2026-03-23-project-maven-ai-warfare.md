# Project Maven: The Pentagon's AI Warfare Initiative and Its True Believers

**Title:** Meet the Gods of AI Warfare

**Source URL:** https://www.wired.com/story/project-maven-katrina-manson-book-excerpt/

**Publication Date:** March 23, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI military applications, AI governance and safety

## Summary

Wired published a book excerpt on March 23, 2026 from journalist Katrina Manson chronicling the inside story of Project Maven — the Pentagon's AI initiative that began with a mission to use computer vision to analyze drone footage and has since evolved into one of the most consequential and controversial applications of AI in active warfare. The piece traces the initiative from its early days when it faced significant internal Pentagon skepticism, through the Google employee protests that caused Google to exit the contract in 2018, to its current status as a mature operational system with a community of true believers inside the defense establishment. Project Maven is now described as an active system used in military operations including targeting analysis in the US-Iran conflict. The narrative captures the culture shift inside the Pentagon: AI skeptics who once questioned the initiative have become its most fervent advocates after seeing it operate in the field.

## Key Data Points

- Project Maven originated as a Pentagon AI initiative focused on analyzing drone footage with computer vision
- Google's employee backlash over Maven in 2018 led to Google's exit from the contract — a landmark moment in AI ethics
- The system has since evolved and is described as actively deployed in US military operations, including analysis related to the US-Iran conflict
- Former skeptics inside the Pentagon are described as having become "true believers" after seeing Maven's operational performance
- The book excerpt frames Project Maven as a case study in how AI goes from contested pilot to institutional norm inside large organizations
- Palantir is among the current vendors supporting Maven's expanded capabilities

## Why It Matters

Project Maven is the most concrete, operational example of AI moving from enterprise pilot to embedded infrastructure — but in the highest-stakes environment possible. For professional audiences, the Maven story is instructive on two levels: first, it shows how AI adoption inside large institutions follows a predictable arc (skepticism, pilot, operational dependency, true believers); second, it forces the AI governance conversation into its most serious frame. The ethical questions raised when AI assists in targeting decisions — who is accountable, what guardrails exist, what happens when the system is wrong — are the same questions enterprise leaders must answer when deploying agents in consequential business workflows. Maven is where AI governance stops being abstract.
