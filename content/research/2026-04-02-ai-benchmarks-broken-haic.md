# AI Benchmarks Are Broken — Here's the Fix That Actually Reflects the Real World

**Title:** AI benchmarks are broken. Here's what we need instead.

**Source URL:** https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/03/31/1134833/ai-benchmarks-are-broken-heres-what-we-need-instead/

**Publication Date:** March 31, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI benchmarks and measurement; human-agent collaboration; AI agents in enterprise

## Summary

Professor Angela Aristidou (University College London / Stanford HAI) argues that the entire framework for evaluating AI is structurally broken: benchmarks test AI performance in isolation against individual humans, but AI is always deployed inside teams, workflows, and organizations where performance is relational and emerges over time. She documents the pattern across healthcare, humanitarian, and enterprise settings: AI models scoring 98% in benchmark tests routinely fail to improve outcomes once embedded in real-world workflows because benchmarks cannot capture coordination dynamics, error detectability over time, or downstream system effects. Her proposed alternative — HAIC benchmarks (Human-AI, Context-Specific Evaluation) — reframes performance measurement around teams, time horizons, and real organizational outcomes rather than isolated task scores.

## Key Data Points

- FDA-approved AI radiology tools that outperform radiologists in benchmarks consistently introduced delays in real hospital workflows due to misalignment with multidisciplinary reporting standards
- Aristidou studied AI deployment across small businesses, health, humanitarian, nonprofit, and higher education organizations in the UK, US, and Asia since 2022
- In a UK hospital system study (2021-2024), the shift from individual diagnostic accuracy to team coordination and deliberation metrics revealed outcomes that single-task benchmarks missed entirely
- A humanitarian sector study used an 18-month longitudinal evaluation tracking "error detectability" — how easily human teams could identify and correct AI mistakes over time
- HAIC benchmarks reframe evaluation across 4 dimensions: (1) unit of analysis shifts from individual to team/workflow; (2) time horizon expands from one-off to longitudinal; (3) outcome measures expand to coordination quality and error detectability; (4) system effects replace isolated outputs
- Current high benchmark scores create a direct path to the "AI graveyard" — adoption followed by abandonment when real-world gaps emerge, eroding organizational trust
- The real risk of current benchmarks: regulatory oversight is shaped by metrics that do not reflect operational reality, leaving organizations to absorb undisclosed deployment risk
- Author: "AI is almost never used the way it is benchmarked"

## Why It Matters

The benchmark problem is not academic — it is a strategic risk for every enterprise procuring AI right now. Organizations are making multimillion-dollar adoption decisions based on leaderboard scores that were not designed to predict team-level or workflow-level performance. For executives selecting AI tools, CIOs setting AI evaluation criteria, and HR leaders assessing AI productivity claims, this piece reframes the question: stop asking "does it outscore humans on the task?" and start asking "does it improve what the team produces, over time, in our actual workflow?" The HAIC framework is a practical lens for more rigorous vendor evaluation.
