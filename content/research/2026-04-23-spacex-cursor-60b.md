SpaceX Strikes Deal With Cursor — With an Option to Buy It for $60 Billion

# Summary
SpaceX announced a partnership with Cursor, the leading AI coding platform, to develop a "next-generation coding and knowledge work AI" — and included a provision to either pay Cursor $10 billion for its work or acquire the company outright for $60 billion later in 2026. The deal pairs Cursor's product distribution to software engineers with SpaceX's Colossus supercomputer, claimed to hold the compute equivalent of one million Nvidia H100 chips. The move comes as Cursor was already in talks for a $50 billion private fundraising round, having grown from a $2.5 billion valuation in January 2025 to $9 billion in May, to $29.3 billion at its November Series D. The deeper issue: neither Cursor nor xAI currently has proprietary models that can match Anthropic's Claude or OpenAI's GPT — the same companies that supply Cursor's models today and are simultaneously building competing coding tools. SpaceX's engagement appears designed to resolve that dependency ahead of its anticipated IPO.

# Key Data Points
- SpaceX option: acquire Cursor for $60 billion, or pay $10 billion for the partnership work — decision due later in 2026
- Cursor's valuation arc: $2.5B (Jan 2025) → $9B (May 2025) → $29.3B (Nov 2025 Series D) → $50B+ (current fundraising target) → $60B acquisition option
- Colossus supercomputer: equivalent compute of 1 million Nvidia H100 chips
- Cursor uses Claude and GPT models today — awkward given Anthropic and OpenAI are now building their own competing coding tools
- Two senior Cursor engineering leaders (Andrew Milich and Jason Ginsberg) left to join xAI, both reporting directly to Musk
- xAI was already renting compute from its data centers to Cursor to train its latest model before the deal was announced
- Deal comes ahead of SpaceX's anticipated IPO, adding perceived AI/software value to what was a rocket and satellite company

# Why It Matters
For any founder or team lead using Cursor (or evaluating AI coding tools), this deal changes the risk calculus. Cursor currently runs on Anthropic and OpenAI models — the same companies that are building Claude Code and Codex to compete with Cursor for the developer market. A SpaceX acquisition would mean Cursor pivots to xAI's models, which currently lag behind the frontier. This is the first major signal that the AI coding tool market is entering a consolidation phase where the platform you build on today may have a different model under the hood within 12 months. The valuation arc ($2.5B to $60B in 18 months) also illustrates the premium the market is placing on developer workflow distribution: whoever controls how engineers write code controls a major leverage point in the agentic era.
