# DeepSeek's Sequel: How Open-Source AI Became China's Soft Power Play

**Source URL:** https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/03/world/deepseek-china-ai-iran-india-elections.html

**Publication Date:** May 4, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI model efficiency and cost, generative AI ROI and enterprise adoption maturity

**Summary:**
DeepSeek released its V4 model last week — the sequel to the release that triggered "AI's Sputnik moment" in early 2025 — and the NYT's Meaghan Tobin reports that DeepSeek hasn't just put China neck-and-neck with the US in the AI race, it has fundamentally changed the nature of the race itself. By open-sourcing its models, DeepSeek has made its technology accessible to developers in Iran, India, and dozens of other countries that might otherwise have defaulted to US platforms, converting a competitive disadvantage (resource constraints) into geopolitical reach. Meanwhile, MIT Technology Review noted V4 can process dramatically longer prompts than the previous generation through a new efficiency architecture. The open-source strategy has disrupted the pricing floor that Western AI valuations were built on, with DeepSeek cutting its API pricing multiple times this quarter.

**Key Data Points:**
- DeepSeek V4 released April 24, 2026 — open-source, available to any developer globally
- V4 can process much longer prompts than V3 due to a new long-context efficiency design
- DeepSeek has cut its API pricing multiple times this quarter in an ongoing Chinese AI price war
- The model has been adopted in Iran, India, and dozens of markets that would otherwise use US-platform models
- AI Weekly noted DeepSeek's pricing moves have "moved the pricing floor that justified Western AI valuations several thousand miles east"
- China's open-source AI strategy is now functioning as soft power, not just a technical release

**Why It Matters:**
For founders and SMB operators, this story has a direct budget implication: high-quality open-weight models are now available at near-zero inference cost, and the gap between free Chinese models and expensive US API pricing is widening. Any business currently paying significant API costs to OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google needs to be aware that comparable (and often cheaper) open alternatives exist and are improving rapidly. Beyond cost, it signals a model landscape shift: the assumption that the best AI will always come from US labs is no longer safe to hold in your product roadmap.
