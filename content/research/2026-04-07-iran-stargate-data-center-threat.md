## Summary
Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps published a video explicitly threatening OpenAI's planned Stargate data center in Abu Dhabi, warning it would be targeted if the US follows through on threats to strike Iranian power plants. The threat landed amid an escalating US-Iran military conflict and followed an earlier general statement from Iran that it would target US-linked data centers with new missile strikes. The Stargate project — a joint venture backed by OpenAI, Oracle, and SoftBank representing a planned $500 billion AI infrastructure investment — now sits at the intersection of great-power conflict and geopolitical risk in a way AI infrastructure never has before. The incident is forcing a reckoning about geographic concentration risk for critical AI compute.

## Key Data Points
- The IRGC published video to an Iranian state-backed news outlet's X account on April 3rd naming the Abu Dhabi Stargate data center as a specific target
- Iran previously announced it will target US-linked data centers broadly with new missile strikes as US-Iran conflict escalates
- The Stargate project represents a planned $500 billion AI infrastructure investment from OpenAI, Oracle, SoftBank, and others
- OpenAI COO Brad Lightcap was simultaneously moved to "special projects" in an executive reshuffle — AI Weekly noted $6 billion in OpenAI shares sat unsold on secondary markets the same week
- Ars Technica reported nearly 50% of US data center projects are already delayed due to Trump tariffs affecting power infrastructure components, many of which are manufactured in China
- The AI Weekly briefing described the week as "three days, three threat vectors nobody had on their bingo card" — North Korea compromised npm packages, Iran targeted data center coordinates, and Anthropic's own security tool received its own CVE

## Why It Matters
AI infrastructure has crossed into national security territory. For any executive whose organization depends on cloud-based AI services — which in 2026 means nearly every enterprise — geographic concentration of compute is no longer an abstract risk. A single targeted strike on a major data center campus would create service disruptions cascading across the models, agents, and applications businesses have built atop them. Risk and continuity teams need to understand which AI services they depend on, where those services run physically, and what their provider's resilience posture looks like. The Stargate threat is the most visible signal yet that AI infrastructure is a military and geopolitical target.
