# ProPublica Journalists Strike Over AI Use, Layoff Protections, and Wages

**Title:** Unionized ProPublica staff are on strike over AI, layoffs, and wages

**Source URL:** https://www.theverge.com/news/908401/propublica-union-strike-negotiations-ai-layoffs

**Publication Date:** April 8, 2026

**Keyword Match:** Human-AI collaboration (#10); AI governance and oversight (#3)

**Summary:**
Roughly 150 unionized staff at ProPublica — one of the leading nonprofit investigative newsrooms in the US — walked off the job on April 8 for a 24-hour strike, with AI governance as one of the central issues. The ProPublica Guild, which has been negotiating a collective bargaining agreement since unionizing in 2023, is demanding that AI use be formally defined and constrained in their contract, not just in a management policy. The core dispute is over who controls how AI gets introduced into the workflow: management released an AI policy unilaterally, which the NewsGuild called an "unfair labor practice." Workers want contractual protections against AI-driven layoffs, a say in how tools evolve, and mandatory public disclosure when AI is used to produce stories. The strike marks a visible moment in a broader shift across media and knowledge work industries where AI governance is no longer an abstract policy question — it's a labor rights issue.

**Key Data Points:**
- Approximately 150 ProPublica Guild members participated in the 24-hour strike on April 8, 2026
- The NewsGuild filed an unfair labor practice charge over management's unilateral AI policy implementation
- Key contract demands: AI use protections, "just cause" provisions for discipline/firing, layoff protections, and wage increases
- ProPublica management introduced an AI policy — but workers say it's "a little bit squishy" and lacks binding commitments
- Workers want public disclosure requirements when AI is used to produce editorial content
- Staff opinions on AI are split: some see it as automation for tedious tasks; others fear it replacing core human work
- The NYT has used AI to parse documents; Fortune has churned out hundreds of AI-written stories — illustrating the spectrum of industry approaches
- This is the first time ProPublica staff have walked off the job

**Why It Matters:**
The ProPublica strike is the clearest signal yet that the "human-AI collaboration" conversation has moved out of leadership strategy decks and onto the picket line. For managers, HR leaders, and executives across any knowledge-work industry — not just media — this story is a template for what happens when AI tools are deployed faster than worker trust is built. The key lesson: unilateral AI policy rollouts create labor conflict. Companies that involve workers in defining AI governance frameworks are more likely to achieve sustainable adoption. Any organization currently drafting AI use policies without employee input is watching this situation unfold as a cautionary case study.
