# Apple Plans to Open Siri to Third-Party AI Assistants in iOS 27

**Title:** Apple Will Reportedly Allow Other AI Chatbots to Plug Into Siri

**Source URL:** https://www.theverge.com/tech/902048/apple-siri-ai-chatbot-update-ios-27

**Publication Date:** March 26, 2026

**Keyword Match:** Apple Siri opening to rival AI assistants (#6)

**Summary:**
Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reported that Apple's iOS 27 update will allow users to choose which AI chatbot they want to link with Siri — including third-party options like Google's Gemini and Anthropic's Claude downloaded from the App Store. Rather than building a more powerful AI itself, Apple appears to be repositioning the iPhone as an AI platform, letting third-party models handle complex queries while Siri manages device-level actions and on-device tasks. This mirrors how Apple previously opened iOS to third-party browsers and keyboards under regulatory pressure, and it intensifies the already heated competition among AI assistants for consumer mindshare. The move effectively turns the iPhone's billion-plus user base into a battleground where OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic will compete for default status.

**Key Data Points:**
- iOS 27 will let users select a third-party AI chatbot — such as Gemini or Claude — to power Siri's responses
- Third-party chatbots will "fetch replies for Siri," similar to how ChatGPT integration currently works
- Apps must be downloaded from the App Store to be eligible — Apple retains App Store control as the gatekeeping layer
- This development comes one week after Google launched "switching tools" to import chat histories from rival chatbots into Gemini, suggesting the AI assistant platform wars are accelerating
- ChatGPT reached 900 million weekly active users as of February 2026; Gemini surpassed 750 million monthly active users — the Siri integration could shift these numbers significantly
- Bloomberg's Mark Gurman, Apple's most reliable source, broke the story — giving it high credibility

**Why It Matters:**
The iPhone is the most-used consumer device on the planet. Opening Siri to third-party AI is not a product tweak — it is Apple acknowledging it cannot win the AI model race and choosing instead to own the platform layer. For AI companies, this creates a high-stakes distribution battle: whoever wins the iPhone's AI default position gains access to the largest single pool of daily active users in tech. For enterprise leaders, it signals that the AI assistant ecosystem is converging toward platform-level integration rather than standalone apps, and that choosing an AI vendor is increasingly a platform choice with long-term lock-in implications.

# Anthropic Wins Court Injunction Against Pentagon's Supply Chain Risk Designation

**Title:** Judge Sides With Anthropic to Temporarily Block the Pentagon's Ban

**Source URL:** https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/902149/anthropic-dod-pentagon-lawsuit-supply-chain-risk-injunction

**Publication Date:** March 27, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI governance and responsible AI (#9); Anthropic new model / cybersecurity risk (#7)

**Summary:**
A federal judge granted Anthropic a preliminary injunction blocking the Trump administration's Pentagon designation of the company as a "supply chain risk" — a label that would have barred Anthropic from doing business with federal agencies. The court found the Pentagon's blacklisting appeared to be an effort to "cripple" Anthropic rather than address a genuine national security threat. The ruling is an early legal victory for Anthropic in its ongoing standoff with the Department of Defense, which escalated after Anthropic refused to allow its Claude model to be used for certain military applications without safety guardrails. The decision allows Anthropic to continue operating normally while the legal process continues and sets a significant precedent for how AI companies can push back against government pressure using the courts.

**Key Data Points:**
- Federal judge issued a preliminary injunction halting the Pentagon's "supply chain risk" designation against Anthropic
- Court characterized the Pentagon's blacklisting as an "attempt to cripple" Anthropic — language that signals judicial skepticism of the administration's stated rationale
- Wired reported the judge's decision clears the way for Anthropic to keep doing business without the label starting next week
- The designation would have barred Anthropic from government contracts and created reputational damage across federal clients
- Multiple outlets (TechCrunch, NYT, The Verge, Wired) reported on the ruling within hours of it dropping, making it one of the most-covered AI stories of the week
- David Sacks, the White House's Special Advisor on AI and Crypto who was involved in the administration's AI policy posture, was simultaneously reported to have left his role on March 26
- Separately, Anthropic's own research this week showed a growing AI skills gap — meaning the company is simultaneously dealing with a high-stakes legal battle and publishing substantive economic research

**Why It Matters:**
This ruling matters far beyond Anthropic. It establishes that AI companies have legal standing to contest government actions that appear motivated by political or competitive pressure rather than legitimate security concerns. For enterprise AI buyers — particularly those in regulated industries or with government exposure — the case clarifies that AI governance is increasingly playing out in federal courts, not just boardrooms. The Pentagon's attempt to designate a safety-focused AI company as a "supply chain risk" also deepens the tensions between responsible AI development and government demands for unrestricted military use, a fault line that will define the industry for years.
