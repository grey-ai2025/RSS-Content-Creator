## Summary
Cognition, the company behind Devin (the autonomous AI software engineer), raised over $1 billion at a $25 billion pre-money valuation, led by Lux Capital, General Catalyst, and 8VC. This more than doubled its $10.2 billion valuation from just eight months ago in September 2025. Cognition now reports $492 million in annualized revenue run rate with 50% month-over-month enterprise growth for six consecutive months. Enterprise customers include Mercedes-Benz, NASA, Goldman Sachs, and Santander. The funding is a direct bet against the prevailing prediction that AI model makers (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google) would absorb the autonomous coding agent market through their own products — Claude Code, Codex, and Jules. Cognition previously acquired the remaining Windsurf assets when Google acqui-hired Windsurf's team last year, positioning it as the independent alternative in a market increasingly dominated by vertically integrated AI labs.

## Why It Matters
A $25 billion valuation for an AI coding agent company — not a model lab, not a foundation model provider, but an application layer company — is the clearest market signal yet that autonomous AI coding agents are becoming a durable, standalone software category. The conventional prediction was that Anthropic (Claude Code), OpenAI (Codex), and Google (Jules) would absorb this market. Cognition's $492M ARR and 50% MoM enterprise growth says otherwise. The major enterprises paying for Devin are not doing it because it's the only option — they are doing it because a specialized, independent agent that isn't tied to one model vendor provides accountability and flexibility that bundled offerings from AI labs do not. This is the same logic driving OpenRouter's growth — enterprises are not betting the stack on one AI provider.

## Why It Matters for Founders and Operators
For any founder evaluating AI coding tools, this funding round validates that the autonomous agent category is real — not a demo or a benchmark, but a live enterprise deployment with $492M in revenue and growing. The practical question is what this means for team structure. If Goldman Sachs and NASA are using Devin to autonomously complete engineering tasks, the bar for what "junior developer work" means is shifting again. Founders should evaluate whether their current AI coding setup (human-directed tools like Cursor or Copilot vs. autonomous agents like Devin or Claude Code) matches the scale and type of work they are doing. For teams where engineering bottlenecks are holding back product velocity, autonomous coding agents are no longer experimental — they are enterprise-grade infrastructure at $25B in market value.

## Summary

Governor JB Pritzker confirmed he will sign it. Unlike California and New York's AI laws (which require companies to self-report safety incidents and publish guardrail information), Illinois requires an outside auditor to verify whether AI labs are actually following their own safety commitments. Both OpenAI and Anthropic publicly endorsed the bill. The law emerges against a backdrop of federal inaction: Congress has passed no meaningful AI safety legislation, President Trump has been rolling back Biden-era AI rules, and Trump canceled a planned AI executive order last week. Illinois is now effectively setting the de facto national AI safety standard.
