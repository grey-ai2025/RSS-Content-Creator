# Salesforce Overhauls Slack with 30 AI Features — Including MCP-Powered Agent Orchestration

**Title:** Salesforce announces an AI-heavy makeover for Slack, with 30 new features

**Source URL:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/31/salesforce-announces-an-ai-heavy-makeover-for-slack-with-30-new-features/

**Publication Date:** March 31, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI agents / agentic AI; Model Context Protocol (MCP); agentic AI production deployments

## Summary

Salesforce unveiled 30 new AI features for Slack on Tuesday, including a major upgrade to its Slackbot agent. The centerpiece is "reusable AI skills" — user-defined or built-in task templates that Slackbot executes autonomously across multiple contexts. A single command like "create a budget" triggers Slackbot to pull data from connected channels, third-party tools, and data sources, draft a plan, and automatically schedule a meeting with the right attendees — all without human intervention. Slackbot now also functions as a Model Context Protocol (MCP) client, enabling it to connect and route work to Salesforce Agentforce or any other enterprise agent in the stack. The agent can operate outside Slack entirely, monitoring desktop activity to make proactive suggestions based on deals, calendar, conversations, and user habits.

## Key Data Points

- 30 new AI features announced at a San Francisco event; rolling out over coming months
- New "reusable AI skills" feature: user-defined or built-in tasks that Slackbot executes autonomously across contexts
- Slackbot is now an MCP client — connects and orchestrates with Salesforce Agentforce and external tools
- Agent can now operate outside Slack: monitors desktop activity (deals, conversations, calendar, habits) and makes proactive suggestions
- Slackbot added meeting transcription and summarization; can produce recap + action items for participants
- CEO Marc Benioff: Salesforce has "about a million businesses running on Slack" with "2.5x revenue growth" since acquisition
- Follows a January 2026 update that gave Slackbot initial agentic capabilities (email drafting, meeting scheduling, inbox filtering)
- Privacy protections built in; users can adjust permissions on desktop monitoring

## Why It Matters

This announcement represents one of the clearest examples of agentic AI moving from experiment into the enterprise production environment. Slackbot becoming an MCP client is the detail that matters most for technical and product professionals: it means Salesforce is building an agent orchestration layer inside a tool that one million businesses already use daily. This is not a demo or a pilot — it is a production deployment at scale. For enterprise leaders, it raises an immediate question: if your AI strategy doesn't account for agents that can route, delegate, and execute across your entire tool stack, you are already behind the curve Salesforce is setting.

**Publication Date:** March 30, 2026

**Keyword Match:** AI agents / agentic AI; AI accountability and governance; cybersecurity and AI

## Summary

Okta co-founder and CEO Todd McKinnon argues that the rapid proliferation of AI agents inside enterprise environments has created an urgent identity crisis — literally. Every AI agent operating inside a company needs to be authenticated, permissioned, and auditable, just like a human employee, but most companies are deploying agents without any formal identity infrastructure underneath them. McKinnon frames the threat bluntly: running agents without a proper identity layer is "naive," and companies that do so are creating attack surfaces and accountability gaps they cannot see. Okta is positioning itself as the identity provider for the agentic era, extending its core platform from human identity management to AI agent identity management. McKinnon also introduced the concept of the "SaaSpocalypse" — the scenario where AI agents replace or bypass traditional SaaS applications entirely, disrupting the existing enterprise software stack.

## Key Data Points

- Okta CEO Todd McKinnon: every AI agent needs authentication, permissioning, and an audit trail — the same infrastructure required for human employees
- McKinnon called it "naive" to deploy AI agents without a formal identity layer in place
- Okta is extending its core identity platform to cover non-human identities, specifically AI agents
- The "SaaSpocalypse" concept: AI agents that execute tasks end-to-end may render traditional SaaS applications obsolete — McKinnon says it would be naive NOT to prepare for this scenario
- Without proper agent identity, organizations cannot answer basic audit questions: which agent took which action, with what permissions, and when
- AI agents operating with human-level credentials (as many enterprises currently do) represent a compounding security and compliance risk
- Identity for AI agents is becoming an enterprise governance issue, not just a security issue

## Why It Matters

Most of the public conversation about AI agents focuses on what they can do. Okta's CEO is drawing attention to a gap that most enterprise AI deployments have not addressed: what governs them. As companies accelerate agent deployment in 2026, the question of agent identity — who (or what) authorized this action, what did it have access to, and is there an audit trail — is moving from theoretical to urgent. For compliance officers, CISOs, and anyone building enterprise AI strategy, this is the accountability infrastructure question the Accenture/Wharton "intelligence may be scalable but accountability is not" framing points directly at. Okta is positioning itself at the center of this problem, but the underlying issue applies to every enterprise deploying agents regardless of vendor.

# The Global Gig Workers Filming Their Chores to Train Humanoid Robots

**Title:** The gig workers who are training humanoid robots at home

**Source URL:** https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/04/01/1134863/humanoid-data-training-gig-economy-2026-breakthrough-technology/

**Publication Date:** April 1, 2026

**Keyword Match:** generative AI moving from hype to pragmatism; AI in HR and workforce; agentic AI infrastructure (physical AI / humanoid robot training pipeline)

## Summary

A global gig economy has emerged to solve one of the most critical bottlenecks in humanoid robotics: real-world movement data. Companies like Micro1 have hired thousands of contract workers across more than 50 countries — Nigeria, India, Argentina — to strap iPhones to their foreheads and film themselves doing household chores: folding laundry, washing dishes, ironing. The footage is sold to robotics companies including Tesla, Figure AI, and Agility Robotics to train their humanoid robots. The work pays $15 an hour and is providing meaningful income in lower-wage economies, but raises serious questions about data privacy, informed consent, and whether gig workers understand how their footage will be used. As MIT Technology Review designated humanoid robots a 2026 Breakthrough Technology, this story reveals the unglamorous but essential labor infrastructure underneath the AI revolution.

## Key Data Points

- Micro1 (Palo Alto) has hired thousands of contract workers in 50+ countries to record household chore footage
- Workers are paid approximately $15/hour — competitive income in markets like Nigeria and India with high unemployment
- Footage sold to humanoid robotics companies including Tesla, Figure AI, and Agility Robotics
- Robotics industry spent over $6 billion on humanoid robot development in 2025
- Companies spending more than $100 million per year on real-world training data (Micro1 CEO estimate)
- Scale AI has gathered more than 100,000 hours of movement footage; Micro1 has "tens of thousands of hours"
- DoorDash now pays delivery drivers to film themselves doing chores — expanding the data economy
- In China, state-run robot training centers employ workers wearing VR headsets and exoskeletons
- Workers vetted and managed by an AI agent named "Zara" that conducts interviews and reviews video samples
- UC Berkeley roboticist Ken Goldberg: humanoid robots may need even more training data than LLMs — "it's going to take longer than people think"
- Workers are not told which robotics companies receive their footage or how it will be shared

## Why It Matters

This story exposes the human labor infrastructure underneath the "AI is automated" narrative. Every humanoid robot demo at a trade show is powered by thousands of contract workers in developing economies filming themselves folding laundry for $15 an hour. For professionals thinking about AI strategy and AI's economic impact, this is a grounding story: the transition to physical AI is not frictionless automation — it requires a new global labor market that raises real questions about data ownership, consent, and economic equity. It also reframes the humanoid robot timeline: the data collection bottleneck is real, the work is happening at scale, but UC Berkeley's framing ("it's going to take longer than people think") provides a useful counterweight to the hype cycle.

# AI Recruiting Startup Mercor Hit by Supply Chain Attack via Compromised Open-Source LiteLLM

**Title:** Mercor says it was hit by cyberattack tied to compromise of open-source LiteLLM project

**Source URL:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/31/mercor-says-it-was-hit-by-cyberattack-tied-to-compromise-of-open-source-litellm-project/

**Publication Date:** April 1, 2026 (published March 31, confirmed April 1)

**Keyword Match:** cybersecurity and AI; AI accountability and governance

## Summary

Mercor, the AI recruiting and model-training startup valued at $10 billion after a $350M Series C, confirmed it was compromised via a supply chain attack rooted in the open-source LiteLLM project. Malicious code was introduced into a LiteLLM package by a group called TeamPCP; the Lapsus$ extortion group then claimed to have stolen data from Mercor as a result. LiteLLM is downloaded millions of times per day and serves as an AI gateway used by enterprises worldwide, making the incident a systemic risk event rather than an isolated breach. Mercor confirmed the breach and launched a third-party forensic investigation but declined to confirm whether customer or contractor data was accessed. The incident highlights a structural vulnerability in the AI toolchain: the open-source libraries that power enterprise AI deployments are high-value targets with limited security governance.

## Key Data Points

- Mercor valuation: $10 billion (following $350M Series C in October 2025, led by Felicis Ventures)
- Mercor works with OpenAI and Anthropic to train AI models via domain expert contractors
- Facilitates more than $2 million in daily contractor payouts
- Attack vector: malicious code injected into LiteLLM's open-source package by group "TeamPCP"
- Lapsus$ claimed responsibility for subsequent data theft from Mercor; posted sample data including Slack and ticketing data
- LiteLLM downloaded millions of times per day per security firm Snyk — widespread enterprise exposure
- Malicious code was identified and removed from LiteLLM within hours of discovery
- LiteLLM subsequently dropped compliance startup Delve and moved to Vanta for compliance certifications
- Mercor declined to confirm whether any customer or contractor data was accessed, exfiltrated, or misused
- Third-party forensics investigation launched; investigation ongoing

## Why It Matters

This incident is a case study in a risk vector that most enterprise AI strategies have not fully accounted for: the open-source supply chain. Enterprises deploying AI tools at scale almost universally depend on open-source libraries — LiteLLM is just one of dozens of widely-used AI gateways and toolkits. When those libraries are compromised, the blast radius is enormous. The Mercor breach demonstrates that AI startups — even well-funded, high-profile ones servicing OpenAI and Anthropic — are operating on security infrastructure that was not built for the threat level they now attract. For enterprise security leaders and AI architects, this is the practical argument for why "AI-native security tooling" is not optional overhead — it is the cost of operating in 2026.
